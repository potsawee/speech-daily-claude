## ★ 1. Unsupervised Speech Recognition at the Syllable Level — `2608.22907` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Liming Wang, Kai-Wei Chang, Kunio Kashino, David Harwath, Mark Hasegawa-Johnson, James R. Glass* — **MIT**, **NTT**, **UT Austin**, **UIUC** · eess.AS · 2026-08-24 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22907)

*Problem:* Unsupervised ASR from unpaired speech and text needs costly G2P converters and unstable GAN training that fails where phoneme boundaries are fuzzy.
*Method:* Shift the unit from phone to syllable and swap the GAN for masked language modelling over a shared speech/text encoder, plus boundary refinement and positional n-gram distribution matching.
*Result:* 21.8% CER on LibriSpeech matched vs wav2vec-U's 35.6% (~40% relative), transferring to Cantonese (23.5%) and Taigi (26.5%); code released as SylCipher.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed author (David Harwath), with James Glass's MIT group and Mark Hasegawa-Johnson alongside. Long-tail and low-resource ASR is primary focus, and this goes after the two things that have kept UASR unusable — the G2P dependency and GAN instability — by changing the *unit* rather than patching the objective, which is the more durable kind of fix. Quality 8 for a real 40% relative cut over the standard baseline, transfer to three genuinely different writing systems, released code, and a limitations section that volunteers what most preprints hide: the method is not language-universal, needs language-specific syllabification knowledge, breaks on abjads like Hebrew and Arabic, and still degrades under speech/text domain mismatch. Short of 9 because 21.8% CER is a long way from supervised usability, and the headline comparison is against wav2vec-U rather than the stronger wav2vec-U 2.0.

## 2. FireRedAudio: A General-Purpose Audio Language Model with Decoupled Continuous Representations for Understanding and Generation — `2608.24168` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Junjie Li, Xuelong Geng, Kun Xie, Feiyu Shen, Yichen Wu, Ziqi Dai, Yichen Han, Yan Jia, Kai Huang, Junjie Chen, Yixuan Li, Manzhen Wei, Fenglong Xie, Lei Xie, Xu Tang, Yao Hu* — **Xiaohongshu (RedNote)**, FireRed team · cs.CL, cs.SD · 2026-08-25 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24168)

*Problem:* A unified audio LLM is pulled two ways — understanding wants compact long-context features, generation wants reconstructible ones preserving fine acoustic detail — and one discrete tokenizer compromises both.
*Method:* One 9B LLM, two decoupled continuous *input* paths: an audio encoder for recognition and understanding, a RedAE pathway for generation, with the LLM emitting text or conditioning a flow-matching DiT on continuous latents. ~2.75T tokens, progressive multitask training.
*Result:* Best-in-table 2.53% WER on LibriSpeech test-clean and 14.94% average WER on FLEURS-102; 0.71% CER on AISHELL-1, 82.0 on MMAU test-mini, 0.83% CER on Seed-TTS-Eval zh; hour-long audio with second-level timestamps; code released.

**Why it ranked:** The window's headline release, and it lands directly on your representation question. Rather than forcing a single tokenizer to serve both recognition and synthesis, it keeps two continuous input pathways inside one trainable autoregressive LLM — the clearest public statement yet that the understanding/generation representation conflict is a plumbing problem, not a scaling one. Relevance 9 because multilingual ASR and modality-representation design arrive in the same system, with FLEURS-102 breadth rather than the usual English-and-Mandarin pair. Quality 8 for that breadth, a full semantic-and-acoustic editing comparison against Ming-UniAudio-Edit, and released code. Held at 8 on three counts: "first publicly disclosed unified audio-language model to provide separate continuous input representations" is a carefully fenced novelty claim; some Table 4 error rates are low enough that I would want independent replication before quoting them; and nothing ablates what the decoupling itself buys versus the 2.75T-token training budget.

## 3. Do Spoken Language Models Hear Speech as They Read Text? Bridging Structural Gaps Between Speech and Text — `2608.22908` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Hyeonyu Kim, Hwayeon Kim, Youngwon Choi, Myeongkyun Cho, Huu-Kim Nguyen* — **Maum AI**, **KAIST**, **Atmanity** · cs.CL, cs.AI · EMNLP 2026 Findings · 2026-08-24 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22908)

*Problem:* SLMs post strong downstream numbers yet follow instructions worse than their text backbones; the paper argues speech and text representations were never structurally aligned to begin with.
*Method:* Measure alignment directly with centered kernel alignment, then decouple what adapters normally conflate — dynamic query allocation matches speech length to text-embedding length during training, and a lightweight speech-rate predictor supplies that length at inference.
*Result:* CKA rises from 0.357–0.430 across BLSP-emo, DiVA, Qwen2-Audio-Instruct and DeSTA2 to 0.640; 350M trainable parameters on 69k hours stay competitive on AIR-Bench, SpeechR and MMSU.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — this is your central question posed sharply ("why do SLMs lose the backbone's instruction-following?") and answered with a *measurement* rather than another benchmark. Separating length mismatch from semantic correspondence is the right decomposition: prior adapters ask one bottleneck to solve both, and the CKA diagonal-structure visualisation makes the failure legible in a way error rates never do. Quality 7 because the diagnosis outruns the remedy. The compute is honest and small (350M trainable, 8×H100, 8k steps, 69k hours), and a 0.357→0.640 CKA jump is not marginal — but on the forgetting metric that motivates the entire paper, their −12.18% Speech-IFEval rate is *beaten* by DeSTA2's −3.57%, a fact the abstract omits, and the downstream results are "competitive against strong baselines" rather than ahead of them.

## 4. DiaScriber: A Speech LLM for Joint Diarization and Transcription in Multi-Speaker Scenarios — `2608.22796` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Bingshen Mu, Xian Shi, Xiong Wang, Zhifang Guo, Ting He, Xize Cheng, Yu Xi, Jin Xu, Lei Xie* — **ASLP@NPU** (Northwestern Polytechnical University), with independent researchers · eess.AS, cs.SD · 2026-08-24 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22796)

*Problem:* Multi-speaker ASR has to answer "who spoke what and when", and fast turn transitions plus overlapping speech still break systems that bolt diarization onto recognition.
*Method:* End-to-end diarization-and-transcription on a Qwen3.5-Omni base (600M audio encoder, 23B MoE with 2.6B active), trained in three stages — 85k hours continual pretraining, ~4.6k hours SFT, then RL on 1k unseen multi-speaker recordings — over pipelines that simulate turn transitions and overlap.
*Result:* 5.4% average DER and 11.8% cpWER across thirteen test sets; 3.6% DER and 12.0% cpWER on AliMeeting, ahead of VibeVoice-ASR and MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize.

**Why it ranked:** Multi-speaker ASR through a speech LLM is squarely primary focus, and the interesting move is the third stage — RL applied to *joint* diarization-and-transcription is unusual, and it is where the generalization-to-unseen-scenarios claim comes from. The data pipeline is the other honest contribution: explicitly simulating fast turn transitions and overlap, rather than hoping meeting corpora cover them. Quality 7 because the recipe is specific and the open-set results (AliMeeting, AISHELL-4, MagicData-RAMC, AMI, MLCSLM-EN) are reproducible numbers on public data — but eight of the thirteen test sets are internal, no code or weights are announced, and there is no ablation separating the RL stage's contribution from the 85k-hour pretraining, so "outstanding generalization ability" is asserted more than demonstrated.

## 5. WnW: Waxing-and-Waning KV Cache for Long-Form Speech LLMs — `2608.22704` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Yiming Yao, Chenyang Lyu, Xuanfan Ni, Longyue Wang, Weihua Luo, Yazheng Yang, Jinsong Su* — **Alibaba Group**, **Xiamen University** · cs.CL, cs.SD · EMNLP 2026 Main · 2026-08-24 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22704)

*Problem:* On long-form audio the KV cache dominates memory, and prefill-only compression evicts audio positions permanently — with no route back once decoding needs them.
*Method:* Offline calibration sorts KV heads into three roles: anchor heads stay on GPU and act as a decode-time importance observer; tidal heads keep a CPU-resident complement recalled chunk-by-chunk on aggregated anchor scores; fixed heads keep only a GPU subset and discard the rest.
*Result:* At 20% on-GPU audio KV, 6.23% WER on LibriSpeech-Long test-clean against a 6.79% full cache — where prefill-only baselines exceed 190% WER and fail to terminate; ~0.6 MB/step transfer, 27.4 ms/token median decode.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 7 — this is infrastructure rather than modelling — but it earns its slot on evidence and on a diagnosis that generalizes. Prefill attention piles onto the start of the audio as a sink while decode-time attention spreads broadly, and the two importance rankings barely overlap; that explains *why* prefill-only eviction fails on audio specifically, not merely that it does, and it is the kind of mechanism worth carrying into any long-form speech-LLM design. Quality 8 for two independent 3B backbones (Voxtral-mini-3b, Qwen2.5-Omni-3B), out-of-domain checks on French ASR, English→French translation and PriMock57 medical consultations, and measured transfer volume and per-token latency instead of a big-O claim. Short of 9 because it needs per-model offline calibration, the French ASR gap (22.68% vs 20.42% full cache) shows the method is not free under language shift, and every result is at 3B.

### Also considered, not surfaced

**Closest to the cut:**

- `2608.22101` — *Beyond Fresh Starts: Stateful Inference for Streaming ASR in Conversational Voice Agents* (Sameep Chattopadhyay, Alexander Erdmann, **Mari Ostendorf**, UW). Voice-agent pipelines reset recognizer state every turn, discarding cross-utterance context; two state-management strategies give 15–21% average relative WER reduction *at utterance onsets* on two SOTA streaming models. Squarely primary-focus and immediately actionable — held out only because it is a short paper with no venue, no code and no ablation, against a top five whose evidence is much deeper.
- `2608.22872` — *Better Retrieval, Worse Robustness: How Multi-hop RAG Amplifies Upstream ASR Errors* (EMNLP 2026 Main). Entity-graph linking and iterative reformulation *amplify* ASR errors rather than absorbing them: the clean-to-worst-accent F1 gap is 36–67% larger under their combination than under naive dense retrieval, and 87–96% of degradation on 2WikiMultiHopQA traces to corrupted query entities. Strongly on-topic for entity-aware recognition; capped by TTS-synthesized accents standing in for real speech and a single author.
- `2601.18904` — *MetaSICL: Globalizing Auditory LLMs for Underserved Speakers and Languages via Meta Speech In-Context Learning* (Haolong Zheng, Siyin Wang, Zengrui Jin, Mark Hasegawa-Johnson). A post-training recipe that teaches an auditory LLM to *use* in-context demonstrations, trained only on high-resource speech yet improving children's ASR, audio reasoning, and unseen-direction speech translation. Excluded from ranking because it is a January v1 only now cross-listed into eess.AS, not new work — but it is the most on-topic thing the listing pages turned up and worth a read.
