## 1. ★ RetroThinker: Enabling Retrospective Thinking in Speech LLMs — `2609.11864` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Yi-Jen Shih, Puyuan Peng, Abdelrahman Mohamed, David Harwath* — **The University of Texas at Austin** and **FAIR, Meta Superintelligence Labs** · eess.AS, cs.AI, cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-10 · IEEE SLT 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11864)

*Problem:* Speech LLMs trail text-only LLMs on reasoning, and real-time spoken interaction forbids the extra thinking time that would close the gap — chain-of-thought and concurrent reasoning both leave an accuracy-latency trade-off standing.
*Method:* Multi-stage post-training of Moshi: supervised fine-tuning on curated retrospective-thinking data, then length-based DPO, so the model self-verifies and forward-corrects its own CoT steps mid-inference — reasoning while the user is still speaking.
*Result:* 11% absolute accuracy gain on GSM8K over non-retrospective baselines at comparable latency.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 because "can a speech-native model reason without a cascade" is the question that decides whether Speech LLMs stay a latency optimisation or become the default architecture, and revision-during-streaming is a mechanism that transfers directly to the recognition side — the same "commit now or wait for more evidence" decision drives streaming endpointing and incremental SLU. Quality 8 for a follow-list author at a follow-list lab, SLT acceptance, and the decision to build on **Moshi**, a public model, so the result is reproducible rather than a proprietary-stack claim; the length-based DPO stage is also the right instinct, since the failure mode of self-correction is runaway verbosity and they optimise against it explicitly rather than capping it post-hoc. Held to 8 by the evaluation: **GSM8K alone** is a thin basis for a claim about spoken reasoning, it is a text-origin benchmark read aloud, and one backbone means the framework's generality is asserted rather than shown. Take the retrospective-decoding mechanism as the contribution and treat the 11 points as a single-benchmark data point.

## 2. ★ Pushing the Boundaries of Streaming Multi-Speaker ASR: A Systematic Study of Architectural Trade-offs — `2609.10265` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Taejin Park, Ivan Medennikov, Kunal Dhawan, Weiqing Wang, Jagadeesh Balam, Boris Ginsburg* — **NVIDIA**, Santa Clara · eess.AS, cs.SD · submitted 2026-09-09 · Interspeech 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10265)

*Problem:* Streaming multi-speaker ASR has four competing architectures for combining diarization and recognition, and no like-for-like comparison — published numbers differ in base model, data and diarizer, so the architectural question has never actually been isolated.
*Method:* Build all four — cascaded, masked input, word-level SOT, and diarization-conditioned Self-Speaker Adaptation — on one shared open-source streaming ASR and diarizer pair, and introduce PI-DTW to fix WL-SOT's label-alignment bottleneck.
*Result:* Diarization conditioning wins at 16.18%/23.36% average cpWER (oracle/real diarization) against cascaded's 45.25%/42.27%.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — streaming ASR is Tier-1, and the axis this paper measures is the one practitioners actually get burned by: **single-speaker regression.** Table 2 is the part to read. SSA costs 0.28 WER points against the unmodified Nemotron Speech base model (7.44 vs 7.16 average on the OpenASR leaderboard), while WL-SOT collapses to 16.95 — a 2.4× degradation that a multi-speaker-only evaluation would never have exposed. Quality 8 for a controlled design where the shared foundation means the differences really are architectural, a genuine algorithmic contribution in PI-DTW rather than a pure survey, and conspicuous honesty: they benchmark against **offline DiCoW-v3.2 at 15.11% average cpWER and report that it still beats their best streaming system at 23.36%**, and they re-ran the open-source baselines on identical audio and annotations rather than quoting published numbers. Held to 8 because the conclusion is a ranking of existing approaches rather than a new best system, the diarizer is held fixed so diarizer quality and architecture choice remain entangled, and the largest oracle-to-real gap (AMI SDM, 20.27 → 39.91) says the winning architecture is also the one most exposed to diarization error — a caveat worth carrying into your own deployment maths.

## 3. StreamAlign: Streaming Text-Aligned Speech Tokenization — `2609.09719` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Kang-wook Kim, Jinyoung Park, Jinsoo Kim, Sehun Lee, Sang Hoon Woo, Gunhee Kim* — **Seoul National University**, **UC Berkeley**, **KRAFTON** and **Georgia Institute of Technology** · cs.CL, cs.SD, eess.AS · submitted 2026-09-09 · EMNLP 2026 Findings · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09719)

*Problem:* Text-aligned speech tokenizers align speech units to LLM token space, but they depend on offline ASR — so they need the complete utterance before tokenizing, and the ASR/LLM vocabulary mismatch coarsens acoustic granularity from subword to word level.
*Method:* Online alignment combining character-level RNN-Transducer alignment with word-level ASR guidance, plus a proactive word-boundary classifier that finalises subword tokens before the next word onset.
*Result:* Latency 560 ms → 270 ms; WER 4.41 and UTMOS 4.23, best among evaluated tokenizers, at the lowest unit rate.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 10 and the best fit in the window — this is your Primary Focus stated in its own terms. Speech-text modality alignment, speech tokenizers, and streaming are three separate Tier-1/Core entries on your profile and this paper is the intersection of all three; it is also the only tokenizer in its Table 1 that is simultaneously streaming *and* text-aligned, which makes it the reference point for anyone arguing that text alignment is incompatible with real-time interaction. Quality 8 for a well-posed mechanism — the character-level-alignment-with-word-level-guidance trick is a real answer to the ASR/LLM vocabulary mismatch rather than a workaround, and the proactive boundary classifier is what converts the idea into a latency number — evaluated on both axes that matter, reconstruction (WER, UTMOS, SECS, F0 RMSE) and downstream spoken language modelling (SALMon, spoken StoryCloze, human-rated continuation), with a public project page. The **Limitations section is unusually candid** and worth reading before you adopt it: English-only, read-style and web-scale speech, and an acknowledged dependence on the guiding streaming recogniser — though they check in §4.5.4 that recognition errors are not copied deterministically into the units, which is the right control. Held to 8 because speaker similarity is the consistent loser (SECS trades off against Mimi and MagiCodec), and multilingual and code-switched behaviour — the settings you care about most — is explicitly left open.

## 4. Xiaomi-CocktailASR-1 Technical Report — `2609.11274` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Yiru Zhang, Hang Su, Lichun Fan, Ying Zeng, Chang Liu, Yifeng Wang, Yuquan Liang, Tao Li, Lian Li, Wenhao Yang, Jian Luan, Cong Zou, Heng Qu* — **Xiaomi Inc.** (Xiaoai Plus, ASR team), China · cs.SD, cs.CL, eess.AS · submitted 2026-09-10 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11274)

*Problem:* LLM-based ASR has advanced fast but has no answer to the cocktail-party setting; existing target-speaker ASR degrades single-speaker accuracy and cannot say "this speaker is not here."
*Method:* End-to-end LLM-based TS-ASR that takes reference speech as a voiceprint prompt and transcribes the target directly — no separation, no diarization front-end — trained to emit empty text on speaker-absent mixtures, with an optional chain-of-thought mode.
*Result:* 4.11%/12.29% WER on LibriMix 2/3-mix and 2.90%/4.91% on LibriSpeechMix, ahead of prior specialist TS-ASR.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — LLM-based ASR is Tier-1, and the negative-rejection capability is the under-served half of the problem: a recogniser that transcribes confidently when the target speaker never spoke is the failure mode that makes voice assistants unusable, and almost nobody reports it. Quality 7 for numbers that are hard to argue with. The table is the strongest evidence: **Qwen3-ASR-1.7b scores 68.75% and StepAudio2 71.23% WER on LibriMix 2-mix, and Gemini-2.5-Pro 48.41%**, against CocktailASR-1's 4.11 — general-purpose audio LLMs simply do not do this task, and the paper shows that rather than asserting it. It also clears the specialist baselines it was actually competing with (TCP 4.84, CONF-TSASR 5.40, MT-LLM 6.70), which is the comparison that counts. Held to 7 by the structural limits of a technical report: **no ablation** separating the voiceprint-prompt design from the training data or the LLM backbone, the CoT mode is announced without an accuracy attribution, no open-weights commitment is stated, and the evaluation leans on synthetic mixtures (LibriMix, LibriSpeechMix) where the real-world portion is described but not broken out. Treat it as a bar to clear, not a reproducible baseline — and note that it and #2 reach opposite architectural conclusions on the same problem, which is the more interesting thing about reading them together.

## 5. BuzzASR: A Swarm of 100+ Monolingual Speech Recognition Models — `2609.09554` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Shivam Singh, Aditya Yadavalli, Catherine Arnett, Alex Warstadt* — **UC San Diego** and **EleutherAI** · cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-09 · EMNLP 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09554)

*Problem:* Massively multilingual ASR models underperform on their own long tail; monolingual fine-tuning is a known fix that has only ever been applied to a handful of languages, so nobody knows how far down the tail it holds.
*Method:* Fine-tune Whisper separately for each of the 102 FLEURS languages, plus a stronger variant that replaces the multilingual BPE tokenizer with a monolingual one and augments with text-only fine-tuning.
*Result:* Beats Whisper-large-v3 on 77 of 102 languages, average CER cut by over 2.8×; SOTA open-source CER on 27 languages; 3.3× average tokenizer compression gain, up to 21.7×.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — multilingual and low-resource ASR is Tier-1, and this is the rare paper in that area that ends with **102 downloadable models** rather than a table, which makes it immediately useful as a baseline or a starting checkpoint for any language in your set. The tokenizer-replacement result is the part worth your attention independently of the models: a 3.3× average and 21.7× peak improvement in characters-per-token says Whisper's multilingual BPE is badly mismatched to most of its tail languages, which is a concrete, transferable diagnosis of *why* massively-multilingual recognisers underperform there, not just a demonstration that fine-tuning helps. Quality 7 rather than higher because the authors are explicit that the core method is long-known — this is a scaling and engineering contribution, and the novelty lives entirely in the tokenizer-replacement variant and the breadth of the sweep. Full release (models, code, per-language results) and EMNLP acceptance carry it; the honest ledger is that it **loses to Whisper-large-v3 on 25 of 102 languages** and the paper's framing does not dwell on which ones or why, and FLEURS plus Common Voice is read speech, so the gains should be read as an upper bound on what monolingual specialisation buys you in the field.
