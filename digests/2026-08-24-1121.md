## ★ 1. TurboBias 2.0: Streaming Context-Biasing for Production-Efficient ASR Systems — `2608.21343` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Vladimir Bataev, Lilit Grigoryan, Andrei Andrusenko, Nikolay Karpov, Vitaly Lavrukhin, Boris Ginsburg* — **NVIDIA** (Yerevan & Santa Clara) · eess.AS, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.SD · 2026-08-21 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21343)

*Problem:* Context-biasing methods raise rare-word accuracy but ignore what production needs: streaming, batched decoding, and a *separate* phrase list per concurrent user.
*Method:* Extend GPU-accelerated TurboBias with a case-insensitive boosting graph (no lowercase/title-case list expansion) and per-stream biasing graphs inside batched inference, plus streaming Transducer beam search.
*Result:* On Contextual Earnings-22 with parakeet-unified-en-0.6b, keyword F-score goes 65.2 → 91.2 (P 95.8 / R 87.0) while WER drops 14.4% → 12.2%, beating the published CTC-WS/Argmax point (88.2 F, 12.7% WER); 128-stream speed study; shipped in NeMo.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed author (Boris Ginsburg). Contextual biasing and rare-word/entity recognition sit at the centre of your primary focus, and this is the rare version that treats *per-user* context lists as a first-class constraint rather than an afterthought — the case-insensitivity result alone (target-cased 80.6 vs case-insensitive 81.4 F, with the capitalization dependence removed) is the kind of detail you only find by shipping. Quality 8 because the numbers are real, the ablation isolates each contribution, the CTC-WS comparison is scrupulously honest about being a reproduction below the published point, and the whole thing is merged into NeMo as three named PRs; short of 9 only because the contribution is engineering refinement of TurboBias 1.0 rather than a new idea, and half the streaming evidence rests on an internal medical set nobody else can run.

## 2. Do SpeechLMs Hear Their Own Opinions? Diagnosing and Mitigating Previous-Belief Contamination in Streaming Emotion Understanding — `2608.20769` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Haoyue Liu, Zhichao Wang, Ye Chen* (**Xi'an Jiaotong University**, XJTU-POLIMI Joint School); *Haonan Deng* (**UC Berkeley**); *Xiaoying Tang* (**CUHK-Shenzhen**, FNii-Shenzhen) · cs.SD, cs.AI · 2026-08-21 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20769)

*Problem:* Streaming speech models feed their own previous prediction back as history — but nobody has tested whether that history overrides what the current audio actually says.
*Method:* A counterfactual diagnostic that flips only the injected prior label while holding audio fixed; then EmoUpdate, a training-free fix separating perception from state revision via an acoustic "firewall", an evidence-shrunk belief filter, and a closed-form decontamination operator.
*Result:* Flipping the prior alone drops current-audio accuracy 72.50% → 30.42% and flips 65.69% of predictions, with prior-pull ranging 4.76–98.20%. EmoUpdate wins all eight model–benchmark settings across four SpeechLMs.

**Why it ranked:** This is Friday's #3 — "Represented but Ignored" — restated as a *behavioural* failure with a mechanism you can act on, and it lands on your central question from another angle: when a speech-LLM's text-side prior and its acoustic evidence disagree, the prior wins, catastrophically. That a 42-point accuracy collapse can be induced by changing one token of injected context is the finding worth carrying around. Quality 7 rather than higher because the diagnostic is far stronger than the remedy: the counterfactual design, leakage controls, difficulty-stratified analysis and compute-fairness audit are genuinely careful, but the headline "+69.71 S-BAcc" is measured against the authors' own controlled baselines rather than published methods, the three-component framework is more apparatus than the problem needs, and CREMA-D is acted emotion — a shaky floor for a claim about perception.

## 3. OmniAssistBench: Assistant-style Interaction Benchmark for Omni-LLMs — `2608.21360` — **Relevance 6/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Xianyun Sun, Chaoyou Fu, Zhengye Zhang, Feiyang Duan, Qingyuan Cao, Yonghui Niu, Sihang Yuan, Ge Zhang, Caifeng Shan* — **Nankai University**, **Nanjing University**, **University of Waterloo** · cs.CV · 2026-08-21 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21360)

*Problem:* Omni-LLMs are pitched as real-time assistants, but static offline datasets cannot evaluate interaction, where the model's own answer changes what the user does next.
*Method:* Reverse-engineer internet videos into multi-turn interaction scripts with predefined route priors, embed every user question as TTS speech in the video stream, and score against those priors; 300 videos, 685 QA, 1,000+ expert person-hours.
*Result:* Gemini-3-Pro scores 66.4/100, Qwen3-Omni-Instruct 51.2. Models mishandle gesture prompts, lose historical context across turns, and answer before the target event.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance is only moderate — this is a video-assistant benchmark at heart — but two of its findings are squarely speech-side and worth your attention: with audio-only input models "blindly reply to any spoken query they hear," failing to track *which* user is speaking across turns, and the audio-only/visual-only ablation isolates that this failure is caused by the presence of distracting speech. That is a concrete, measurable speech-input weakness in the current Omni-LLM generation. Quality 8 for a benchmark built the expensive way — filmed real-world cases, priors that collapse the diverging-path problem, an open-ended metric designed to defeat multiple-choice shortcuts — with a clean frontier-vs-open gap; short of 9 because TTS-injected questions are a proxy for real interactive speech, and 300 videos is small for the number of sub-tasks it slices.

## 4. Training DeepFilterNet with Accurate Room Acoustic Simulations Improves Single-Channel Speech Enhancement — `2608.20971` — **Relevance 6/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Alessia Milo, Georg Götz, Steinar Guðjónsson, Daniel Gert Nielsen, Jesper Pedersen, Finnur Pind* — **Treble Technologies** · eess.AS, cs.LG, physics.comp-ph · 2026-08-21 · IWAENC 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20971)

*Problem:* Nearly every speech pipeline augments with image-source-method RIRs because they are cheap — but nobody has measured what that approximation costs downstream.
*Method:* Hold DeepFilterNet3 fixed and swap only the RIR corpus — DNS4 image-source vs. a hybrid wave-based/geometrical-acoustics dataset — then evaluate on *unseen measured* RIRs with objective metrics and downstream NeMo ASR.
*Result:* Objective gains are modest (PESQ +0.166, 95% CI [+0.149, +0.184]); WER gains are not. Hybrid reaches 0.1274 WER vs ISM's 0.1452 against a 0.1671 noisy baseline — 23.8% relative improvement vs 13.1%.

**Why it ranked:** Enhancement is secondary for you, but the finding is not an enhancement finding — it is a *training-data* finding with an ASR number attached, and it is directly actionable: the realism of your synthetic reverberation roughly doubles the relative WER benefit you get out of the same model, and in the small-data regime the ISM pipeline delivers almost nothing (0.5–3.3% relative) where hybrid delivers 12–16%. Quality 7 for a disciplined single-variable design, evaluation on measured rather than simulated RIRs, utterance-paired bootstrap CIs on every metric, released code, and a limitations section that refuses to attribute the gain to any individual acoustic factor; capped there because it is a 5-page workshop paper from the vendor of the simulation SDK being validated, and a single enhancement model on a single test set cannot generalize the claim.

## 5. A Factorial Ablation of a Speech-to-SFT Pipeline: Differential Effects on Data Quality and Downstream Transfer — `2608.20394` — **Relevance 6/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Wonsup Shin, Jingu Kim* — **Flitto** · cs.SD, cs.CL · submitted 2026-07-01, announced 2026-08-24 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20394)

*Problem:* Industry pipelines that turn recorded speech into LLM fine-tuning data stack refinement stages that have never been publicly ablated, so each stage's marginal value is unknown.
*Method:* A 2×2 factorial over transcript refinement and SFT-quality refinement on Korean medical and finance conference recordings, fine-tuning 9 models across 5 families (2.4B–70B), judged by four cross-provider LLM judges, six blind human experts, and three MCQA benchmarks.
*Result:* Judged quality rises consistently and all six humans agree — yet mean downstream MCQA gain is not significant, concentrating only on family–domain aligned pairs.

**Why it ranked:** A negative result, honestly reported, on a question anyone building speech-derived training corpora will hit: better-rated data is not the same as more useful data, and the paper diagnoses why — the refinement stage shifts composition toward explanatory items while MCQA probes factoid recall. That format-mismatch explanation is the transferable part. Quality 7 is high for a two-author industry report because the evidence is unusually broad for the claim: cross-provider judges *plus* blind human raters *plus* three benchmarks agreeing, a Whisper-medium STT swap as a robustness check, a non-hallucination audit, and code, prompts and all SFT checkpoints released. Held below 8 by a Korean-only, two-domain scope and by MCQA being a narrow proxy for the downstream utility it is generalizing about.
