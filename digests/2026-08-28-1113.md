## ★ 1. AudioSpan: Spanning the Duration and Depth of Audio Comprehension — `2608.26431` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Wen Huang, Yunfei Chu, Meng Gao, Haolin He, Jin Xu* — **Qwen Team, Alibaba Group**, **Tsinghua University**, **CUHK** · cs.SD, eess.AS · 2026-08-26 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26431)

*Problem:* LALM benchmarks still use second-long clips where scores saturate and models converge; recent long-form sets extend duration but test long audio much as short clips are tested.
*Method:* Pair 10-minute-to-2-hour audio with 3,240 questions across perception, understanding and reasoning, built two ways — Native QA drawn from the content, and Anchor QA that plants acoustic anchors and scores the perception-to-reasoning chain only to its first error.
*Result:* Across 12 LALMs the hard part comes *before* reasoning — distilling a few relevant facts from a long, redundant signal; best ~79.2%, falling steadily with duration, worst on temporal grounding.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed lab (Qwen Team, Alibaba). The Anchor-QA design is the reason this is worth your time rather than another leaderboard: planting known acoustic events and scoring only up to the first broken link turns a long-audio QA score into a *diagnosis* of where the chain snaps, which is exactly what aggregate accuracy hides. Relevance 7 — long-form audio comprehension is Core rather than Primary for you, but the finding (perception, not reasoning, is the bottleneck at length) bears directly on how speech-conditioned LLMs degrade over real recordings. Quality 7 for 12 models spanning open and closed (Gemini-3.1-Pro through Voxtral and Phi-4-Multimodal), a released HF dataset, and a genuinely novel construction. Held at 7 on two counts: every item is machine-generated through a captioning → QA → adversarial-critic pipeline with no human verification reported, and the top of the table is a Qwen model on a Qwen-authored benchmark — the ranking is worth treating as indicative, not settled.

## ★ 2. TurnBench: A Multi-Domain Benchmark for Turn-Taking Dynamics in Spoken Dialogue — `2608.25218` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Freeman Jiang, Ramon Sanabria, Soham Deshmukh, Bandhav Veluri, Simon Michael Vuch Williams, Elliott K. Suen, Garreth Lee, Kevin Yoonho Choi, Takuya Umeki, Riku Kubo, Sathvik Udupa, Chien-yu Huang, Shih-Yun Shan Kuan, Zhuoyan Tao, Satyapriya Krishna, Sefik Emre Eskimez, Yu Tsao, Hung-yi Lee, Shinji Watanabe* — **Sesame AI**, **Carnegie Mellon University**, **National Taiwan University**, **Academia Sinica**, Mundo AI, Oto, Brno University of Technology · eess.AS, cs.CL · submitted to IEEE SLT 2026 · 2026-08-25 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25218)

*Problem:* Turn-taking evaluation has no consistent, linguistically grounded protocol and no hand-annotated data covering diverse conversation types, so duplex systems are compared on incommensurable numbers.
*Method:* A 30-hour triple-annotated corpus of dyadic human conversation spanning six interaction styles, with conversation type as a controlled experimental variable, plus a standardized protocol for end-of-turn and interruption detection; 14 heterogeneous systems benchmarked.
*Result:* End-of-turn recall is stable across types while interruption false positives are strongly type-dependent and concentrate in backchannel-dense styles; humans start speaking a median 151 ms *before* a turn ends, and no system matches that without excessive false positives.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed authors (Hung-yi Lee, Shinji Watanabe) and followed labs (CMU, NTU). This is the best-built artifact in the window: triple annotation, conversation type promoted from confound to controlled variable, 14 systems, and a released 30-hour eval corpus *plus* a 104-hour training set, a public leaderboard and an interactive viewer — the release is the contribution, and it is unusually complete. The 151 ms anticipation result is the kind of finding that reframes a design target: current systems are not merely slow, they are structurally reactive where humans are predictive. Relevance 7 because full-duplex and conversational modelling sit in your Core tier rather than the ASR/alignment centre. Quality 8 rather than 9 only because 30 hours is modest for a benchmark meant to arbitrate a field, the corpus is dyadic English-centric, and an industry-led benchmark hosting its own leaderboard carries the usual standing question about who curates the ranking.

## 3. Can We Read the Mind of an Audio LLM? A Verbalizable, Multilingual Middle-Layer Workspace — `2608.24958` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Jiajun Fan, Jingyuan Li, Prashanth Gurunath Shivakumar, Qi Luo, Jia-Hong Huang, M. Maruf, Roger Ren, Yile Gu, Rahul Pandey, Ge Liu, Ivan Bulyko* — **Amazon AGI Foundations**, **University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign** · cs.SD, cs.AI, cs.CL · 2026-08-25 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24958)

*Problem:* An audio LLM is a black box in a specific way — you see what it says, never what it worked out on the way there, and CoT monitoring only helps if the model writes its reasoning down.
*Method:* Apply a logit lens at the *audio-token* positions of a base Qwen3-Omni-30B, with activation patching, single-layer deletion, and a waveform-swap control that holds the text identical and changes only the sound.
*Result:* The answer becomes legible in words in the middle layers before any token is emitted — 40.0% balanced accuracy vs 21.8% on silence, hidden multi-hop chains with no CoT, 38.5% of top-1 readouts in Chinese on English inputs, and paralinguistic content the model's own caption discards.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — this is your Primary Focus stated as a mechanism question and answered by reading the model directly. The three findings that matter most to you: the middle-layer workspace is *language-agnostic* (concepts surface in several scripts at once), it is *paralinguistic* (it forms speaker role and affect that a text transcription throws away, and answers correctly more often from audio than from its own caption), and the audio-driven signal separates cleanly from the text prior in the 35–80% depth band. If speech–text alignment is about how much of the backbone's knowledge survives the audio path, this says the knowledge is *there* and legible — the losses are downstream. Quality 7: the controls are the strong part — activation patching recovering 95.7% of the decision gap, layer deletion mapping entry/interior/output roles, and the waveform-swap isolating audio from an option prior. But the paper says plainly that "the quantities are controls, not benchmark scores," 40.0% balanced accuracy is a modest absolute signal, the headline Watergate→Nixon chain is a single anecdote, no code is released, and everything rests on one model family.

## 4. SpeechGym: An Audio-Native Gym for Training Voice Agents via Reinforcement Learning — `2608.26432` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Jiajun Fan, Jingyuan Li, Prashanth Gurunath Shivakumar, Jia-Hong Huang, Qi Luo, M. Maruf, Ivan Bulyko, Ge Liu, Roger Ren* — **Amazon AGI Foundations**, **University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign** · cs.SD, cs.AI, cs.CL · 2026-08-26 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26432)

*Problem:* Voice agents must call tools and hold multi-turn dialogue entirely through speech, but existing frameworks either cascade TTS/ASR around a proprietary API where gradients cannot flow, or stay in text — they measure voice agents without being able to improve them.
*Method:* Two omni-modal models converse in native audio with no external ASR or TTS and no API boundary, over the unmodified tasks, tools and success check of τ²-bench, so modality is the only variable and the loop stays locally trainable; outcome-only GRPO is replaced by a per-turn process reward crediting each successful tool call.
*Result:* Pass@1 on the independent τ-Voice benchmark rises 24% → 53% with no further tuning, carrying an open-weights 30B model from last place to second, while turns fall 26 → 24 and tokens 51,195 → 48,398.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — spoken language understanding under a real task pressure, and the failure analysis is the useful half: the agent picks the right tool and the right argument slot then fills it with a value *misheard from the waveform*, which is an ASR error wearing an agentic costume. That reframes voice-agent quality as a recognition problem, which is your territory. Quality 8 because the central methodological claim is sharp and falsifiable — outcome-only GRPO is gradient-starved when nearly every rollout in a group fails identically, and per-turn credit restores variance to nearly every group — and it is validated by cross-pipeline transfer to a benchmark the authors did not implement, more than doubling success. Not 9: no code or environment release is announced, results rest on a single 30B open-weights model, "second on that leaderboard" depends on which systems are reported, and the efficiency gains (two fewer turns, ~5% fewer tokens) are marginal next to the success jump.

## 5. Direct or Mediated? Task-Dependent Audio Information Routing in Large Audio Language Models — `2608.27026` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Yizhou Zhang, Wangjin Zhou, Xin Gu, Yichi Wang, Wei Tan, Yi Zhao, Zhi Gong, Keisuke Imoto, Tatsuya Kawahara* — **Kyoto University**, **Tencent (WXG)** · cs.SD, cs.MM · 2026-08-27 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27026)

*Problem:* LALMs are evaluated almost entirely on single coherent audio segments, leaving their behaviour under unfamiliar input configurations — and the mechanism behind it — unexamined.
*Method:* Concatenate two audio segments into one input as a controlled probe, then trace routing through the decoder with layer-wise attention knockout and probe prompt-token representations, across four LALMs.
*Result:* A striking task-dependent gap — ASR barely moves (WER up 0.10–1.30 points, still under 4.5%) while AQA collapses from 85–98% to as low as 35%. ASR uses direct retrieval from audio tokens; AQA depends on a mediated route through prompt tokens, and the audio attributes stay decodable even as accuracy craters.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — a mechanistic account of *how* audio reaches the answer, separating a direct audio→answer path from a prompt-mediated one, which is the routing-level version of your modality-alignment question. Read alongside #3 it is the more interesting pairing in this digest: two independent groups, different methods (attention knockout vs logit lens), same conclusion — the information survives the audio path and then fails to be *used*. That the dissociation is task-dependent, with ASR on the robust direct route, also explains why recognition benchmarks keep looking healthy while understanding degrades. Quality 7 for a clean controlled design, four models including Qwen3-Omni and Step-Audio-R1, and a causal intervention rather than correlational probing. Capped there because the concatenation setup is artificial and it is fair to ask whether it stresses a real deployment condition, the paper is short (8 pages, 2 figures) with no release, and the AQA accuracy bands are reported coarsely enough that per-model conclusions are hard to pin down.

### Also noted, not ranked

- **★ `2608.26992` — Benchmarking Fast Domain Adaptation for Unsupervised Speech Units** (Robin San Roman, Manel Khentout, Tu Anh Nguyen, Paul Michel, **Yossi Adi**, Emmanuel Dupoux). Followed author, and ABX-Accent — 10 English accents, <10 h unlabelled each — is a reasonable low-resource adaptation benchmark. Left off the list on quality: the baseline is a Contrastive Predictive Coding model with Zero Resource Challenge metrics, which reads as several years behind current SSL practice, the data is promised only "upon paper acceptance," and even the title is submitted with underscores in it. Worth a glance if accented-speech adaptation is live for you; not worth a slot.
- **★ `2608.25289` and `2608.25285`** (**Junichi Yamagishi**) — self-embedding audio watermarking combined with ultra-low-bitrate neural codecs, and a training-free proactive defence against partial speech manipulation. Followed author, competent work, but watermarking and anti-spoofing sit in your Secondary tier and neither is a standout.
- **`2608.26434` — AfriSwitch** (Gabrial Zencha Ashungafac, Busayo Awobade, Tobi Olatunji — **Intron Health**). The closest miss. 61.36 h of human-transcribed *in-the-wild* code-switched speech across 16 African languages, released CC BY 4.0 on HuggingFace with switch-level tags, per-utterance Code-Mixing Index and switch-point counts. Five systems zero-shot: best average 35.93% WER (Sahara V2.5), none below 24% on any language, and the useful finding that Africa-targeted training predicts performance better than model scale or nominal language coverage. If code-switching is active work for you, take this one first — it lost the slot to #5 only on relevance to the alignment question, not on merit.
- **`2608.26005` — VoiceMem** (Zhifei Xie, Ziyang Ma, Dongchao Yang, Shuicheng Yan and colleagues; 161 HF upvotes). Streaming memory for duplex SLMs, 134 ms retrieval inside VAD latency, deployed. Genuinely practical, but the "informational left brain / emotional right brain" framing oversells a retrieval-plus-persona stack, and beating Mem0's top-200 with top-5 retrieval is not the comparison it appears to be.
- **`2608.25976` — Lost but not erased: Finding traces of a forgotten language in neural speech models** (Peter Plantinga and colleagues, Concordia/McGill). Trains ASR models on one language then switches abruptly, as a model of international adoptees. First-language traces persist in the *lowest, pre-phonemic* layers and are functional — 14% faster relearning, an advantage that vanishes when the earliest layers are swapped in from a non-adopted model. Framed as cognitive science, but it is a clean, well-controlled study of where catastrophic forgetting does and does not bite in a speech encoder.
