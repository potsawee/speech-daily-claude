### 1. ★ CRESiST: Self-Enhancing Explorative Policy Learning for Simultaneous Speech Translation — `2611.06501` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

Chenyang Le, Pei Zhang, Yuxiang Chen, Bing Han, Derek F. Wong, Baosong Yang, Yanmin Qian — **Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Alibaba Group (Token Hub); University of Macau; University College London** · cs.CL, cs.SD · 2026-11-04 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06501)

*Problem:* LLM-based simultaneous speech translation recomputes the KV cache for every new prefix. It also needs external segmentation (VAD), so it can't run on unbounded audio streams.
*Method:* Qwen2.5-Omni-7B reads 400 ms speech chunks interleaved with text tokens. This allows full KV-cache reuse and needs no segmentation. An RNN-T "actor" samples read/write paths; the speech LLM acts as "critic", scoring them with consistency and latency rewards and distilling the best policy back into the actor.
*Result:* On unsegmented MuST-C and ACL 60/60 streams it scores 73.7–86.0 XCOMET at 0.8–1.5 s LongYAAL. That is within about 2 points of oracle segmentation and well ahead of InfiniSST, StreamAtt and StreamUni. On FLEURS zh↔en it beats the commercial Seed LiveInterpret 2.0.

**Why it ranked:** Simultaneous speech translation with a speech LLM is primary-tier, and this comes from followed author Yanmin Qian (NeurIPS 2026), with ablations of the training stages and the actor architecture. It is held at 8 for four reasons. The targets are Qwen3-235B pseudo-translations. The backbone is the older Qwen2.5-Omni. Offline quality drops on ACL 60/60, which the authors attribute to forgetting. Code is promised, not yet released.

### 2. ★ Pandemonium: Longform Target-Speaker Speech Recognition with Cross-Speaker Interactions — `2611.10659` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**

Alexander Polok, Dominik Klement, Ivan Medennikov, Antoine Laurent, Petr Pálka, Samuele Cornell, Shinji Watanabe, Sanjeev Khudanpur, Jagadeesh Balam, Jan Černocký, Lukáš Burget, Matthew Wiesner — **Brno University of Technology; NVIDIA; pyannoteAI; Carnegie Mellon University; Johns Hopkins University** · eess.AS, cs.CL, cs.SD · 2026-11-07 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10659)

*Problem:* Diarization-conditioned Whisper (DiCoW) transcribes each speaker separately, in 30 s windows. It never sees the other speakers or the longer conversation.
*Method:* The diarization conditioning moves into a long-form Parakeet (DiCoP). Cross-speaker attention and a refiner that re-estimates the diarization mask are added. Training runs synthetic mixtures → 4.6k h of pseudo-labelled YODAS2 conversations → real meetings.
*Result:* With predicted diarization it beats the best published single-microphone results on NOTSOFAR-1, AMI, ICSI, Mixer6 and DiPCo; on DiPCo it scores 27.4 cpWER against 34.0. With oracle diarization the average falls from 16.3 cpWER (DiCoW) to 14.7, at RTFx ≈330. The models and 5.4k h of pseudo-labels are released.

**Why it ranked:** It is long-form multi-talker ASR from followed author Shinji Watanabe with CMU, JHU and NVIDIA, with clean ablations and models and data released. It is held at 8 for three reasons. The backbone is English-only (Mandarin pseudo-labels were dropped). With predicted diarization it gains only 0.8 cpWER on average over DiCoW. Cross-speaker attention on its own adds just 0.3: most of the gain comes from the mask refiner and long-form training.

### 3. Confucius4-R2T2: A Stable-Prefix Streaming ASR Model for Real Real-Time Transcription — `2611.06043` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

Daniel Huang, Qi He, Shaoxiong Duan, Yingjie Li, Qiao Cheng, Yitao Duan — **NetEase Youdao** · cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.SD · 2026-11-04 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06043)

*Problem:* Translators and voice agents act on streaming ASR output as it arrives, so text that is later revised costs them. Yet stable-prefix systems usually give up accuracy or add lag.
*Method:* It defines a Longest Stable Prefix (LSP) label, proven to be monotone and consistent with the full-audio output. Qwen3-ASR-1.7B is post-trained on LSP targets from 1,500 h of Chinese and English, with the encoder and projector frozen and the architecture unchanged.
*Result:* At 160 ms chunks the average error over 13 Chinese and English sets is 5.76%; full-context decoding gets 5.15%. That beats Nemotron 3.5 Streaming (14.21), Voxtral Realtime (19.35) and three commercial APIs (6.01–8.27), with lower lag. Gains carry over to 10 languages it was not trained on, and the weights are open.

**Why it ranked:** It turns an LLM-based ASR model into a stable-prefix streaming one, which sits right on your streaming and LLM-ASR focus. The core idea is simple and formal, the weights are open, and it is tested on 13 sets. It is held at 8 for three reasons. The proprietary APIs' configurations are opaque, and two of them still beat it on Chinese. Lag is measured after the fact against forced alignments. The "three-quarters error reduction" headline compares against an unadapted 160 ms baseline that hallucinates.

### 4. Walrus Technical Report — `2611.13689` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

Alkis Koudounas, Osamu Take, Hayato Futami, Quentin Jodelet, Emiru Tsunoo — **Sony Group Corporation (Speech & Language AI Lab)** · eess.AS, cs.CL · 2026-11-10 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13689)

*Problem:* Multi-task speech LLMs trade tasks off against one another, and it is unclear how much of their quality comes from data rather than architecture.
*Method:* The Gemma 4 E4B architecture, with its USM encoder, is kept unchanged and all weights are fine-tuned through a three-stage data curriculum. The stages run from broad multilingual data, through multi-talker, code-switching and context/keyword-biased ASR and synthetic dialogues, to domain data. Task- and dataset-level loss weights protect ASR from forgetting. Training uses 600M samples, more than 20 tasks and about 100 languages.
*Result:* It beats 4–10B speech LLMs on:
- MLS (6.89 WER);
- VoxPopuli;
- CS-FLEURS code-switching (7.23 CER, against 32.30);
- LibriMix cpWER;
- CoVoST-2 speech translation (42.6 BLEU);
- speech-to-speech translation;
- MMAU-Pro (71.9%).

A variant trained only on commercially usable data is also reported.

**Why it ranked:** Multilingual and code-switching ASR, speech translation and forgetting-aware task weighting come together in one speech LLM, and the data pipelines (including keyword and context biasing) are documented in detail. It is held at 7 for these reasons:
- No ablation separates the curriculum from the weights, which the authors concede.
- The code-switching training data and part of that test set (XTTS) are both TTS-generated.
- LibriMix is in-domain.
- Keyword-biased ASR is never evaluated.
- Text-ability retention is not measured.
- The paper does not say whether the weights are released; it gives only a project page.

### 5. Beyond the Transcript: Measuring and Improving the Use of Acoustic Information in Audio Language Models — `2611.11573` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**

Linkai Peng, Baorian Nuchged, Yuyang Yao — **University of Connecticut; The University of Texas at Austin; University of Oxford** · cs.CL · 2026-11-08 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11573)

*Problem:* Audio-LM benchmark scores mix up real acoustic understanding with what the transcript alone gives away. So it is unclear how much these models actually use the sound.
*Method:* Each recording is scored against TTS re-readings of its transcript and against transcript-plus-audio. This is done for 19 open models and Gemini 3.1 Pro on 92 tasks from 6 benchmarks. Linear probes then check each layer of Gemma-4 12B and Qwen2.5-Omni-7B. Finally, LoRA trains only the layers where the acoustic signal fades, with a KL "content anchor" to the base model.
*Result:* Open models trail Gemini by 28.6 pp on speaker/emotion-type tasks and 41.9 pp on stress/intonation-type tasks, about half of it from failing to use the audio. Yet the cues are readable in early layers. The fix lifts accuracy on those tasks by 12.8–44.4 pp, while lexical accuracy stays within 1 pp (it drops 5.6–10.5 pp without the anchor).

**Why it ranked:** It cleanly measures how speech LLMs underuse acoustic information and gives an adaptation recipe that avoids forgetting, which sits squarely on your speech–text alignment and knowledge-retention theme; the code and adapters are released. It is held at 7 for three reasons. The training data (80% LibriSpeech) covers the question formats of 34 of the evaluated tasks. The TTS reference mixes in natural-versus-synthetic speech differences, which the authors acknowledge. Only two backbones are repaired.

### Close calls (checked, ranked, placed below the line)

- **★ Nemotron 3 Diarization** — `2611.12246` — NVIDIA (Boris Ginsburg) — R 6 · Q 8.
  - Streaming Sortformer is extended to 8 speakers, with a pure-Transformer encoder and learned upsampling from 80 ms to 10 ms frames.
  - DER is 12.73 on DIHARD III (previous best 14.1) and 9.10 on CALLHOME (10.08). Training is 2.2× faster.
  - Feeding its posteriors to DiCoW gives 18.95 tcpWER on NOTSOFAR-1 single-channel, which beats the CHiME-8 winner (23.63). The weights are open.
  - It sits below the line only because diarization is a secondary interest. It pairs naturally with #2.
- **★ Speculative Listening (full-duplex SLMs)** — `2611.06982` — CUHK-Shenzhen (Zhizheng Wu) — R 7 · Q 7.
  - Training-free, lossless decoding: during idle listening time the model drafts its next chunk as if silence will follow. Real audio leaves 95.3% of chunks unchanged.
  - Across 4 models, speak chunks run 2.6–2.8× faster, tool-call chunks up to 23.4× faster, and the delay before a response starts drops by 73%. It comes with a new benchmark, TickTock-Bench.
- **E-Gaborformer** — `2611.08092` — Deepdub — R 8 · Q 6.
  - A strictly causal Gated DeltaNet-2 / sliding-window-attention encoder with Gabor-atom convolutions. Its streaming state is a constant 19 MB per call.
  - It matches E-Branchformer in a controlled LibriSpeech study.
  - The headline "28–31% lower WER than Nemotron 3.5 Streaming" rests on a 24-recording, 1,214-word production set. Nemotron 3.5 was also used as a training teacher, and nothing is released.
- **KeyASRBench** — `2611.10852` — Southwest University / UCASS — R 9 · Q 5.
  - It measures whether whole keyword phrases (names, products, versions) survive ASR.
  - WER/MER is 5.3–12.9%, but keyword error rate is 13.6–33.8%.
  - It asks exactly your entity-preservation question. But it is a two-author pilot whose 10 h challenge set was selected using the very models it evaluates.
- **★ IRIS-Embed** — `2611.09122` — NTU (Hung-yi Lee) — R 6 · Q 7.
  - Audio LLMs become instruction-aware speech-retrieval embedders through contrastive training and layer weighting that depends on the instruction.
  - INSPIRE Recall@50 rises from 45.2 to 62.9; INSPIRE is the group's own benchmark. MAEB improves by 9.4–19.8 points.
- **Earlybird** — `2611.14421` — single author — R 7 · Q 6.
  - A 29M streaming recognizer trained on audio cut mid-word, so it learns to predict the word still being spoken.
  - The median word is right 90 ms before it ends; other streaming models get it 90–400 ms after it ends. Its confidence is calibrated.
  - It roughly ties Whistle on the Open ASR Leaderboard sets. A clever idea, but small-scale and English-only.
- **★ Holistic parallel supervision for expressive S2ST** — `2611.06010` — JHU CLSP (Berrak Sisman, Philipp Koehn) — R 8 · Q 6.
  - Training pairs come from professional TV dubbing plus multilingual voice conversion, for En↔De and En↔Es.
  - Prosody, timing and speaker similarity improve for every system tested. But absolute ASR-BLEU is low, and evaluation is on mExpresso only.
- **★ LongCap** — `2611.11125` — CMU (Shinji Watanabe) and independent researchers — R 6 · Q 7.
  - Hour-scale timestamped audio captioning, scored with diarization-style metrics.
  - It extrapolates to 16 h of audio after training on recordings of 40 min or less.
- **LibriSpeech-Real-QA** — `2611.06496` — Tsinghua / CUHK / SenseTime — R 7 · Q 5.
  - A streaming spoken-QA benchmark, for example counting mentions of a name as the speech plays.
  - Commercial real-time models still wait for the end of the turn before answering.
