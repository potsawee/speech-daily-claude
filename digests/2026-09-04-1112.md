## ★ 1. VibeVoice-ASR-Streaming Technical Report — `2609.02812` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 9/10**

*Yujie Tu, Zhiliang Peng, Jianwei Yu, Li Dong, Songchen Xu, Yaoyao Chang, Wenhui Wang, Zilong Wang, Zehua Wang, Yan Xia, Jiajun Zhang, Xie Chen, Furu Wei* — **Microsoft Research** (the VibeVoice line; affiliations are not printed on the arXiv page, but the VibeVoice-ASR models ship under `microsoft/` on HuggingFace and Azure AI Foundry) · eess.AS · 2026-09-02 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02812)

*Problem:* Unified end-to-end speaker-attributed ASR models like VibeVoice-ASR replaced the ASR-plus-diarization cascade, but they still run offline, which rules them out for real-time voice assistants and agents.
*Method:* Interleave fixed-size audio chunks, a small lookahead window, and previously emitted text on a single causal sequence, so an LLM backbone produces "who said what" as speech arrives with no separate diarization stage.
*Result:* The 7B model reaches the lowest average WER/CER across five evaluation sets and best-or-tied speaker attribution on 12 of 13 settings; 1.5B and 7B weights plus inference code are released.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed authors (Furu Wei, Xie Chen) and a followed lab. Relevance 10 — the highest possible fit and the highest in the window: streaming ASR, LLM-based ASR, and speaker attribution in one system, which is Tier-1 keyword territory three times over. The interleaving scheme is the part worth studying rather than the WER: putting *previous text* back into the input alongside each new audio chunk is a concrete answer to how you keep an LLM's linguistic context alive under a streaming constraint, which is exactly the tension in every streaming Speech-LLM design. Quality 9 for the combination of margin, breadth and release — best-or-tied on 12 of 13 attribution settings is not a cherry-picked table, and shipping both 1.5B and 7B weights means the claim is checkable. Two things hold it off 10, and one is worth acting on: **the abstract promises released weights, but Microsoft's public VibeVoice-ASR docs currently describe only the offline 7B model (60-minute single-pass, 64K token budget) and say nothing about a streaming variant** — so verify the streaming checkpoints are actually live before planning around them. Second, this is a technical report with no venue and no ablations in the abstract: the chunk size, lookahead duration and the latency/accuracy curve they buy are the whole engineering contribution, and none of it is quantified where you can see it. Read the report for those numbers before believing the offline-parity implication.

## ★ 2. Compressing Streaming Neural Audio Encoders via Latent-Space Distillation — `2609.04102` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Prasanth Yadla, Mohammad Samragh, Dongseong Hwang, Mingbin Xu, Yuanyuan Zhang, Chung-Cheng Chiu, Yongqiang Wang, Yuan Liu, Zhen Huang, Xiaodan Zhuang* — **Apple** (on-device Dictation / foundation-model team; stated in the abstract) · cs.SD · 15 pages · 2026-09-03 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04102)

*Problem:* On Apple devices the always-on speech tokenizer sits in DRAM beside a sparsely-activated foundation model under Instruction-Following Pruning, so every parameter it holds is taken directly from power, latency and expert residency.
*Method:* Distil the student encoder against neither the discrete token nor the output distribution but the **pre-quantizer latent** — the last representation both token interfaces share — regressing the teacher's per-frame latent under squared error, with one affine layer absorbing the width mismatch.
*Result:* At 2.8× compression the student stays within 1.9% relative WER of its teacher on five of six teacher–student pairs with no fine-tuning, and beats an independently trained tokenizer of identical capacity by 3.9% relative.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — this is the encoder→LLM interface your Speech-LLM work sits on, described by people who ship it. The choice of distillation target is the transferable idea: picking the representation *upstream of every branch point* means one recipe covers both token interfaces and works whether the tokenizer was pretrained alone or jointly with the LM, which is a general principle for any modality-adapter stack with more than one consumer. It is also the cleaner counterpart to Wednesday's stride-k Whisper result — where that paper threw tokens away train-free and paid for it on accented speech, this one shrinks the encoder itself and keeps the frame rate, and the two are composable. Quality 8 for being a real deployment study with an honest denominator: six teacher–student pairs, two token interfaces, both pretraining regimes, and the result stated as "five of six" rather than averaged into a single flattering number. Held at 8 because the evidence is narrower than the framing suggests — 2.8× is a single compression point with no curve showing where the method breaks, "within 1.9% relative WER" is a same-domain claim with no accented, noisy or far-field breakout, and the one failing pair is the interesting case the abstract does not characterise. There is also no code and no venue, and the whole setup is specific enough to Apple's stack (IFP-pruned experts, their two token interfaces) that reproducing it outside will take work.

## ★ 3. Alignment-Free Text-Audiobox for Voice Dubbing and Full-Duplex Dialogue Synthesis — `2609.03992` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 9/10**

*Sanyuan Chen, Min-Jae Hwang, Sho Inoue, Anna Sun, Bokai Yu, David Kant, Dongmin Hyun, Dorian Desblancs, Gregory Antonovsky, Oleg Repin, Peng-Jen Chen, Xutai Ma, Zehai Tu, Juan Pino, Wei-Ning Hsu* — **Meta AI** (the Audiobox line) · cs.CL, eess.AS · 2026-09-03 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03992)

*Problem:* Audiobox-class speech generation still depends on forced alignment and explicit duration prediction, and on EnCodec-rate representations that make long-form and full-duplex synthesis expensive.
*Method:* A 3B latent-diffusion transformer with a flow-matching objective over DAC-VAE features that compress 48 kHz audio to a **25 Hz** latent (>10× tighter than EnCodec), consuming raw text through an off-the-shelf encoder and learning text–speech alignment purely by cross-attention; pretrained on 480k hours, then fine-tuned for dubbing and full-duplex dialogue.
*Result:* A step-change over Meta's latest internal dubbing system in prosody and voice similarity, naturalness and shareability; full-duplex dialogue approaches human recordings on short-form and natively models turn-taking, back-channelling and emotional dynamics.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Three followed authors (Sanyuan Chen, Wei-Ning Hsu, Juan Pino) and a followed lab. Relevance 7 — dubbing and TTS are Secondary for you and this would normally not make the list, but two of its three departures are squarely in your Core interests, and the *alignment-free* claim is the one to take seriously. Deleting the forced aligner and letting cross-attention discover text–speech correspondence is the generation-side statement of your modality-alignment question, and the fact that it works at 3B/480k hours is evidence about how much alignment supervision a large model actually needs — which bears directly on whether your recognition-side pipelines need explicit alignment either. The 25 Hz DAC-VAE latent is the other transferable piece: a >10× compression that *improves* resynthesis quality is a codec result, and a 25 Hz interface is a very different budget to hand a downstream LLM. Quality 9 — this is the most substantial system in the window by a wide margin: 3B parameters, 480k hours, three fine-tuned downstream tasks, a multi-diffusion scheme for arbitrarily long generation, and evaluation against a *deployed internal* system rather than an academic baseline, from the lab that built Audiobox and Voicebox. Not 10 because the evidence is the weakest part of an otherwise strong paper: every headline comparison is against "the latest internal dubbing system" and "the latest internal model", which are unnamed, unpublished and unreproducible, the gains are described qualitatively ("large gains", "step-change") with no numbers in the abstract, and the reranking strategy selects on automated metrics that are then implicitly part of the evaluation story. No code or weights are mentioned. Read it for the three design decisions, not the win.

## 4. AVERT: Audio-Verified Adjudication for Spoken Dialogue State Tracking — `2609.01828` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 6/10**

*Chunggi Lee, Hanspeter Pfister* — **Harvard University** (School of Engineering and Applied Sciences) · cs.CL · 2026-09-01 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01828)

*Problem:* In spoken dialogue state tracking, ASR errors concentrate in **entity values** and persist across turns; a text-only editor working from the transcript alone cannot recover a value contradicted across turns, an omitted slot, or a value the audio never supported.
*Method:* AVERT scores each candidate value by combining cross-turn agreement with a trained audio-conditioned verifier, then applies three operators — *vote*, *add*, *swap* — each restricted to the slots where its error type is actually common.
*Result:* On SpokenWOZ a base speech-LLM reaches 33.04 JGA, a text editor 38.34, and AVERT 40.13 with no retraining of either; the audio verifier's contribution is statistically significant, and removing the per-operator slot restriction drops AVERT below the editor.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — this is your entity-aware recognition problem stated precisely and attacked at the right layer. "ASR errors concentrate in entity values and persist across turns" is the one-line version of why rare-word and proper-noun recognition is the bottleneck in spoken understanding, and the fix — go **back to the audio** to adjudicate a value the transcript cannot settle — is the operational form of the argument that a strong text prior needs acoustic evidence to overrule it. The taxonomy generalises past dialogue state tracking: inconsistent-across-turns, omitted, and unsupported-by-audio are three error types any entity-heavy transcription task has, and the finding that *restricting each operator to the slots where its error is common* is load-bearing is a genuinely useful negative result — unrestricted voting overwrites correct categorical values and makes things worse. Quality 6, and the number is about scale rather than care. In its favour, the paper is unusually honest for its size: it reports that AVERT (40.13) only edges a 1B end-to-end system consuming the full spoken history (39.32) *and* volunteers that it spends two 1B decoders to that system's one, which is the comparison most authors would have omitted. Against: two authors, no venue, no code or data released, a single benchmark, and a +1.79 JGA headline gain over the text editor at roughly double the inference cost — which is a real result but a thin one. Take the error taxonomy and the operator-restriction finding; treat the JGA number as a demonstration, not a system to adopt.

## 5. Decoupling Turn-Taking from Semantics: A Decoupled Data Approach for Finite-State-Machine-Based Full-Duplex Dialogue — `2609.03321` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Yihang Li, Chenhui Chu* — **Kyoto University** · cs.CL · EMNLP 2026 Main Conference · 2026-09-03 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03321)

*Problem:* Neural-FSM full-duplex dialogue serialises turn-taking control and response generation onto one causal tape at low fine-tuning cost, but it is trained on LLM-synthesised text, and an LLM cannot fake the fine-grained acoustic timing of real human conversation.
*Method:* Learn turn-taking from **real human–human spoken** dialogue and semantics from configurable human–agent **text** dialogue, using a rule-based event-guided transform that serialises real speech into FSM tapes by deterministic event mapping (no LLM annotation), plus a Source-Aware Calibrated loss that both fixes the long-tailed state-transition token distribution and routes each data source to the capability it supervises.
*Result:* Substantially better turn-taking proficiency **while recovering the foundation LLM's semantic capability**; code and model released.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 7 — full-duplex dialogue is a Core interest, but the reason this is on the list rather than in the flagged pile is the second half of its result. "Recovering the foundation LLM's semantic capability" while adding a speech-timing skill is your knowledge-retention / catastrophic-forgetting problem in a different costume, and the proposed mechanism is a clean one worth stealing: rather than regularising or replaying, **route each capability to the data source that genuinely carries it** — timing from real audio, semantics from text — so the two never compete for the same gradient. That decomposition applies to any speech adaptation where one skill only exists in the acoustic data and the other only in text. Quality 7 — EMNLP 2026 Main, with code and model public, and the rule-based deterministic transform is the right call: it makes the timing supervision reproducible and removes the LLM-annotation loop that caused the problem in the first place. Held at 7 because the paper is small in the ways that matter for a claim like this. Two authors and no scale reported in the abstract; "substantially improves" and "recovering" are both unquantified, which is a real gap when the entire contribution is a trade-off between two competing capabilities and the interesting question is exactly how much of each you keep. It also inherits the NFSM framework's constraints wholesale, so this is an improvement to one full-duplex architecture rather than a result about full-duplex dialogue in general.
