**The theme is measurement of a gap between what a speech model knows and what it actually says — arriving three times, independently, in five days.** #3 (TTI-Chicago) measures it in *generation*: matched-data speech-only vs. speech-text LMs, and finds joint modelling buys semantic coherence while costing speaker similarity and predicted quality. #5 (Huawei) measures it in *output mode*: the same model answering in speech-and-text mode is 42.87 points less accurate than answering in text mode, on identical audio. #4 (Tencent) measures it in *grounding*: an ASR model rewarded only in text space stops listening and starts guessing from its language prior, and the errors are insertions. ## 1. ★ Enabling Streaming User Transcription in Full-Duplex Speech-to-Speech Models — `2609.15759` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Ke Hu, Nourchene Ferchichi, Edresson Casanova, Ankita Pasad, Elena Rastorgueva, Chen Chen, Nithin Rao Koluguri, Piotr Żelasko, Yifan Peng, Hainan Xu, Zhehuai Chen, Boris Ginsburg* — **NVIDIA** · cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-14 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15759)

*Problem:* Full-duplex speech-to-speech models listen and speak at once but never write down what the user said, so conversation logging, accessibility captions and quality monitoring all require bolting on a second recogniser.
*Method:* Add a lightweight streaming ASR head in parallel to the agent text head of SALM-Duplex — few extra parameters, no architectural surgery, turn-taking and barge-in preserved.
*Result:* 10.21% average streaming WER across the eight Open ASR Leaderboard sets inside the duplex model; 7.73% as a standalone streaming recogniser.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 because this sits on the seam between two Tier-1 areas — streaming ASR and speech LLMs — and answers a question the duplex literature has been quietly ducking: does making a model conversational cost you recognition? The answer here is a number you can check. Quality 8 for evaluating on the **Open ASR Leaderboard** rather than an in-house set, so the 10.21% is directly comparable to everything else on that board, and for reporting the standalone variant at **7.73%** — which is the honest control, since it isolates how much the duplex context costs the ASR head (roughly 2.5 WER points, and they let you see it). A follow-list author at a follow-list lab, on the NeMo team whose Canary and Parakeet models are already the reference open recognisers, with **code release committed**. Held to 8 because the contribution is architectural plumbing rather than a new idea — a parallel head is the obvious first thing to try — there is no venue acceptance yet, and the per-set spread is wide (3.9% LibriSpeech-clean against 18.36% AMI), so "10.21% average" flatters the meeting-speech case that duplex assistants will actually meet.

## 2. ★ Sequential Adapter Stacking for Cross-Lingual Low-Resource ASR — `2609.15758` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Thai Thi Thanh Thao Dang, Mengjie Qian, Kate Knill* — **Department of Engineering, University of Cambridge**, UK · cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-14 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15758)

*Problem:* Whisper's performance collapses on languages it never saw in pre-training, and the standard fix — full fine-tuning on scarce target data — overwrites the multilingual knowledge that made the model worth adapting.
*Method:* Stack a trainable target-language adapter on top of a frozen source-language adapter, so transfer flows through a fixed related-language representation; compared against warm initialisation and attention-based fusion.
*Result:* 5–8% relative WER cuts over full fine-tuning on Asturian, Assamese and Xhosa, largely surviving at one hour of target data.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — multilingual and low-resource ASR is Tier-1, and the experimental design targets the exact confound the area usually leaves in: each target gets a **close, medium and distant source** (Asturian ← Spanish/French/Mandarin, Assamese ← Bengali/Hindi/Mandarin, Xhosa ← Zulu/Swahili/Mandarin), so "relatedness helps" is measured rather than assumed, and all three targets are genuinely **unsupported by Whisper** rather than merely under-represented. Quality 7 for a controlled, honest study from a follow-list author at a follow-list lab: the headline numbers (Asturian 14.9 vs 15.7, Assamese 34.1 vs 36.3, Xhosa 38.5 vs 41.9) are reported against full fine-tuning on identical data, and the one-hour result is the one that matters operationally, since it says the method degrades gracefully where the data actually runs out. Held to 7 because the gains are small in absolute terms — a point or three of WER at 34–42% baseline error, where the systems remain unusable either way — adapter stacking is a modest recombination of established parameter-efficient pieces rather than a new mechanism, and there is no venue acceptance or artifact release stated. Read it for the relatedness ablation more than the method.

## 3. Quantifying the Generation Modality Gap in Speech-Text Language Models — `2609.14743` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Ju-Chieh Chou, Karen Livescu* — **Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago (TTIC)**; *Jiawei Zhou* — **Stony Brook University** · cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-13 · SLT 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14743)

*Problem:* Everyone agrees speech-only LMs generate less coherent content than text or speech-text LMs, but nobody can say by how much: the systems are trained on different data and scored with different metrics, so the modality gap has never been isolated.
*Method:* Train speech-only, text-only and speech-text LMs on matched data with a shared flow-matching acoustic generator, then score continuations on semantic coherence, phone n-gram structure, speaker consistency, quality and emotion distribution.
*Result:* Joint modelling substantially improves semantic coherence and closes much of the scaling gap, but lowers speaker similarity and predicted quality.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 and the cleanest fit in the window — speech–text modality alignment is your Primary Focus stated in the profile's own words, and this is the paper that finally supplies the controlled measurement the area has been arguing without. Quality 8 for the design: **matched training distributions and matched generation settings** are the entire contribution, and they are what every prior speech-vs-text comparison was missing, from a group (Livescu, TTIC) whose published record is exactly this kind of careful representation-level analysis. The finding is also the right kind of uncomfortable — the improvement is **not uniform**, phone-level statistics barely move, and speaker similarity and predicted quality *get worse* under joint modelling, which is precisely the trade-off a paper with a thesis to sell would have buried. Held to 8 because it is a diagnosis, not a fix: no method is proposed for recovering the acoustic quality that joint training costs, the scale is small by construction (matched-data training forces it), and the semantic-coherence metric routes through transcription and a reference LM, so it inherits both of their biases. Take the evaluation suite as the deliverable.

## 4. Grounded in Sound: Reinforcement Learning with a Frozen Acoustic Judge to Curb ASR Insertion Hallucinations — `2609.14455` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Tingzhen Xiong, Rilin Chen, Weiwei Li, Wentao Zhang, Qicong Xie* — **Tencent**, Shenzhen · cs.SD, cs.MM, eess.AS · submitted 2026-09-13 · IEEE SLT 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14455)

*Problem:* RL post-training for ASR rewards text-space agreement with the reference and never asks whether the hypothesis is supported by the audio, licensing a shortcut — guess from the language prior — that emits fluent ungrounded words once acoustics degrade.
*Method:* Augment GRPO with an acoustic-fidelity reward from a separately pretrained, permanently frozen 0.3B character-level wav2vec2-CTC judge, used only in training; a LoRA-adapted Qwen2-Audio-7B decodes greedily at inference.
*Result:* Insertions down 28.3% on AMI-IHM and 22.3% on AMI-SDM, with AMI-SDM WER 35.89% → 34.71%.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — "the model stopped listening and started predicting" is the failure mode underneath entity hallucination, rare-word substitution and over-correction, all Tier-1 on your profile, and this paper isolates it as a *reward-design* problem rather than a data problem. Quality 8 on methodology that is unusually disciplined for a preprint: **four prespecified analyses**, a meeting-level **clustered bootstrap** rather than naive utterance-level significance, a six-tier difficulty gradient across 33,282 utterance-condition instances, and the striking control that output **collapses 85–90% on unintelligible audio that still carries energy and voice activity** — which is the experiment that actually demonstrates grounding rather than asserting it. The authors also report that the gain is **not** recovered by 32-best CTC rescoring yet is internalised into one greedy pass, and state plainly that WER is unchanged on five of the six tiers. Held to 8 because the WER movement is real but tiny (1.18 points on the single hardest tier), it is one backbone and one judge — the paper says so, framing itself as "a mechanism paper, demonstrated in one instantiation" — and **no code release is stated**, which for an RL recipe is the difference between a result and a reproducible one.

## 5. Reducing the Output-Mode Gap in Speech Language Models via Joint-Output On-Policy Distillation — `2609.15313` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Daxin Tan, Dehua Tao, Chengxi Deng* — **Huawei AI Lab, Leibniz Research Center**; *Hanlin Zhang* — **The Chinese University of Hong Kong**; *Xiao Chen* — **City University of Hong Kong** · eess.AS, cs.CL, cs.SD · submitted 2026-09-14 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15313)

*Problem:* Speech LLMs that interleave text and acoustic tokens feed their own acoustic output back into the context for subsequent text prediction, so the same model on the same audio answers markedly worse in speech-and-text mode than in text-only mode.
*Method:* Distil the model's stronger S2T policy into joint generation on student-generated trajectories, with a text-only projection supplying soft targets and a preservation objective guarding native non-text predictions.
*Result:* Gap on Spoken-MQA 42.87 → 16.26 points, GSM8K 29.72 → 13.04, spoken-answer accuracy +7.49.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — the output-mode gap is the modality gap seen from the generation side, and it lands on the profile's knowledge-retention concern from an angle nobody was watching: the capability is not lost in *training*, it is lost at *decode time*, every turn, because the model's own speech tokens pollute its text context. Naming and measuring that is worth the read on its own. Quality 7 for demonstrating the phenomenon on **two independent interleaved architectures** (Step-Audio-2-mini and Baichuan-Audio-Instruct) before proposing anything, comparing against **matched SFT baselines** on the same trajectories rather than against the untrained base only, and running ablations that are genuinely load-bearing — removing the text-vocabulary restriction drops spoken-answer accuracy by 89%, which tells you the constraint is the method rather than a detail. Held to 7 by the honest asymmetry the paper does not foreground: on Baichuan-Audio the gains are **2.92 and 0.61 points**, against 26 and 17 on Step-Audio, so the headline is effectively one model and the generality claim rests on the phenomenon rather than the fix. No venue, no stated code or checkpoint release.
