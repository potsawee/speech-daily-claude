## 1. The Null Token Knows: Reducing Message-Free Hallucination in ASR and NMT — `2608.15940` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Kirill Borodin, Vasiliy Kudryavtsev, Ivan Viakhirev, Grach Mkrtchian* — affiliation not stated on the preprint · cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.SD · 2026-08-16 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15940)

*Problem:* Encoder-decoder ASR and NMT emit fluent text from inputs carrying no recoverable message — Whisper hallucinating over silence or noise being the canonical case.
*Method:* Treat the reserved null token (EOT/EOS) as an abstention signal: audit its native score across recognizers and translators, probe Whisper's decoder states, and compare scalar logit shifts and supervised row edits against conventional external gates — scoring suppression **and** deletion.
*Result:* Decoder states separate speech from non-speech near-perfectly (AUROC ≈1.0) after the first block across six Whisper scales, Canary-1B, OWSM v3.1, CTC/RNN-T, NLLB-200 and MarianMT; a trained row edit cuts fabrication 91.7% → 3.3% but deletes 33.05 reference words/min against stock's 1.58.

**Why it ranked:** Hallucination on message-free audio is the failure that quietly wrecks long-form and always-on ASR pipelines, and this is the rare paper that finds the signal is already inside the model rather than proposing another bolt-on detector — the practical takeaway (raise the null-token score, then measure what you deleted) is usable this afternoon on a Whisper deployment. Quality 8 for genuinely broad coverage — six Whisper scales plus Canary, OWSM, CTC/RNN-T and two NMT systems on a locked source-disjoint eval set — and for reporting the deletion cost instead of the flattering suppression number alone; held there because it ships no model or code, is an AAAI-27 submission rather than an accepted paper, and lists no affiliation.

## 2. FireRedTTS3: Unified Speech Generation and Editing with Semantically Enriched Speech Representations — `2608.17492` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 9/10**
*Feiyu Shen, Kun Xie, Yichen Wu, Ziqi Dai, Yichen Han, Junjie Li, Xuelong Geng, Fenglong Xie, Lei Xie, Xu Tang, Yao Hu* — **Xiaohongshu (RedNote), FireRed Team** · cs.SD · 2026-08-18 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17492)

*Problem:* Continuous autoregressive TTS keeps acoustic detail and inherits an LLM's instruction-following, but accumulates error over the generation; existing fixes bolt on semantic modules, multi-stage tokenizer pipelines, or more complex AR architectures.
*Method:* Fix it at the representation level instead — a frozen audio encoder trained on speech-*understanding* tasks acts as a semantic teacher regularizing the audio feature space, tightening text–speech alignment while the architecture stays simple. Two variants: Base for multilingual/multi-dialect zero-shot cloning, Instruct for voice design and speech editing.
*Result:* Seed-TTS-Eval 1.64% WER (EN) / 1.01% CER (ZH) / 6.50% hard-set CER at 78.8% speaker similarity; 3.75% average error across 24 languages on MiniMax-MLS-Test at 84.8% similarity; 85.8 / 80.7 APS on InstructTTSEval — ahead of CosyVoice3, Qwen3-TTS, MiniMax-Speech, Seed-TTS and MegaTTS3. Code and models released.

**Why it ranked:** The best-executed artifact in the window and the one most likely to get shared in a speech group chat — but it earns its place here on mechanism as much as on numbers, because "use a speech-understanding encoder as a semantic teacher to regularize the generative representation" is a speech–text alignment result wearing a TTS jacket, and that is the axis you track. Quality 9 for winning on four independent public benchmarks including a 24-language one, against the current frontier of open TTS rather than a convenient baseline, with weights and code actually on GitHub; short of 10 because the contribution is a well-chosen regularizer on an established recipe, not a new paradigm, and the editing evaluation leans on one benchmark.

## 3. Cached LLM Probability Retrieval for Speech Recognition — `2608.16023` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Sheng Li, Takahiro Shinozaki, Tatsuya Kawahara* — **Institute of Science Tokyo · Kyoto University** · eess.AS · 2026-08-17 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16023)

*Problem:* An LLM gives ASR a strong linguistic prior, but direct rescoring costs a forward pass per N-best hypothesis, and the cheaper routes — generative error correction, distillation — both demand training.
*Method:* Query a local teacher LLM **offline** for next-token probabilities over ASR-relevant context–target pairs, then serve them during recognition through cache lookups with backoff and optional scoring for significant misses. Training-free, and the acoustic model is untouched.
*Result:* Improves on 1-pass ASR in 28 of 39 settings spanning Whisper base/small/large-v3, wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT-large and SpeechBrain CRDNN, on LibriSpeech test-other, AMI IHM and six FLEURS languages; 8.13% absolute average WER/CER reduction for Whisper-small, 13.01% on AMI, 9.22% on Mandarin FLEURS, against LoRA-GER baselines on Qwen2.5-0.5B and Qwen3-8B. Benefit peaks at context length 8.

**Why it ranked:** Dead-centre on your primary axis — injecting LLM knowledge into recognition — and it is the cheap version of the idea: no fine-tuning, no acoustic-model surgery, a lookup table you can drop in front of an existing recognizer, with multilingual FLEURS results rather than a LibriSpeech-only story. Kawahara and Shinozaki are a credible pedigree for this kind of careful systems work. Quality 7 rather than higher because 28-of-39 concedes it loses in eleven settings, the headline 8.13% is a *best-cache* average that reads oracle-flavoured until the selection protocol is spelled out, and the paper is still under review with no code.

## ★ 4. INSPIRE: A Benchmark for Instruction-Aware Speech Retrieval — `2608.16203` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Chen-An Li, Hung-yi Lee* — **National Taiwan University · NTU AI-CoRE** · cs.SD, cs.CL, eess.AS · 2026-08-17 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16203)

*Problem:* Speech retrieval matches on one fixed similarity, so a user cannot state what "relevant" means for their query — the words spoken, who spoke them, how, what was in the background, or a combination of those.
*Method:* A benchmark of ~17,225 documents and 680 spoken queries expanded into 4,080 query–instruction pairs, where natural-language instructions define the relevance criterion; four paradigms are evaluated — six LALMs (3–30B), ASR-plus-text cascades, self-supervised encoders (HuBERT, WavLM), and contrastive audio-language models — plus reranking.
*Result:* No paradigm covers the space. Cascades lead on semantics (62% Recall@10 on DailyTalk) but lose paralinguistics; SSL encoders lead on speaker matching (17.5% Recall@10) but cannot follow instructions; multi-attribute queries degrade every method.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed author (Hung-yi Lee). It defines a task rather than nudging a number on an existing one, and the failure it isolates is exactly your modality-gap question in retrieval clothing — text-side systems have the instruction-following and lose the acoustics, speech-side systems have the acoustics and lose the instruction-following, and nothing yet has both. Quality 8 for comparing four genuinely distinct paradigms across a benchmark of real scale, with an honest negative headline instead of a proposed model that conveniently wins, and Interspeech 2026 long-paper acceptance behind it; short of 9 because absolute recall is low enough across the board that the benchmark's discriminative power at the top end is still unproven.

## 5. Speaker-Normalized Semantic Speech Tokens via Iterative S2U-T2U Refinement — `2608.16235` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Hanlin Zhang, Daxin Tan, Dehua Tao, Chengxi Deng, Xiao Chen, Linqi Song* — **City University of Hong Kong · Huawei AI Lab · Chinese University of Hong Kong** · eess.AS · 2026-08-17 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16235)

*Problem:* Semantic speech tokens are meant to carry linguistic content only, but tokenizers trained on audio leak speaker identity and duration into the token space — noise that every downstream speech-LM then has to model.
*Method:* Iterative Semantic Token Purification alternates speech-to-unit and text-to-unit training. Each round trains a T2U model on the current tokenizer's deduplicated sequences, then uses its decoded predictions as CTC targets for a freshly initialised S2U model, progressively biasing the token space toward what text can predict.
*Result:* On ~8k h of English and Mandarin, S2U–T2U agreement improves 72.0–86.7% relative in WER with BLEU up 58.9–73.4 points, sequence consistency UED falls 344.61 → 59.44, probe-recoverable speaker accuracy drops, and independently trained de-tokenizers confirm the tokens still support intelligible voice conversion and TTS.

**Why it ranked:** Speech tokenizers are the substrate everything else in your stack sits on, and "make the token space match what text can predict" is a clean, testable definition of semantic purity — one that attacks the modality gap at the representation rather than patching it downstream. Quality 7 for training at real scale (8k h, Emilia de-tokenizers) and for verifying the claim two ways — probe-recoverable speaker information *and* reconstruction quality — rather than asserting disentanglement; capped there because the headline gains are S2U–T2U self-agreement, a metric the training loop directly optimises, with no downstream ASR or speech-LM result yet and no code announced.
