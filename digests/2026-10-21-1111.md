### 1. ★ ConvexLLM: Scaling Convex-Hull Speech Interfaces to General-Purpose Audio Intelligence — `2610.23539` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 8/10**

Ming-Hao Hsu, Shujie Liu, Yuxuan Hu, Yuxiang Wang, Yingda Shen, Jinyu Li, Zhizheng Wu — **CUHK-Shenzhen, Microsoft Research, Microsoft Research Asia** · cs.CL, cs.SD, eess.AS · 2026-10-16 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23539)

*Problem:* MLP and Q-Former connectors give the LLM unfamiliar acoustic vectors to decode, and its text skills erode.
*Method:* C-Gate writes each audio position as a top-16 convex mixture of the decoder's own frozen text embeddings, keeping speech inside the text-embedding hull; under 0.3M hours of adaptation.
*Result:* VoiceBench 75.36, the best of the open-weight models compared. In a matched 2B test it beats MLP and Q-Former (60.35 vs 58.80 and 54.19) and keeps more text BBH (68.2 vs 63.6 and 56.2; base 76.6).

**Why it ranked:** Three followed authors (Jinyu Li, Shujie Liu, Zhizheng Wu) tackle your core question of attaching speech without costing the backbone its knowledge, and the matched-interface comparison makes the claim checkable. It isn't a 9 because it scales the authors' own earlier C-Gate, runs one seed per interface, and MLP still wins on WER (2.10% vs 2.46% on test-clean).

### 2. ★ Uncovering Audio-Text Alignment in Large Audio-Language Models through Cross-Modal Concept Neurons — `2610.28189` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

Tzu-Quan Lin, Chih-Kai Yang, Yi-Cheng Lin, Hao Tang, Hung-yi Lee — **University of Edinburgh (CSTR), National Taiwan University** · cs.SD, cs.CL, eess.AS · 2026-10-20 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28189)

*Problem:* Audio LLMs built on text-only backbones recognise emotion, gender and language from audio, but it is unclear where, or whether, that audio evidence joins the text representation.
*Method:* It finds FFN "concept neurons" that fire for an attribute both when it is heard and when it is written. It then tests them by steering and deactivation in Qwen2.5-Omni, Audio Flamingo 3 and DeSTA2.5-Audio.
*Result:* Text-derived steering beats random neurons on 35 of 48 labels in the two backbone-tuned models, but only 7 of 24 in frozen-backbone DeSTA2.5. Deactivating 0.2–0.7% of neurons breaks the target task first.

**Why it ranked:** A followed author (Hung-yi Lee) and lab (Edinburgh) give causal evidence for a shared speech–text subspace, and the frozen-vs-tuned-backbone contrast bears directly on knowledge retention. It is held at 7 because the authors note DeSTA2.5 also receives an ASR transcript, which confounds that contrast, and because it is a diagnosis on three 7–8B models with no fix proposed.

### 3. ★ Encoder-Free Multimodal LLMs Learn Implicit Encoders — `2610.24239` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 8/10**

David Harwath, Desh Raj, Xavier Velez, Sandra Arcos-Holzinger, Willie Jeng, Karen Livescu, Leibny Paola Garcia-Perera, Sanjeev Khudanpur — **UT Austin, NVIDIA, Johns Hopkins University, University of Melbourne, TTI-Chicago** · cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG, eess.AS · 2026-10-16 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24239)

*Problem:* Gemma-4 12B and Inkling-Small drop modality encoders and feed raw samples or dMel frames straight into the LLM, so it is unclear what their early layers actually do.
*Method:* Layer-wise CKA under swapped cross-modal context, joint vs. independent encoding on OmniBench, and SUPERB/PWCCA probing against HuBERT, WavLM and wav2vec 2.0.
*Result:* The first ~12 layers act as context-invariant "implicit encoders": encoding each modality separately costs no accuracy. Their frame-level features still trail SSL encoders on phone recognition and ASR.

**Why it ranked:** A followed author (David Harwath) and labs (JHU, NVIDIA) show where speech meets text inside the new encoder-free models. The ASR-relevant twist is that fine-grained phonetic detail is exactly what these models lose. Three complementary tests make the analysis solid, but OmniBench accuracies are modest (40–44% against 25% chance) and nothing is proposed to close the ASR gap.

### 4. CAFE: Confining Feature Distillation to Language-Routed Adapters for Compact Multilingual Automatic Speech Recognition — `2610.19134` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

Tianchi Liu, Nicholas Moratelli, Khurram Azeem Hashmi, Mohammadreza Zolfaghari, Rishabh Jain, Louis Lu, Changdae Park, Amril Nazir — **Tether AI Research** · cs.CL, cs.AI · 2026-10-13 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19134)

*Problem:* When a 254M multilingual student is distilled from Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3, adding a CKA feature loss helps non-English languages but hurts English.
*Method:* A gradient analysis shows 97.5–99.5% of the CKA gradient falls in the encoder subspace English uses (~81% expected by chance). CAFE therefore routes that gradient only into language adapters that English bypasses.
*Result:* Average WER on the Open ASR Leaderboard falls from 8.55% to 8.02%, beating an adapter-only capacity control (8.51%). English WER is steadier across checkpoints, and MLS-8 WER (6.58%) beats Whisper-medium (7.76%).

**Why it ranked:** It sits squarely on compact multilingual ASR and diagnoses why a standard trick fails before fixing it. The evidence includes a capacity control, significance tests, 48 test sets in 24 languages, 564k training hours and a released checkpoint (`qvac/TranscribePsy-250M-EU`). The absolute gain is modest and comes from a single teacher–student pair.

### 5. PronCFT: Pronunciation Contextual Fine-Tuning for Contextual Biasing of Out-of-Vocabulary Words in LLM-Based ASR — `2610.25766` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 7/10**

Yifan Xu, Kaixun Huang, Mengzhe Geng, Mingyu Cui, Shen Huang, Shidong Shang, Xunying Liu — **The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Tenlai Team (Beijing)** · eess.AS · 2026-10-19 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25766)

*Problem:* LLM-based ASR swaps rare and out-of-vocabulary names for familiar ones ("cloud" for "claude"). It treats phoneme hints in the prompt as markers to boost a candidate, without checking them against the audio.
*Method:* It fine-tunes Qwen3-ASR-1.7B to transcribe phonemes, match them to the context candidates and output the matched spelling. Candidates are respelled during training to block spelling shortcuts, and a curriculum then removes the intermediate steps.
*Result:* With structured output, OOV recall rises from 59.89% to 83.57%. On plain transcripts it reaches 70.64%, against 63.56% for a reimplemented PAC. General WER stays flat (6.05% → 6.01%).

**Why it ranked:** It is this window's most direct hit on entity and rare-word recognition in LLM-based ASR. Wrong-phoneme and misspelling controls show the model really reads the phonemes, recall gains pass McNemar tests, and code and test sets are released. It is held at 7 because the 23.7-point headline is measured against a no-phoneme baseline: like-for-like, it beats PAC by about 7 points, and the OOV set is English-only.
