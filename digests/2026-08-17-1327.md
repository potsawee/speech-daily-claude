## ★ 1. VoiceChat-TTS: A Low-Latency Continuous Speech Synthesis Model for Interactive Agents — `2608.13831` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 9/10**
*Edresson Casanova, Jaehyeon Kim, Mariana Graterol Fuenmayor, Shehzeen Hussain, Viacheslav Klimkov, Valentin Mendelev, Mikyas Desta, Paarth Neekhara, Piotr Zelasko, Chen Chen, Elena Rastorgueva, Ke Hu, Ankita Pasad, Xuesong Yang, Aya Alja'fari, Rajarshi Roy, Rohan Badlani, Jason Roche, Jason Li, Zhehuai Chen* — **NVIDIA** · eess.AS, cs.CL · 2026-08-13 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13831)

*Problem:* Duplex speech-to-speech models cut latency by collapsing the pipeline, but then force ASR, interruption handling and synthesis to be optimised jointly, and speech quality pays for it. Turn-based TTS keeps the quality and loses the interactivity.
*Method:* A streamable TTS driven directly by an LLM's text-token stream, with explicit control tokens for interruption and learned silence when no text is pending — a 778M streaming module over a 199M 31-codebook RVQ codec at 12.5 Hz, supporting mid-utterance barge-in without resetting the KV cache.
*Result:* 9.62 ms next-frame latency at concurrency 1 (15.24 ms at 4), 2.1× faster than Qwen3-TTS-12Hz; 2.00% WER and 0.757 SECS on unseen speakers at Squim-MOS 4.380; 96.8% interruption-obey rate at 320 ms, 100% with forced silence. Code in NVIDIA NeMo, checkpoint on HuggingFace.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed lab (NVIDIA), and the most directly usable paper in the window for anyone building a voice agent — it keeps modular TTS quality while getting duplex behaviour, and 9.62 ms next-frame latency with a 96.8% barge-in obey rate are deployable numbers rather than demo numbers. Quality 9 for a twenty-author system paper that measures latency, fidelity, interruption *and* four-turn stability instead of picking the flattering axis, with code and weights actually released; held off 10 because interruption is scored on NVIDIA's own protocol against a single external streaming baseline.

## 2. Confucius4-TTS: Transcript-Free Cross-Lingual Zero-Shot TTS with a Learnable Speaker Encoder — `2608.11650` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Huaxuan Wang, Huimin Wang, Ruiyu Zhang, Yingjie Li, Yitao Duan* — **NetEase Youdao, Beijing** · cs.SD, cs.CL · 2026-08-12 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11650)

*Problem:* Most zero-shot TTS still needs a transcript of the audio prompt at inference time. That quietly rules out cross-lingual cloning, because in-the-wild reference audio is almost never transcribed.
*Method:* Two stages — text-to-semantic then semantic-to-acoustic. The LLM-based T2S stage (24 layers, 1280-dim) uses a learnable speaker encoder over self-supervised representations to lift timbre straight from untranscribed audio; a conditional flow-matching S2A turns the predicted semantic tokens into mel. Trained on ~500k hours across 14 languages.
*Result:* 3.73% average WER over six CV3-Eval cross-lingual directions, lowest on four; Seed-TTS-Eval 1.49 WER / 0.700 SIM English and 0.94 CER / 0.765 SIM Chinese; above 72% speaker similarity across 11 languages, best WER on German and Thai. Code, checkpoints and demos released.

**Why it ranked:** Sits on the TTS, zero-shot-cloning and multilingual axes at once, and the differentiator is what ships — full weights and code for a 14-language system, where Friday's Luna-TTS, which beats it on English speaker similarity (0.768 vs 0.700), released nothing. Dropping the prompt-transcript requirement is the capability that actually unblocks in-the-wild cross-lingual cloning, not a benchmark decimal. Quality 8 because it is evaluated across three public benchmarks plus human ranking rather than one cherry-picked pair; docked because the headline cross-lingual human eval runs on an internal set and the intra-lingual numbers trail the frontier.

## ★ 3. VoiceDesigner: Text-to-Voice Generation and Editing via Unified Diffusion Modeling and Data Augmentation — `2608.13613` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Jiarui Hai, Karan Thakkar, Mounya Elhilali* — **Johns Hopkins University**; *Ke Chen, Yunyun Wang, Jiaqi Su, Rithesh Kumar, Zeyu Jin* — **Adobe Research** · eess.AS, cs.LG · 2026-08-12 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13613)

*Problem:* Text-to-voice systems synthesise a speaker from a written description, but cover a narrow slice of real and fictional voices and offer no principled way to then edit one — clone it, or change its emotion and tone.
*Method:* A single diffusion transformer handling generation, cloning and instruction-guided editing, fed by a hybrid data pipeline that combines DSP transformations with speech generation models to manufacture a voice corpus spanning human and character timbres.
*Result:* 0.66 style accuracy at 1.22% WER on generation, best on both; 1.70% WER and 0.757 speaker similarity on cloning; MOS-Editing 4.129 with 0.884 timbre similarity preserved through the edit. Demo page released.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed lab (Johns Hopkins, Elhilali) with Adobe Research, and it closes the loop the text-to-voice line has been missing — designing a voice from a description is only useful if you can keep editing it afterwards, and this is the first system to put generation, cloning and instruction editing on one backbone with numbers for all three. Quality 8: the synthetic data pipeline is described honestly as synthetic, and the editing results are the strongest part. Held there because the evidence leans on subjective MOS, nothing beyond a demo page is promised, and "competitive perceptual quality" concedes it does not win on raw fidelity.

## ★ 4. Whisper-Aware LLM: Self-Supervised Uncertainty Learning for Robust Whispered Speech Recognition — `2608.10836` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Gaopeng Xu, Zhenyu Wang, Zheng Xue, Yinfeng Xia, Haitao Yao* — **Qwen Business Unit, Alibaba** · cs.SD, cs.AI · 2026-08-11 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10836)

(*"Whisper" here means whispered speech, not the OpenAI model.*)

*Problem:* Whispered speech carries no voicing, so ASR fails in two opposite directions — missing the speech entirely, or hallucinating transcripts out of noise — and tuning against one failure mode reliably worsens the other.
*Method:* Targeted self-supervised tasks teach an audio-LLM to quantify the physical deficiency of its own acoustic input; that learned uncertainty is then spent at decoding time through Confidence-Fused Decoding, which feeds the decoder both high-level instructions and frame-level attention modulation.
*Result:* 1.31% CER on AISHELL6-Whisper, a 17% relative cut over Seed-ASR's 1.58%, while hallucination on the noise set falls from a 25.2–35.5% baseline range to 4.5%. General ASR is untouched: 1.34% CER on AISHELL-1, 1.91% WER on LibriSpeech-clean.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed lab (Alibaba Qwen). The transferable idea is not whispered speech at all — it is that an audio-LLM can be trained to represent how degraded its own input is, and that this one signal moves accuracy and hallucination in the *same* direction instead of trading them off. That is the reliability lever audio-LLM work keeps reaching for. Quality 8 for seven baselines including Seed-ASR and Qwen3-ASR, and for reporting clean-speech numbers to show the specialisation costs nothing; held there because no code release is stated and the hallucination set is the authors' own.

## 5. Trajectory Dynamics in Self-Supervised Learning Latent Space for Audio Deepfake Detection — `2608.13817` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Tomás Andrade Weber* — **Barcelona Supercomputing Center (CASE Department)** · eess.AS, cs.LG, cs.SD · 2026-08-13 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13817)

*Problem:* Anti-spoofing systems pool SSL features into utterance-level statistics, discarding the temporal structure that human articulation is physically constrained to produce — and that synthetic speech has no particular reason to respect.
*Method:* A causal LSTM next-frame predictor trained on bonafide speech only, over a deepfake-specialised Wav2Vec2 backbone (Stage 1), compared against a global-average-pooling baseline on identical features so that temporal modelling is the only variable; an optional MLP on frozen LSTM states adds spoof supervision (Stage 2).
*Result:* 0.75% EER on ASVspoof 2021, claimed best published, plus 1.11% on ASVspoof 2019, 2.43% Codecfake, 3.28% In-the-Wild. On the hardest cross-corpus sets the bonafide-only Stage 1 beats its own supervised Stage 2 — 30.35% vs 35.41% on Deepfake-Eval-2024, against NII-GAP's 33.36%.

**Why it ranked:** Anti-spoofing is explicitly on the profile, and this is the rare one-class result that is interesting rather than merely competitive: training on real speech alone generalises *better* to unseen synthesisers than training with spoof labels — exactly the direction the field needs as generators outrun labelled attack sets. Quality 8 despite a solo author with no lab track record, because the matched static-versus-dynamic ablation isolates the claim properly, six benchmarks including two cross-corpus ones are reported with seed variance, and code is on Zenodo; held off higher because the SOTA claim rests on a table of published numbers rather than rerun baselines.

### Honorable mentions

- ★ **Ambisonics Encoding of Room Impulse Responses using a Device-Agnostic Diffusion Model** — `2608.14097` — **Meta Reality Labs Research** (Eloi Moliner, also Aalto University Acoustics Lab; Christoph Hold, Juan Azcarreta Ortiz, Sebastian Prepeliţă, Ishwarya Ananthabhotla, Daniel Wong, Sanjeel Parekh, Sanha Lee). IWAENC 2026. Encoding RIRs into high-order Ambisonics from sparse or irregular arrays is ill-posed, so linear methods simply cannot recover high-order spatial detail; a diffusion prior over HOA RIRs plus posterior sampling reconstructs it while staying consistent with the measurements. EDC 0.018 vs 0.049 for the linear baseline on FDTD and 0.019 vs 0.106 on Treble-10, accurate to 12th order, with a 13-listener binaural test confirming it and baselines degrading under rotation where it does not. The highest-quality paper in the window on execution — held out of the top five purely on relevance, since spatial audio and room acoustics sit off the TTS/ASR/SLM profile. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14097)
- **MuseCritic: Learning Multi-Aspect Song Rewards through Natural-Language Aesthetic Critiques** — `2608.11755` — **Fudan NLP Group** (Jiabao Zhuang, Changhao Jiang, Hanchen Wang, Tao Ji, Tao Gui, Qi Zhang, Xuanjing Huang and colleagues). A semi-scalar reward model that writes a five-dimension natural-language critique first and predicts the score *from* it, with the second training stage learning from its own critiques to kill the train/inference distribution shift. Macro MSE 0.2875 → 0.2316 on SongEval, 71.35% on the out-of-domain Music Arena preference pairs, and — the part that matters — using it as a GRPO reward improves Muse-0.6B on all nine SongEval and Audiobox Aesthetics metrics, so the reward survives being optimised against. Code released. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11755)
- **X2-Turn: Frame-Synchronous Dual-Head Modeling for Joint Streaming ASR and Turn State Prediction** — `2608.10878` — Kaiqi Fu, Rime Wen, Altman Lin, Shawn Qin, Roy Gan, Hao Wang, Qian Wang (no affiliation block on the preprint). Interspeech 2026. The natural companion to VoiceChat-TTS above: a turn-state head running frame-synchronously alongside the ASR head on shared streaming representations of Voxtral Realtime, so interruption, backchannel and completion are distinguished continuously rather than at chunk boundaries, with no separate ASR model at inference. 91.0%/93.0%/96.0% completion, incomplete and backchannel accuracy in Chinese at 288 ms; 92.1%/84.6% in English at 225 ms. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10878)
- **AT-ADD: All-Type Audio Deepfake Detection Challenge Summary** — `2608.14249` — Communication University of China, **Ant Group**, Institute of Automation CAS (Ruibo Fu), Beijing Institute of Technology and Shanghai Jiao Tong University (Guangtao Zhai). ACM MM 2026. Two tracks — robust speech deepfake detection under realistic channel variation, and type-agnostic detection spanning speech, environmental sound, singing voice and music. Best systems reached 90.71% and 96.10% Macro-F1, and the convergent recipe across entrants was large-scale SSL representations plus augmentation, multi-crop inference and structured fusion or routing. Read alongside the Trajectory Dynamics entry: generalisation to unseen generators is still the unsolved axis. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14249)
- **Omni-LiveAvatar: Minute-Level Real-Time Streaming Joint Audio-Visual Avatar Generation** — `2608.13602` — Lunjie Zhu, Xingtong Ge, Fangyu Lin, Yi Zhang, Zhening Liu, Mengfei Li, Yumeng Zhang, Guanglu Song, Yu Liu, Jun Zhang (affiliations not itemised on the preprint). Submitted 08-07 but only cross-listed into cs.SD in today's batch, so it is new to this feed. Progressive autoregressive distillation turns a bidirectional joint audio-video diffusion model into a few-step streaming generator with no auxiliary stabilisation, plus a synchronised audio-video long/short-term memory under a bounded budget. 33× faster than its LTX-2 teacher at 19.57 FPS on one H200, and holds identity 98.61 vs 67.60 for the next baseline over minute-long sequences where competitors collapse. Code released. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13602)
- **Evaluating Pre-trained Speech Encoders for Spontaneous Speech Detection and Out-of-Domain Synthetic Speech Generalisation in Indic Languages** — `2608.12536` — Varun Rai, Pavan Kumar J, Sujith Pulikodan, Nihar Desai. Five frozen encoders (AST, Vaani-FastConformer, Wav2vec2, Whisper, BEATs) across 22 Indic languages, but the finding worth carrying is geometric rather than multilingual: out-of-domain deepfake generalisation is predicted by how close a training system sits to *unseen TTS* embeddings, not by its distance from natural speech. That inverts the usual intuition about picking spoof training data. Also surfaces an encoder-dependent trade-off between language-discriminability and spontaneity detection. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12536)
- **Singer-Informed Vocal Source Separation for Multi-Singer Music Mixtures** — `2608.14516` — Jocelyn Xu, Minje Kim (affiliations not stated on the preprint). IWAENC 2026. Music separation extracts one vocal track and cannot tell two singers apart; a short enrollment clip of the target singer, injected as a learned embedding via concatenation or FiLM, turns it into target-speaker extraction for song. Target-singer SI-SDR 0.33 → 5.58 dB on duets built from DAMP-VSEP with non-overlapping enrollment, residual 1.47 → 7.98 dB, vocal FAD 2.28 → 0.37. Honest about the cost: on genuine solos the unconditioned baseline still wins at 17.80 dB. Code and checkpoints released. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14516)
- **CASA: Content-Acoustic Speaking Assessment with Speech Encoder and Large Language Model** — `2608.13101` — Aalto University (Nhan Phan, Ilona Lähteenmäki, Anna von Zansen, Olli-Pekka Pauna, Yaroslav Getman, Tamás Grósz, Mikko Kurimo). Whisper-medium plus Qwen3.5-2B and three handcrafted fluency features, reaching RMSE 0.358 on the Speak & Improve Corpus 2025 — a new best at roughly half the inference parameters of the previous one — while keeping delivery and content separable enough to ablate. The value here is the analysis rather than the number: individual versus complementary contributions of acoustic and content channels, and run-to-run variability actually reported. Code released. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13101)
- **FastThaiG2P: Lightning-fast Thai Grapheme-to-phoneme Conversion for Voice Agent Pipelines** — `2608.12814` — Charin Polpanumas, solo. No research novelty — a PyThaiNLP-tokenised dictionary with normalisation rules — and it is listed here purely as tooling: 0.15 ms per utterance at a 0.5% OOV rate, used end-to-end to phonemise the 20-hour Som-TTS set and train an 82M StyleTTS 2 model that runs at 0.25 RTF on CPU via ONNX. If Thai is on your roadmap, this is a working front end you do not have to build. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12814)
