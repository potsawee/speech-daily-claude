## 1. ★ Pruned CTC for Memory-Efficient Large-Vocabulary ASR Training — `2609.33645` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 9/10**

*Yifan Yang, Xiaoyu Yang, Zengrui Jin, Xian Shi, Yuxuan Wang, Yu Xi, Ziyang Ma, Qi Chen, Ruiyang Xu, Hui Wang, Dongchao Yang, Jin Xu, Xie Chen* — **Shanghai Jiao Tong University (X-LANCE)**, **University of Cambridge**, **Tsinghua University**, **Alibaba Token Hub**, Nankai University, CUHK, SII · eess.AS · v1 submitted 2026-09-27 (v2 09-29) · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33645)

*Problem:* Conventional CTC materialises a frame-by-vocabulary activation tensor, making training with a native LLM vocabulary prohibitively memory-intensive.
*Method:* Every valid CTC alignment uses only target tokens plus blank, so restrict alignment computation to the batch's union of those while retaining full-vocabulary normalisation; add finite-beam pruning, then build LLM-CTC for non-autoregressive ASR on causal LLMs.
*Result:* 5.1× lower full-step memory at 180K vocabulary for 17% step-time overhead, matched accuracy, and LLM-CTC within 7% relative WER of LLM-CE at 7–10× faster recognition.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 10 — this is three of your Primary Focus buckets in one paper (end-to-end/CTC ASR, LLM-based ASR at native vocabularies, and streaming), and the alignment angle is explicit: extending LLM-CTC to bounded-history streaming **"avoid[s] chunk-level speech–text alignments"**, which is the exact failure mode that makes streaming speech-LLMs awkward. Quality 9 — it is the rare preprint that earns its claim twice: they **prove** the vocabulary reduction is *exactly* equivalent to full-vocabulary CTC in both loss and gradients (so the memory saving is free, not a trade-off), and then back it with breadth most efficiency papers never attempt — three corpora (LibriSpeech, GigaSpeech, AISHELL-1) for the objective itself, **six Qwen3 sizes from 0.6B to 32B** for LLM-CTC on GigaSpeech, and a bounded-history streaming fine-tune of Qwen3-ASR landing within **3% relative WER of matched offline models**. Source code and pretrained checkpoints are promised at `github.com/yfyeung/PrunedCTC`. Held at 9 rather than 10 only because the headline LLM-CTC result is still a 7%-relative WER regression against autoregressive LLM-CE — you buy 7–10× decoding speed with a small accuracy debt — and because "matches standard CTC accuracy" is parity rather than a new frontier.

## 2. ★ DuraS2ST: Chain-of-Thought and Reinforcement Learning for Duration-Aligned Speech-to-Speech Translation — `2609.33742` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Yayue Deng, Dingdong Wang, Yuxuan Hu, Jinyu Li, Yanqing Liu, Yuanyuan Wang, Weidong Chen, Helen M. Meng, Shujie Liu, Xixin Wu* — **Microsoft** and **The Chinese University of Hong Kong** · cs.SD, cs.CL, eess.AS · v1 submitted 2026-09-27 · EMNLP 2026 Main · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33742)

*Problem:* Dubbing-style S2ST needs strict duration consistency, but existing systems generate target speech with no explicit temporal planning, so duration control is unsolved.
*Method:* One speech LM first emits a chain-of-thought planning target wording and phonetic length, then synthesises speech tokens; supervised on a new 440K duration-aligned CoT corpus, then tuned with multi-modal RL using a Duration Margin Reward and Modality-Aware Reward Attribution.
*Result:* On CVSS-T it beats competitive open-source and commercial baselines on the quality/duration trade-off.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — speech translation is a named Primary Focus, and this is the interesting version of it: making duration an explicitly *reasoned-about* variable inside a single speech LM rather than a post-hoc alignment fix, which is the same "plan in text, then realise in speech tokens" pattern your alignment work keeps running into. Quality 8 — accepted to **EMNLP 2026 Main** (so it has cleared real review, unlike most of this window), from Microsoft's speech group with Helen Meng's CUHK lab, and the contribution is a package rather than a single trick: a 440K-example corpus, a reward that trades translation quality against duration explicitly, and per-token-span reward attribution to stop the RL signal smearing across modalities, with a project page and code at `github.com/Mia11939/DuraS2ST`. Not higher because the abstract reports its headline as a qualitative "strong balance" and "outperforming" rather than numbers, evaluation is confined to CVSS-T, and the commercial baselines are unnamed — you have to open the paper to learn what was actually beaten and by how much.

## 3. ★ In-Context Adaptation of Encoder-Decoder Models in Speech Recognition — `2609.33865` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Yen Meng, Sharon Goldwater, Hao Tang* — **Centre for Speech Technology Research, University of Edinburgh** · cs.CL, cs.SD, eess.AS · v1 submitted 2026-09-27 · IEEE SLT 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33865)

*Problem:* Recent work reads in-context ASR adaptation as a capability of LLM-based speech models specifically; whether it is inherent to encoder-decoder models generally is untested.
*Method:* Feed speech-text demonstrations at inference in two layouts — collated and interleaved — across six encoder-decoder models spanning conventional cross-attention and LLM-based architectures, with controlled experiments on three English datasets isolating lexical versus speaker contributions.
*Result:* All six adapt out of the box, up to 30% relative improvement with oracle demonstrations and 23% using first-pass hypotheses; collated demonstrations help most consistently.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — test-time adaptation to new speakers, accents and domains is Primary Focus, and the finding is directly load-bearing for how you'd build one: if in-context adaptation is architecture-agnostic, then a plain Whisper-class encoder-decoder gets it too and you do not need an LLM decoder to buy it. The collated-vs-interleaved result is the immediately actionable part — interleaving is the layout the LLM-speech literature defaults to, and here the simpler collated format is the one that works across the board. Quality 8 — accepted to **SLT 2026**, from Edinburgh CSTR (Goldwater and Tang), and it is careful science of the kind that is scarce in a window of preprints: a claim about generality tested across six models rather than demonstrated on one, with ablations separating *why* it works (lexical and speaker information both contribute) from *that* it works. Not higher because it contributes understanding rather than a method, three English-only datasets is a narrow base for an "inherent ability" claim, and the 30% headline is an oracle number — the honest figure is the 23% from first-pass hypotheses.

## 4. MultiTalk: Scaling Full-Duplex Speech Models to Long, Multi-Party, Bilingual Conversation — `2609.36903` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 9/10**

*Ke Wang, Houxing Ren, Zimu Lu, Yunqiao Yang, Zhuofan Zong, Mingjie Zhan, Hongsheng Li* — **CUHK MMLab** and **CPII under InnoHK** · cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.SD · v1 submitted 2026-09-29 · NeurIPS 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36903)

*Problem:* Full-duplex speech models stay short and dyadic; meetings and group lessons need one model tracking many speakers over long spans, and neither open corpora nor benchmarks support codec-frame-level modelling of that.
*Method:* Extend the Moshi paradigm along both axes at once in English and Chinese — release 57.6k hours of synthetic long-form multi-party dialogue with controllable overlap, backchannels, interruptions and addressee shifts, plus a real-recording benchmark, then train a bilingual Moshi-style model.
*Result:* Sustains coherent multi-party conversation and substantially beats Moshi, MiniCPM-o-4.5 and Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct on MultiTalkBench.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 rather than higher — full-duplex and speech-to-speech are Core Interests for you, not Primary, and this is a conversational-modelling paper rather than a recognition one. But it earns the slot on the standout-quality exception, and Quality 9 is the highest here on evidence: **NeurIPS 2026** acceptance, and it attacks data, model *and* evaluation together instead of claiming one and hand-waving the others. The benchmark design is what makes it credible — built from **real human recordings** averaging **32.6 minutes** with explicit probes for long-range entity tracking, topic coherence and addressee selection, which is a much harder target than the short dyadic sets the field has been scoring on, and the baselines it beats include a 30B omni model rather than only Moshi. Three datasets (MultiTalkPT, MultiTalkFT, MultiTalkBench) are already on the Hub. The reservation that keeps it off the top: 57.6k of the training hours are **synthetic**, so the long-range coreference and addressee behaviour is learned from simulated conversation and only *tested* on real speech.

## 5. WenetSpeech-Min: A Large-Scale Minnan Speech Corpus with Dual Transcriptions for Dialectal Speech Processing — `2609.36834` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Haoyu Zhang, Chunjiang He, Hongtao Li, Zeyu Zhu, Qituan Shangguan, Chengyou Wang, Jingbin Hu, Ziyu Zhang, Bingshen Mu, Yanbo Wang, Shuai Wang, Jinhui Ye, Chengdong Liang, Binbin Zhang, Pengcheng Zhu, Chuang Ding, Qianze Feng, Qingyang Hong, Liumeng Xue, Lei Xie* — **ASLP@NPU (Northwestern Polytechnical University)**, **Nanjing University**, **Xiamen University**, **WeNet Open Source Community**, UNSW, Moonstep AI, Nexdata · eess.AS · v1 submitted 2026-09-29 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36834)

*Problem:* Dialectal speech technology is bottlenecked on real-world data, and for Minnan almost nothing exists at scale with paired Minnan and Mandarin transcripts.
*Method:* Collect ~10,000 hours of Minnan speech from diverse online media, transcribe every utterance **twice** — once in Minnan, once in Mandarin — and ship manually verified evaluation sets with both an ASR benchmark over the two transcript types and a Minnan TTS benchmark.
*Result:* Models trained on it beat the evaluated open-source systems on most metrics and approach commercial ones.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — low-resource and dialectal ASR is Primary Focus, and the dual-transcription design is what lifts this above "another corpus": one recording with both a Minnan and a Mandarin target makes it simultaneously a dialect-ASR set and a **dialect-to-Mandarin speech-translation** set, which is an unusually direct fit for the multilingual and speech-translation ends of your profile. Quality 8 — 10,000 hours of *real* media speech (not synthetic) with manually verified eval splits is a serious data-collection effort, it comes from Lei Xie's ASLP@NPU with the WeNet community attached so the WenetSpeech lineage and the release machinery are both credible, and the results are reported honestly rather than triumphantly: **17.59% dialect CER** (15.21% with extra internal data) and, for Mandarin output, **54.24 BLEU against the best commercial system's 54.25** — parity claimed to two decimal places, not overclaimed. Not higher because a 33.05% Mandarin CER shows how far from solved this is, corpus papers are contributions of substrate rather than ideas, and everything still rests on "we will release" — the artifacts are promised, not yet public.
