## ★ 1. Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS: Freely Controllable and Highly Robust Speech Synthesis with Multi-Stage Training Paradigm — `2607.23938` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Bajian Xiang, Cheng Wen, Han Zhao, Hao Wang, Haoxu Wang, Jiawei Jin, Jiayan Cui, Jie Chen, Mengxi Nie, Tianyu Zhao, Weiqin Li, Xiang Lv, Xiangang Li, Yang Xiang, Yang Zhou* — **Alibaba Group (Qwen Team)** · eess.AS · 2026-07-27 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23938)

*Problem:* Production TTS must jointly nail content consistency, speaker similarity, prosody, controllability, multilingual coverage, latency, and robustness to messy reference audio — trade-offs prior systems handle only partially.
*Method:* A 12.5 Hz low-frame-rate speech tokenizer paired with a five-stage progressive training paradigm that jointly optimizes an autoregressive LM and a flow-matching decoder, controllable via free-form natural-language instructions and inline tags.
*Result:* SOTA or strongest-aggregate across SEED-TTS-Eval, CV3-Eval, instruction-following, long-form and robustness tests; #1 on the independent Artificial Analysis TTS Leaderboard; 16 languages, 20 Chinese dialects, 3-minute one-pass synthesis.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed lab (Alibaba/Qwen) shipping a flagship, leaderboard-topping controllable TTS system — squarely core-interest and about as high-signal as unreviewed preprints get; only capped at 8 on quality because it is a system report rather than a single sharp methodological novelty.

## ★ 2. Memory Efficient Audio Synthesis with Decoupled Temporal Depth Diffusion Transformers — `2607.23811` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Dongseong Hwang, Prasanth Yadla, Kaan Elgin, Shifas Padinjaru Veettil, Sivanand Achanta, Dipjyoti Paul, Ramya Rasipuram, Tyler Johnson, Emad Soroush, Chung-Cheng Chiu, Zhifeng Chen* — **Apple** · cs.SD, cs.CL · 2026-07-26 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23811)

*Problem:* On-device expressive TTS must detokenize semantic audio tokens to high-fidelity waveform within a phone's tight compute/memory budget, where conventional transformer/GAN detokenizers scale memory linearly or quadratically with length.
*Method:* An RVQ detokenizer that decouples temporal and depth processing — streaming encoder, temporal decoder, and one reusable DiT-style depth decoder generating all RVQ levels autoregressively — with causal sliding-window attention and fixed KV cache for constant memory.
*Result:* Powering Siri Expressive Voices on Apple's AMX at ~10 ms/step (16× faster than real time), 21 MB peak runtime memory, 20–320 s streaming, and +0.28 MOS overall (+0.42 conversational) over the prior on-device TTS.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed lab (Apple) with a concrete, deployed on-device synthesis result — core codec/TTS territory, honest MOS gains, ablations, and a genuinely useful constant-memory architecture rather than an incremental tweak.

## ★ 3. OmniVAE: An Audio-Video VAE with Cross-Modal Alignment for Joint Generation — `2607.23855` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Jun Zhan, Chen Yang, Yitian Gong, Donghua Yu, Kuangwei Chen, Wenbo Zhang, Kexin Huang, Qi Luo, Zhe Xu, Ying Zhu, Jin Wang, Tengyue Zhang, Qi Chen, Cheng Chang, Songlin Wang, Junqi Dai, Jiasheng Ye, Xiaogui Yang, Tianyi Liang, Xiangyu Peng, Zhaoye Fei, Shimin Li, Qinyuan Cheng, Xie Chen, Xinchi Chen, Xipeng Qiu* — **Fudan University & SJTU X-LANCE** · cs.SD, cs.CV · 2026-07-26 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23855)

*Problem:* Joint audio–video generation needs fine-grained cross-modal correspondence, but training audio and video VAEs separately leaves their latent spaces unaligned, forcing the downstream generator to learn synchronization from scratch.
*Method:* A jointly trained audio-video VAE that adds a segment-level audio-video contrastive objective for temporal-semantic alignment and distills features from pretrained modality-specific semantic encoders into each latent space.
*Result:* Both objectives consistently improve latent-space learnability, yielding higher generation quality and tighter cross-modal synchronization in downstream text-to-audio-video generation.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed author Xie Chen (SJTU X-LANCE) with Xipeng Qiu's Fudan group — an omnimodal-foundation direction from a strong lineage; only moderately central to pure speech, but the unified-representation idea and thorough ablations make it a solid share. The single HuggingFace Papers audio pick of the day.

## 4. MoLGE: Mixture of Language Group Experts for Efficient Scaling of Massively Multilingual Speech Recognition — `2607.24030` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Sangmin Lee, Woojin Chung, Woongjib Choi, Hong-Goo Kang* — **Yonsei University (DSP&AI Lab)** · cs.CL, cs.SD · 2026-07-27 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24030)

*Problem:* Massively multilingual ASR suffers the "curse of multilinguality," where capacity is diluted across hundreds of languages and per-language MoE schemes explode the number of expert modules.
*Method:* Built on speech SSL models, MoLGE assigns experts to clusters of similar languages (far fewer than per-language MoE) and adds a hierarchical LoRA over disentangled acoustic and linguistic components, with linguistic- and data-driven grouping strategies.
*Result:* On a 495-language benchmark, MoLGE consistently beats dense multilingual baselines with minimal extra trainable parameters, improving both phonetic and orthographic ASR aspects.

**Why it ranked:** Core-interest multilingual ASR with a clean, parameter-efficient MoE-by-language-group idea, a 495-language evaluation, COLM 2026 acceptance and released code — a credible, well-scoped contribution rather than yet another dense-model tweak.

## 5. Music-JEPA: Learning a World Model of Sound from Action — `2607.22000` — **Relevance 6/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Ziyu Wang, Kun Fang, Yann LeCun* — **New York University & Meta AI** · cs.SD · 2026-07-24 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.22000)

*Problem:* JEPA-style self-supervised world models have been applied to music only tentatively, and it is unclear how such frameworks can naturally form a world model of musical sound.
*Method:* Frame piano music as an action-conditioned system — audio is the state, pianoroll the action — and train a JEPA offline on paired audio-pianoroll to predict future audio latents from a current state and action, mirroring how humans learn sound through interaction.
*Result:* The model captures action→sound relationships; its representations support beat tracking, composer identification and key estimation, and enable piano transcription via planning by searching for actions explaining a target sound.

**Why it ranked:** Standout-quality, genuinely novel action-conditioned world-model framing from Yann LeCun's group — only moderately relevant (piano-only, music-focused) but the ambition and pedigree clear the bar under the "let a great paper in even if less relevant" rule.
