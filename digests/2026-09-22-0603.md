## 1. ★ Voice of Reason: Reinforcement Learning for Spoken Math — `2609.18677` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Timothée Weisselberger, Edouard Grave, Alexandre Défossez* — **Kyutai**, Paris (Défossez also **Gradium**) · cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-16 · COLM 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18677)

*Problem:* Speech-native language models buy paralinguistics and low latency but lose the text model's reasoning — GLM-4-Voice scores 27.3% on spoken GSM8K where a cascaded ASR→LLM→TTS pipeline scores 95.7%.
*Method:* Supervised fine-tuning on synthesized spoken question-answering data, then RL with verifiable rewards on the speech model directly, with and without STITCH-style streaming reasoning.
*Result:* 65.5% without any extra reasoning tokens and **74.8% free-form** combined with streaming reasoning — a new state of the art for speech-native mathematical ability, with both checkpoints released.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 and the sharpest instance of the profile's Primary Focus #4 in the window — this is the knowledge-retention question with an unambiguous scalar answer attached, asked about a backbone (GLM-4-Voice) that the whole field uses as a reference. Quality 8 for a baseline table most groups would have trimmed: **Qwen2.5-Omni at 84.7%, Qwen3-Omni-30B at 94.6%, and the cascade at 95.7% all appear in it**, so the paper publishes the fact that its own SOTA is still twenty points behind the thing you would actually deploy. The "even without extra reasoning tokens" result (65.5%, beating the original STITCH's 58.7% which needed them) is the load-bearing one, because it isolates RL's contribution from the reasoning-trace scaffolding that previous speech-math results leaned on. **COLM 2026** acceptance, error bars on every number (±1.1), and two checkpoints on the Hub (`glm-4-voice-of-reason-9b`, `-stitch-9b`). Held to 8 because the evidence is one backbone on one benchmark — GSM8K, whose text-domain saturation is well known — three authors, and no ablation over reward shaping; the mechanism is imported wholesale from text RLVR rather than adapted to speech.

## 2. AURA: Uncertainty-Routed Activation Editing for Acoustic Grounding in Speech Foundation Models — `2609.23979` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Natarajan Balaji Shankar, Zilai Wang, Zihan Wang, Mohan Shi, Kaiyuan Zhang, Abeer Alwan* — **Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, University of California Los Angeles** · eess.AS, cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-21 · IEEE SLT 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23979)

*Problem:* Attention encoder-decoder recognisers such as Whisper emit fluent text that the audio does not support — on non-speech input, on weak acoustic evidence, on imperfect labels — because nothing in the decoder is checking whether it is still listening.
*Method:* Freeze the model and apply sparse scale-and-shift edits to decoder cross-attention heads, routed at inference by uncertainty features that detect over-concentrated attention, diffuse attention and abrupt frame shifts.
*Result:* Hallucination on non-speech audio falls **89.18% → 1.94%** with no prior hallucination-head identification, at 85.8k trainable parameters against LoRA's 41.9M.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — acoustic grounding in AED foundation models is Tier-1 twice over (Whisper-based work, ASR robustness), and it is the recognition-side twin of the failure the 09-16 digest's #4 diagnosed from the RL side: a model that has stopped conditioning on the audio and started generating from its language prior. Quality 8 because the mechanism is genuinely new rather than another parameter-efficient fine-tuning variant — **the routing signal is computed from the model's own cross-attention statistics at inference**, so nothing has to be labelled or identified in advance, which is precisely what prior hallucination-head work required. The evaluation resists the easy framing: alongside UrbanSound8K non-speech it runs **MyST (child), TED-LIUM 3 (adult) and FluencyBank (disfluent)**, i.e. the imperfect-label and disfluency regimes where grounding fixes usually cost accuracy, and reports 14.2% vs LoRA's 14.4% on MyST and a tie at 7.8% on TED-LIUM 3 — parity, honestly labelled, at **500× fewer parameters**. Code and models at `github.com/balaji1312/aura`, scaled across all five Whisper sizes. Held to 8 because the headline 89→1.94 is on non-speech audio, the most forgiving of the four regimes, and "approaches LoRA WER" is parity rather than improvement — the contribution is the cost, not the ceiling.

## 3. ★ NemotronLabs VoiceChat: An Open Full-duplex Speech-to-Speech Model with Tool Calling Capabilities — `2609.21967` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Jagadeesh Balam, Travis Bartley, Edresson Casanova, Zhehuai Chen, Slyne Deng, Slim Essid, Boris Ginsburg, Ameya Mahabaleshwarkar, Oluwatobi Olabiyi, Ankita Pasad, Yifan Peng, Elena Rastorgueva, Piotr Żelasko* and 36 others (49 total) — **NVIDIA** · cs.CL, cs.AI · submitted 2026-09-18 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21967)

*Problem:* Full-duplex speech-to-speech models cannot call tools, and bolting an agent onto one usually destroys the turn-taking behaviour that made it worth building.
*Method:* A streaming FastConformer encoder and Nemotron-Nano-9B decoder with parallel output streams for agent text and structured function calls, an auxiliary RNN-T branch for incremental user transcription, and a streaming TTS decoder — all in one graph.
*Result:* Lowest pause-handling takeover rate among open systems, 100% takeover after interruptions, 93% backchannel resumption, 82.5% tool-selection F1, 55.1 VoiceBench; **11B checkpoint on the Hub**.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — full-duplex is Core rather than Primary, but the **auxiliary RNN-T user-transcription branch** continues exactly the thread the 09-16 digest opened at #1 (also NVIDIA), and the question of what recognition costs when the model is also conversing is a Tier-1 question wearing a duplex hat. Quality 8 for shipping the weights (`nvidia/NVIDIA-NemotronLabs-VoiceChat-11B`) against three separate Full-Duplex-Bench versions rather than an in-house harness, and for a limitations section that is unusually willing to lose the argument: the paper states that **knowledge and reasoning are weaker than the base LLM** because the objectives compete, that tool use degrades past roughly five exposed tools, that argument extraction lags tool selection, and that the context window is about two minutes. That admission is the most useful sentence in the report for this profile — it is the modality tax, quantified by the people selling the model. Concrete deployment numbers too: **p95 118 ms per 160 ms chunk, 1.36× real-time at four concurrent streams on one H100**. Held to 8 because it is a system report with no ablation isolating any one design choice, 49 authors and a component inventory in place of an idea; the companion `2609.19334` below carries the actual mechanism.

## 4. ★ Multimodal Conversational Context for LLM-Based ASR: Data Construction, Training, and Benchmark — `2609.19765` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Longhao Li, Jian Tang, Yuxiang Kong, Jie Chen, Binbin Zhang, Lei Xie, Xiangang Li* — **Alibaba Token Foundry** · eess.AS · submitted 2026-09-17 · technical report · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19765)

*Problem:* Conversational ASR conditions on the *transcripts* of previous turns, which propagates its own recognition errors and discards the pronunciation and speaker evidence that would have disambiguated the entity in the first place.
*Method:* Build dialogues around entities and their confusable homophone forms, then interleave the user's historical **speech** with assistant text for supervised fine-tuning; release MM-ContextASR Bench over five scenarios.
*Result:* Speech+text context gives the highest entity recall on both backbones (87.84% Qwen3-Omni, 85.20% Step-Audio-2-mini); AliMeeting target-speaker CER falls 30.25% → 24.59%.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — the single most on-profile abstract in the window, hitting entity-aware recognition, contextual biasing, rare-word recall and LLM-based ASR in one paper, and answering the question the profile actually poses: does the speech carry information the transcript throws away? It does, and the paper measures how much. Quality 7 for testing on **two independent backbones** (Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct and Step-Audio-2-mini) rather than one, for extending past the headline task into **accent, dialect and target-speaker ASR** where the speech-context claim should generalise and does (KeSpeech 4.40% → 4.17%, CV-Yue 4.14% → 3.74%), and for reporting the uncomfortable finding that both models **mishandle irrelevant and erroneous history** — the failure mode that makes conversational context dangerous in production. Benchmark and evaluation code are public at `github.com/llh666521/MM-ContextASR`. Held to 7 because it is a technical report with no venue, the entity-recall margins over text-only context are thin (87.84 vs 86.48, 85.20 vs 83.56 — roughly a point, on their own benchmark), and the training data is synthesized around confusable forms, which is the construction most likely to flatter a method built for confusable forms.

## 5. Design of the IBM Granite 5.0 TurboCTC ASR Model — `2609.20104` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Brian Kingsbury, George Saon, Masayuki Suzuki, Hong-Kwang J. Kuo, Takashi Fukuda, Samuel Thomas, Vishal Sunder, Avihu Dekel* — **IBM Research** · cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-17 · submitted to ICASSP 2027 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20104)

*Problem:* Open recognisers on the speed–accuracy Pareto frontier are mostly large, mostly trained on undisclosed data, and mostly licensed in ways that make them awkward to build on.
*Method:* A 470M encoder-only CTC model — pyramidal temporal subsampling via strided depthwise convolutions inside Conformer blocks, chunk-wise block-diagonal self-attention, conditioning on middle-layer intermediate predictions, Muon optimizer, ~60k hours of public English corpora only.
*Result:* **5.83% average WER** across the seven Open ASR Leaderboard sets at **13,715 RTFx** — twice as fast as the fastest competitor on the board, permissively licensed on the Hub.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — pure Tier-1 ASR with nothing speculative about it, and the rare kind of paper whose deliverable is a model you can drop into a pipeline this afternoon rather than a claim to reproduce. Quality 8 on three grounds that are hard to fake: **public data only** (MLS, YODAS, CommonVoice-17, LibriSpeech, VoxPopuli, AMI, Earnings-22), so the leaderboard numbers are not contaminated by an undisclosed corpus; a **permissive license with the checkpoint already published** (`ibm-granite/granite-speech-5.0-470m-turboctc`); and Kingsbury and Saon's group, whose CTC and Conformer work has been the reference implementation for a decade. The per-set spread is published rather than averaged away — 1.34% LibriSpeech-clean against 10.17% Gigaspeech and 8.65% Earnings22 — and the 49% speedup is attributed to two named inference changes (1×1 convolutions replaced by linear layers, optimized Conformer attention) rather than asserted. Held to 8 because it is a five-page system description **submitted, not accepted**, with no ablation separating pyramidal subsampling from block-diagonal attention from the Muon optimizer, and the novelty is assembly rather than invention. Read it for the recipe; download it for the Pareto point.
