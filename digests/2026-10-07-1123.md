## ★ 1. Teaching LLMs to Hear Who Spoke What: Metadata-Supervised Pretraining for Encoder-Free Speech-LLMs — 2610.01695 — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 8/10**

Mohan Shi, Ruchao Fan, Sunit Sivasankaran, Keqi Deng, Jinyu Li — University of California, Los Angeles; Microsoft CoreAI (Redmond) · eess.AS · 2026-10-01 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01695) · ★ followed author: Jinyu Li

*Problem:* Encoder-based Speech-LLMs inherit encoders tuned for linguistic content, discarding acoustic cues needed for speaker and paralinguistic understanding; encoder-free variants avoid this but lack a pretraining recipe.
*Method:* Metadata-supervised pretraining (MSP) supervises Mel-to-LLM embedding layers with speaker identity and emotion labels, plus speaker-aware utterance composition and random span masking.
*Result:* On joint ASR + diarization, the encoder-free model beats its randomly initialised encoder-based counterpart and rivals encoders pretrained on far larger corpora.

**Why it ranked:** Sits exactly on your speech–text modality-alignment interest — what the LLM input interface should be and what it costs — and the matched-data comparison plus honest "competitive, better in several settings" framing makes it a credible read from a strong ASR group.

## 2. DirectSpeech2LLM: A Simple End-to-End Framework to Mitigate Prompt Overfitting in Speech-LLMs — 2610.08085 — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 7/10**

Hemant Yadav, Sunayana Sitaram, Roger Zimmermann, Rajiv Ratn Shah — IIIT Delhi; Microsoft Research India; National University of Singapore · cs.CL · 2026-10-06 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08085)

*Problem:* Speech-LLMs trained only on ASR instructions collapse into ASR systems and lose the backbone LLM's instruction-following on unseen tasks — prompt overfitting.
*Method:* A distance-based CTC loss over the frozen LLM embedding matrix, with greedy CTC labels giving geometrically and temporally aligned speech embeddings fed straight to the LLM.
*Result:* Trained on 960 h LibriSpeech only, it beats a cascade on ASR and transfers zero-shot to speech translation and emotion recognition.

**Why it ranked:** This is your knowledge-retention question stated as the paper's thesis, and the evidence is the right kind — two LLM families, scaling in data and capacity, plus a negative finding that explicit geometric alignment matters less than assumed; held back from higher Quality only by the single small training corpus.

## 3. Factorized Delayed Streams Modeling for LLM-based Streaming ASR — 2610.04333 — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

Tatsunari Takagi, Kai Washizaki, Atsushi Kojima, Lianbo Liu, Koki Nikaido, Yui Sudo — SB Intuitions; Toyohashi University of Technology · eess.AS, cs.CL, cs.SD · 2026-10-03 · ICASSP 2027 submission · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04333)

*Problem:* Delayed Streams Modeling bolts ASR-specific `<p>` and `<w>` tokens into the LLM vocabulary and predicts them through the same softmax, polluting the text distribution.
*Method:* Drop `<w>` entirely, then factorise the waiting probability for `<p>` away from the original LLM vocabulary distribution, letting the large softmax be skipped on waiting steps.
*Result:* Better WER than DSM on CSJ and LibriSpeech, much lower GPU memory, and less text-only perplexity degradation.

**Why it ranked:** Streaming LLM-based ASR with a clean architectural fix, and the perplexity result is a direct measurement of how much backbone knowledge the speech adaptation costs — exactly the quantity you care about; a focused rather than ambitious contribution, hence Quality 7.

## ★ 4. AURAL: Adaptive Latent Reasoning with Joint Chunk for Speech Language Models — 2610.01560 — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**

Yuxiang Wang, Kunyu Feng, Yuancheng Wang, Zihang Liu, Shengbo Cai, Qinke Ni, Wan Lin, Tao Feng, Yingda Shen, Ming-Hao Hsu, Zhixian Zhao, Liqiang Zhang, Teddy Sun, Steve Yves, Zhizheng Wu — Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen; Tencent Hunyuan; Tsinghua University; HKUST; Amphion · cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.SD · 2026-10-01 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01560) · ★ followed author: Zhizheng Wu

*Problem:* Chain-of-thought lifts speech-LLM reasoning but its intermediate tokens blow up response latency, and existing latent reasoning trails CoT with fixed, non-adaptive budgets.
*Method:* Model a distribution over multiple latent reasoning continuations, predict future-state chunks jointly, bootstrap from a new 683K-utterance bilingual CoT corpus, then RL for concise reasoning.
*Result:* Matches CoT-RL quality while cutting time-to-first-token 11.8× on Qwen2.5-Omni (1.22 s → 0.10 s); harder questions elicit more latent steps.

**Why it ranked:** Speech-LLM architecture work with a release-sized dataset contribution and an unusually clean latency number, from a group with a track record; relevance is Core rather than Primary since the tasks are reasoning and dialogue rather than recognition.

## ★ 5. Code-Switching Spoken Language Identification as Multi-Label Set Prediction — 2610.01450 — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**

Shunsuke Mitsumori, Matthew Wiesner, Shigeo Morishima, Shinji Watanabe — Waseda University; Johns Hopkins University; Carnegie Mellon University · eess.AS, cs.CL · 2026-10-01 · Accepted at IEEE SLT 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01450)

*Problem:* Code-switched speech slips past the monolingual LID filters used to curate massive multilingual corpora, quietly contaminating the data that speech foundation models train on.
*Method:* Reframe utterance-level CS-LID as multi-label language-set prediction and train a set generator that emits the language set directly, against atomic-pair and score-thresholding baselines.
*Result:* The generator infers language count on unseen pairs without being told it, but still trails oracle Top-k on exact set accuracy; thresholding fails outright.

**Why it ranked:** Code-switching and multilingual data curation are squarely in your primary area and this is the pipeline stage that decides what your training set actually contains; peer-reviewed and candid about its own negative result, which is a quality signal even though it caps the novelty claim.
