### 1. ★ Qwen-Audio-3.1-ASR Technical Report — `2610.28711` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 7/10**

48 authors listed alphabetically, including Zhifu Gao, Xiangang Li, Bin Ma, Binbin Zhang, Chengdong Liang and Jianwei Yu — **Alibaba Token Foundry, Alibaba Group (Qwen)** · cs.SD, cs.CL · 2026-10-20 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28711)

*Problem:* Production ASR also has to handle dialects, long-tail entities and who-spoke-when, but Qwen-Audio-3.0-ASR only transcribed.
*Method:* A BEST-RQ-pretrained encoder feeds a Qwen MoE decoder trained on ~20M hours, then refined with SFT, GRPO and on-policy distillation. It adds two-tier (P0/P1) hotwords, conditioning on earlier transcript text, and speaker-diarized output.
*Result:* Error on seven-language GigaSpeechBench falls from 22.50 to 15.09. P0 hotwords lift person-name recall from 62.1% to 99.4% (Fun-ASR-Flash: 95.3%).

**Why it ranked:** This flagship ASR release from a followed lab (Qwen) is the most detailed look this week at entity-aware LLM-based ASR at scale, with entity mining, tiered hotwords and history conditioning each measured. It is held at 7 for three reasons: the models are API-only with no sizes given, the entity and hotword results come from internal sets, and on public Chinese and English sets it is within a few tenths of a point of Fun-ASR-Flash and of its own predecessor.

### 2. ★ LazyFlash: Forward-Dense, Backward-Sparse Attention for Accelerated Long-Context Fine-Tuning — `2610.31911` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 7/10**

Keqi Deng, Ruchao Fan, Jinyu Li — **Microsoft** · cs.CL · 2026-10-22 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31911)

*Problem:* In long-context fine-tuning of text and speech LLMs, most attention time goes to the backward pass, and sparse attention rarely turns into wall-clock savings.
*Method:* The forward pass stays dense. Inside the FlashAttention backward kernel, query blocks with small attention-output gradients are skipped, under a per-head gradient budget with importance sampling.
*Result:* At 32K tokens the attention backward is 5.7× faster and a full training step 1.66× faster. On 10-minute English ASR, WER falls from 16.16 to 14.90 and entity error from 14.80 to 12.77.

**Why it ranked:** A followed author (Jinyu Li) cuts the cost of training speech LLMs on half-hour utterances, the trained model is still a standard dense one for serving, and the long-form ASR test reports entity error. It stays at 7 because it is an efficiency method tested on one speech setup (Qwen3-8B with a Conformer encoder, 50K hours), and its WER gain over dense training comes from single runs with no variance reported.

### 3. One Mean Log-Mel Vector per Phoneme: Simple and Fast Text Injection for Speech Recognition — `2610.34623` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

Albert Zeyer, Davyd Naveriani, Dorian Koch, Ralf Schlüter, Hermann Ney — **RWTH Aachen University, AppTek** · cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.CV, cs.LG, cs.NE · 2026-10-25 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34623)

*Problem:* Text injection lets ASR learn from unpaired text, but producing encoder input with a neural TTS model means training the TTS, which then dominates training time.
*Method:* A lookup table replaces the TTS. Each phoneme becomes one mean log-mel vector, repeated for a duration sampled from alignment statistics, with silence between words, and this is fed to a CTC+AED Conformer.
*Result:* It matches GlowTTS injection while training 1.8× faster. With an LM it reaches 1.52/3.24% WER on LibriSpeech test-clean/other, the best the authors know of from LibriSpeech data alone. The relative gain shrinks from 24% with 250 h of paired audio to 3% with 25k h.

**Why it ranked:** RWTH strips text injection down to a table lookup and backs it with full code, honest scaling curves, and ablations of durations and features. That makes it directly usable for low-resource and text-only domain adaptation. It isn't a 9 because the gain mostly disappears once paired audio matches the text in scale, and every test set is English.

### 4. Pseudo-Labels as Rewards, Not Targets: Group-Relative Policy Optimization for Semi-Supervised ASR — `2610.32572` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

Shashi Kumar, Yanis Labrak, Sergio Burdisso, Esaú Villatoro-Tello, Petr Motlicek, Kadri Hacioglu, Andreas Stolcke — **Idiap Research Institute, EPFL, Brno University of Technology, Uniphore** · cs.CL, eess.AS · 2026-10-23 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32572)

*Problem:* When the only in-domain data is untranscribed audio, ASR is usually fine-tuned on a teacher's pseudo-labels, so the student learns the teacher's errors.
*Method:* The pseudo-labels are kept but serve only as the reference for a WER reward in GRPO. The student is a speech LLM: a frozen WavLM-Large encoder and a projector, with LoRA on Llama-3.2-1B.
*Result:* On 54 h of banking calls, GRPO on Whisper-Tiny labels (26.4% WER) reaches 14.06% WER, against 17.21% for SFT on Large-V3 labels. SFT copies almost all teacher errors verbatim; GRPO copies 27%.

**Why it ranked:** It is a direct result for adapting LLM-based ASR, and the mechanism is shown: wrong label words rarely appear in rollouts, so their penalty cancels out. A gold-label topline shows GRPO loses 1.39 WER points to pseudo-labels, against 6.94 for SFT. It is held at 7 for three reasons: the setup is small (a 1B LLM and tens of hours of audio), much of SFT's deficit is hallucinated insertions, and for the four non-English languages direct GRPO fails until SFT has taught the model the script.

### 5. Why Full-Duplex Speech Models Collapse in Long Conversations — `2610.32349` — **Relevance 6/10 · Quality 7/10**

Yeonju Kim, Bella Godiva, Se Jin Park, Yong Man Ro — **KAIST, Kyung Hee University** · cs.CL, cs.SD · 2026-10-23 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32349)

*Problem:* Full-duplex models emit a token every frame and fill their context within minutes, yet benchmarks test only a few turns.
*Method:* The FD-LHB benchmark runs five models through 40 replayed conversations of up to 60 minutes and 40 live sessions with an LLM partner. It traces sliding-window failures to evicting the first KV entry and to self-reinforcing repetition, then patches both without retraining.
*Result:* Every model degrades or runs out of context. Over 60 minutes Moshi showed gibberish and long silences in every run. With the patches these fall to 0% and 7.5%, and repetition falls from 65% to 0%.

**Why it ranked:** Full-duplex is a core rather than a primary interest, but this is a clean diagnosis with a cheap fix that holds in both replay and live interaction. A concurrent SNU paper (`2610.34991`) independently finds the same decay over long sessions. It stays at 7 because pinning the first KV entry is essentially StreamingLLM's attention sink (the authors compare against it), and PersonaPlex still falls silent in 47.5% of replays.
