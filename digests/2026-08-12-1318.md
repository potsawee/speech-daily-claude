## 1. ReLMCodec: Designing Predictable Speech Tokens from Pre-Quantization Phoneme Structure — `2608.08286` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Zixiang Wan, Xusheng Yang, Zheng Wang, Peiji Yang* — **Tencent (Shenzhen) & Peking University Shenzhen Graduate School** · eess.AS, cs.SD · 2026-08-08 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08286)

*Problem:* Codec tokens optimized for high-fidelity reconstruction are not necessarily easy for an autoregressive LM to predict — the central, usually unexamined tension in every codec-LM TTS stack.
*Method:* A controlled study shows clearer phoneme structure *before* code assignment predicts easier AR prediction; ReLMCodec then follows a preserve–control–refine recipe — frozen SSL features at the quantizer input, Pre-quantization Anchor-Preserving Adaptation to limit reconstruction-driven drift, and a training-only WavLM-Large L24 teacher to cut phoneme-level token fragmentation.
*Result:* Single-codebook at 650/800 bps: WER 4.16 (vs. FocalCodec 5.01, SemantiCodec 12.23) and, at 64K, WER 3.96 / SIM 0.804 / PESQ 2.40 leading X-Codec2 on every reconstruction metric; downstream TTS WER 4.30 vs. X-Codec2's 9.49.

**Why it ranked:** The single most on-profile paper of the window, and it does the thing most codec papers skip — it *measures* predictability rather than asserting it, then designs against that measurement. The downstream TTS gap over X-Codec2 (4.30 vs 9.49 WER) is the number that matters, and it comes with probing scripts and representation statistics promised alongside the checkpoints. Held at 8 only because the release is acceptance-gated.

## 2. SraVaani 1.0: Scaling Inclusive Speech Recognition for Indic Languages — `2608.08235` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Sujith Pulikodan, Agneedh Basu, Pavan Kumar J, Pranav D Bhat, Suryansh Shukla, Nihar Desai, Prasanta Kumar Ghosh* — **SPIRE Lab & ARTPARK, Indian Institute of Science (IISc) Bangalore** · eess.AS · 2026-08-08 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08235)

*Problem:* India spans 700+ languages and thousands of dialects, but ASR coverage stops at a handful of them — most Indic languages have no publicly available system at all.
*Method:* A FastConformer trained from scratch in three stages — contrastive self-supervised pretraining on 31,255 hours of unlabelled VAANI speech, an audio–image representation alignment stage exploiting VAANI's paired images to enrich the encoder semantically, then TDT-CTC hybrid fine-tuning on 31,263 labelled hours from 24 public datasets across 65 languages and dialects.
*Result:* Mean WER 28.4% across the 17 comparable languages, against Gemini 3 Flash 39.4%, Sarvam Saaras v3 33.9% and IndicConformer-600M 30.2% — and the only model evaluated that transcribes 44 of the 65 languages at all.

**Why it ranked:** Directly on the multilingual / low-resource axis, at a scale that makes the claim real rather than aspirational: 62k hours, from-scratch pretraining, and honest per-language reporting instead of a single averaged headline. The audio–image alignment stage is the genuinely novel piece — using VAANI's image pairing as a free semantic signal for low-resource encoders is a trick that transfers to other under-resourced settings.

## ★ 3. Beyond Reconstruction: Full-Context Generative DiT for Music Generation — `2608.08787` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 9/10**
*Yunjia Li, Menglin Wu, Junyu Dai, Xinyue Fan, Xiangang Li, Haoxu Wang, Jianwei Yu, Huaicheng Zhang, Han Zhao, Weiqin Li, Yufei Shi, Cheng Wen, Sitong Zhao, Qixi Zheng, Haina Zhu, Wei Li* — **Alibaba (Tongyi / Token Foundry)** — no affiliations are printed on the preprint, but 12 of the 16 authors are the team behind `2607.20253`, attributed to Alibaba · eess.AS · 2026-08-09 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08787)

*Problem:* Hybrid generators pair an AR token planner with a flow/diffusion renderer, but the renderer trains on clean target-derived codec tokens and deploys on the LM's imperfect ones — codec-interface exposure bias.
*Method:* FullDiT reframes rendering as full-context generation from an imperfect discrete plan: a conditional DiT fusing eight frame-aligned RVQ streams with separately encoded captions and lyrics under non-causal attention over the whole song, trained with Error-Matched Distractor Conditioning (per-codebook corruption rates matched to teacher-forced top-1 error, near-miss tokens drawn from cosine-KNN) and four-way CFG at inference.
*Result:* EMDC lifts ViSQOL by 0.77 under synthetic corruption; the full system beats five commercial systems on 15 of 18 automatic metrics and ranks top-three on the Artificial Analysis Music-with-Vocals leaderboard.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed lab, and the standout-quality paper of the window even though music is only a secondary interest — the *insight* is not about music at all. Exposure bias at the AR-tokens-to-renderer boundary is the same failure mode every codec-LM TTS system has, and matching corruption rates to the actual teacher-forced error profile is a cleaner fix than the usual noise injection. Matched ablations and an external leaderboard placement, not just self-reported wins.

## ★ 4. CtrlSpeech: Coarse-to-Fine Control for Expressive Speech Synthesis — `2608.08362` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Zhisheng Zheng, Xiaohang Sun, Zhu Liu, Caren Chen, Rohith Kumar, Manoj Aggarwal, Gerard Medioni, David Harwath* — **The University of Texas at Austin & Amazon** · eess.AS, cs.HC, cs.SD · 2026-08-08 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08362)

*Problem:* Zero-shot TTS has largely solved naturalness and timbre cloning, but steering expression at the word or phoneme level — this syllable louder, that one held longer — is still out of reach.
*Method:* Built on DiTAR, CtrlSpeech layers phone-aligned pitch, loudness and duration signals on top of global speaker conditioning, so local prosodic edits apply without disturbing the cloned timbre; trained on ~20k hours of Emilia and GigaSpeech.
*Result:* Beats the DiTAR baseline on zero-shot quality (Seed-TTS test-en 2.58% WER / 0.63 SIM vs. 2.89% / 0.59) while cutting pitch RMSE 67.9→38.4 Hz, loudness RMSE 6.35→4.56 dB and phoneme duration MAE 28.1→11.9 frames under control.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed author David Harwath, and controllability is where expressive TTS is actually bottlenecked right now — the useful part is that control comes essentially free, with zero-shot metrics *improving* rather than paying the usual conditioning tax. Quality held at 7 because the architecture is DiTAR plus conditioning rather than a new idea, and the baseline set (DiTAR, DrawSpeech) is thin; Interspeech 2026 accepted, with demo, code and weights already public.

## 5. CuteTTS: Efficient and High-Quality Speech Synthesis via Autoregressive Modeling of Continuous Latents — `2608.08638` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Yuqian Zhang, Yao Shi, Kexin Huang, Botian Jiang, Zhe Xu, Yiwei Zhao, Min Liang, Shuang Chen, Xipeng Qiu* — **Shanghai Innovation Institute, OPPO AI Center & Fudan University** · cs.SD, cs.AI, cs.CL · 2026-08-09 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08638)

*Problem:* Continuous-latent AR TTS avoids quantization loss, but iterative flow/diffusion sampling and classifier-free guidance multiply the cost of *every* AR step — fatal for streaming latency budgets.
*Method:* A ~0.2B streaming system combining semantically aligned causal VAE latents, patch-level autoregression, explicit speaker conditioning and a bidirectional flow-matching head, then guidance-step distillation that folds CFG and multiple solver steps into one interval-conditioned student.
*Result:* LibriSpeech test-clean 2.16% WER / 78.9 SIM and Seed-TTS 2.04% (en) / 1.41% (zh); distillation cuts first-audio latency 23.3% (49.0→37.6 ms) and RTF 40.8% (0.184→0.109) for ~0.2–2 points of SIM.

**Why it ranked:** The second strong continuous-latent AR system in a week (after SemBridge on 08-10), which is itself the signal — this is where zero-shot TTS is converging, and CuteTTS attacks the axis SemBridge didn't: inference cost. Sub-40 ms first-audio at 0.2B with Seed-TTS numbers intact is a genuinely deployable operating point. Docked for a hedged "competitive" framing against unnamed same-size baselines and no clear release commitment.

### Honorable mentions
- **SonicWeave: Chunk-Routed Mixture-of-Experts for Unified Audio Scene Generation** — `2608.09571` — CUHK (Helen Meng, Xixin Wu, Dongchao Yang), Kuaishou Kling Team & Tsinghua SIGS. One flow-matching model for speech, music, sound effects, singing and their mixtures, routed at the *chunk* level by a conflict gate that trusts a global text/diffusion-phase prior when local acoustic state is unreliable. Consistent gains over matched Dense and Base-MoE baselines across TTS/TTA/TTM; the missing piece is comparison against external SOTA. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09571)
- ★ **Multilingual Emotion Neurons in Large Audio-Language Models** — `2608.08772` — Johns Hopkins CLSP (Xiutian Zhao, Philipp Koehn, Berrak Sisman) & Imperial College London (Björn Schuller). First neuron-level causal account of how LALMs encode emotion across 12 typologically diverse languages: per-language emotion neurons barely overlap, so the authors pool cross-lingual evidence (CR-Fusion) to isolate transferable units that give more precise affective control zero-shot and in low-resource settings. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08772)
- ★ **DuplexWorld: Can voice agents help you get through the day?** — `2608.10716` — Centific Global Solutions & University of Maryland (Dinesh Manocha). Six worlds (banking, insurance, travel, healthcare, logistics, Pathfinding), 156 scenarios, 350+ hours, scored on agentic, conversational and speech-naturalness axes rather than tool-calling alone. Best agents reach Pass@1 0.490 / turn-taking 0.653 / DNSMOS 3.378 — useful failure-mode analysis, expected headline. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10716)
- **A Unifying Perspective on Audio Generative Modeling: Latent Representations and Modeling Strategies** — `2608.08667` — Dongchao Yang (CUHK), solo. Organizes audio generation around two diagnostic axes — dependency horizon and conditional ambiguity — instead of the tired semantic-vs-acoustic split, arguing RVQ's residual order gives ordered *capacity*, not ordered semantics, and that AudioLM's cascade is one placement of the boundary rather than a template. A position paper, so profile-deprioritized, but from the UniAudio author it is worth an hour. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08667)
- **VoxZip: Semantic-Anchored Temporal KV Cache Compression for Long-Context Audio Inference** — `2608.08569` — Zhejiang University (Xize Cheng, Zhou Zhao, Tao Jin). Train-free two-stage compression using ASR transcripts as semantic anchors to temporally align and fuse audio tokens, then attention-decay eviction with early-token-bias correction. Holds >90% of uncompressed Qwen3-Omni performance at 20× compression; 1.9× throughput and 3.3× lower peak memory at 4×. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08569)
- **Beyond Naturalness: Probing Automated Text-To-Speech Evaluators on Linguistically Grounded Dimensions** — `2608.09930` — Oluwanifemi Bamgbose, Lindsay Devon Brin et al. Deconstructs "naturalness" into 10 perceptual dimensions annotated by trained linguists over 860 utterances, then benchmarks four MOS predictors and four Audio-LLM judges: MOS predictors collapse onto signal quality, Audio-LLM judges detect selectively and prompt-dependently, neither covers linguistically structured errors. Dataset and schema released; still work-in-progress. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09930)
- **VoxSumm: A Multilingual Corpus of Long-Form Spoken News for Joint Summarization and Translation** — `2608.10359` — Yejin Jeon, Min Ma, David Ifeoluwa Adelani et al. Formalizes joint speech summarization *and* translation, with 10,045 BBC article–summary pairs across 24 languages and ~703 hours. Finding worth noting: translate-then-summarize compounds instruction-following failures, and summarizing into English beats summarizing into non-English targets. [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10359)
