### 1. ★ Low-Latency LLM-Based Streaming Multi-Talker ASR with Verifiable Rewards — `2611.19136` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**

Jing Pan, Ruizhi Li, Ju Lin, Jing Zheng, Florian Metze — **Meta** · cs.CL, cs.SD · 2026-11-14 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19136)

*Problem:* A wearable device transcribes a live two-person conversation into fixed "wearer" and "partner" sides of a dialogue UI. The speaker tag must come before each turn, but SFT barely learns these sparse tags, and waiting to be sure of a tag delays the text that follows it.
*Method:* A streaming speech LLM (MoE with about 5.5B active parameters, one audio token per 80 ms) is post-trained with RLVR in a GRPO style. The reward is a fixed-role speaker-attributed WER, with attribution and deletion errors up-weighted, multiplied by a turn-level penalty for late turn onsets. Training uses simulated near-field/far-field two-talker mixtures.
*Result:* On 1,181 real recordings from the device (8 language pairs), SA-WER falls from 20.51% to 13.14%. That beats the SFT model's best-of-8 oracle (14.61%), and role swaps drop from 3–5% to 0.3–0.5%. Adding the delay reward cuts median turn onset from 0.92 s to 0.32 s, at 12.70% SA-WER.

**Why it ranked:** It applies sequence-level RL to a production-scale streaming LLM-ASR model for multi-talker, latency-critical use, from a followed lab (Meta), with a clean SFT → RL → RL-plus-delay breakdown on real recordings. It is held at 7 for four reasons. It is a 4-page paper on an in-house model and in-house data. Training uses only simulated mixtures. There are no external baselines. Nothing is released.

### 2. Learning from Distribution: Rethinking One-Hot Supervision for ASR and QA in Speech Language Models — `2611.20436` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 6/10**

Yujie Guo, Hongjie Chen, Jiaming Zhou, Jian Kang, Jie Li, Yongxiang Li, Yong Qin — **Nankai University; China Telecom AI (Xingchen AGI Lab)** · cs.SD · 2026-11-16 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20436)

*Problem:* Training a speech LLM on ASR destroys its spoken-QA ability, yet training it on QA alone keeps reasonable ASR. The authors blame ASR's near-deterministic targets combined with one-hot cross-entropy, which pushes the model into transcribing everything.
*Method:* LFD replaces one-hot CE with forward KL to a frozen text teacher. The teacher, Qwen3-4B, reads the transcript and is prompted to repeat it (or to answer, for QA), with a tail-aware top-k approximation. The setup is a Whisper-large-v3 encoder, an adapter and Qwen3-4B, trained on 8k hours of Emilia, with the data held identical between CE and LFD.
*Result:* After ASR-only training, QA accuracy is 2.4% with CE and 40.2% with LFD, with no QA data, and LibriSpeech WER also improves (6.89 → 6.57). Under full fine-tuning the gap holds: QA is 1.9% versus 42.9% at equal WER (5.52 versus 5.57). At 2k hours, CE fails to align at all (114% WER) while LFD reaches 9.8%. Top-1 teacher probabilities (k=1) are already enough.

**Why it ranked:** This is your knowledge-retention question exactly: why ASR training erodes the LLM, and a simple, data-free fix, shown with tightly controlled CE-versus-LFD pairs, scaling runs and full fine-tuning runs. It is held at 6 for five reasons:
- Only one small architecture is tested.
- Evaluation covers English only (LibriSpeech and TELEVAL).
- There is no head-to-head comparison with DiVA, BLSP-KD or the authors' own TS-OPD.
- The method sits close to existing cross-modal distillation.
- No code is mentioned.

### 3. Structured Speaker Conditioning for Diarization-Guided Target-Speaker ASR — `2611.16371` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**

Ruoyu Wang, Shutong Niu, Jun Du, Weixin Zhu, Yannan Wang — **University of Science and Technology of China; Ethereal Audio Lab** · eess.AS · 2026-11-12 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16371)

*Problem:* Diarization-conditioned target-speaker ASR in the DiCoW style gives the encoder only per-frame activity states. These cannot tell apart speakers with similar activity, and they describe overlaps and turn changes only coarsely.
*Method:* SSC builds the target condition from three parts:
- Each speaker's activity history, plus factorized pairwise and three-way relations to the other tracks (TCRE, linear in the number of speakers).
- A session-level reference identity for the speaker (RSC).
- FiLM plus a low-rank cross-channel modulation of the encoder (CLRM).

It adds 1.46M parameters on Qwen3-ASR-0.6B. It is then moved to FireRedAudio and post-trained with GSPO against target errors.

*Result:* With oracle diarization it gives a 6.2% mean relative cpER reduction over DiCoW-style four-state conditioning on AliMeeting, AISHELL-4, AMI-SDM and NOTSOFAR-1. With predicted diarization, the full system is 17.9% and 17.3% better than DiCoW and MOSS-TD respectively. On three unseen domains (MISP-Meeting, AISHELL-5, DiPCo) it scores 14.0/28.8/26.7 cpER, against DiCoW's 23.3/45.7/29.2.

**Why it ranked:** It is multi-talker meeting ASR on LLM-based recognizers, with ablations reported with paired bootstrap confidence intervals, two backbones and out-of-domain tests. It pairs naturally with last week's Pandemonium. It is held at 7 for three reasons. The headline wins over DiCoW and MOSS-TD come with a different, larger backbone plus RL; the controlled gain from SSC alone is 6.2%. No code release is mentioned. The paper is dense with engineering.

### 4. Hy ASR 3.0 Technical Report — `2611.14908` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 6/10**

Auden Li, Eitan Wang, Steve Yves (project leads) and 60 co-authors of the Hy ASR team — **Tencent (Hunyuan)** · cs.CL, eess.AS · 2026-11-11 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14908)

*Problem:* An LLM-based recognizer has to cover Mandarin, English, Cantonese and 12 more Chinese dialect groups. It should use whatever context is available (hotword lists, earlier QA turns, summaries) and stay robust on long-form, child and elderly, quiet and noisy speech.
*Method:* The model is built in five steps:
- An SSL encoder trained on tens of millions of hours, iterating BEST-RQ → HuBERT → RVQ targets.
- The Hy3 LLM as decoder, with an audio-conditioned hotword retriever that feeds a text prefix.
- Pre-training on ROVER-fused pseudo-labels, followed by SFT with iterative pseudo-labelling.
- Three expert RL models (general, dialect and context-biasing), merged by multi-teacher on-policy distillation.
- A final RL pass on keywords whose recognition depends on context.

*Result:*
- It has the lowest aggregate errors among the compared systems: 4.64% on Chinese, 5.22% on English and 5.06% on Cantonese. The comparison covers Qwen3-ASR Flash, FunASR Flash, Doubao ASR 2.0, Seed 2.0 Lite and StepAudio 3 ASR Max.
- It scores 3.77% on KeSpeech, against 4.33%.
- Keyword recall is 61.9% when the clue is elsewhere in the same utterance and 83.9% with dialogue context, ahead of Seed 2.0 Lite by 7.2 and 1.7 points.

**Why it ranked:** It is a big-lab LLM-ASR recipe aimed at contextual biasing and entity disambiguation, with a clearly described expert-RL plus multi-teacher on-policy distillation stage and decontaminated public benchmarks. It is held at 6 for four reasons. The public-benchmark gains are only 2.7–3% relative. The context and robustness results use internal test sets. The authors concede there are no with/without-context ablations within the model. Neither the model size nor the weights are given.

### 5. Low Language Transfer Speech-to-Text — `2611.16260` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 6/10**

Husein Zolkepli — **Scicom (MSC) Berhad, Malaysia** · cs.CL · 2026-11-12 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16260)

*Problem:* When you fine-tune an open multilingual ASR model on a few hundred clips of a language it has never seen, how much does its pre-training actually transfer, and what does that cost the languages it already knew?
*Method:* 28 open models are scored untouched on 50 very-low-resource languages in 11 scripts. Among them are Whisper, Qwen3-ASR, Parakeet, Canary, Nemotron, MMS, omniASR, w2v-BERT, Gemma and Voxtral. 24 of them are then fine-tuned on at most 1,000 clips per language (82 h in total), with a dev set choosing the epoch. Further runs compare LoRA with full tuning and against random initialization, trade data against epochs, measure forgetting, and check FLEURS transfer.
*Result:*
- Macro CER falls by 16.6–53.1 points. The best is 9.5 (omniASR CTC 7B), then 11.0 (MMS 1B), but WER stays at 35 or above.
- The same architectures trained from scratch stay 60 or more points behind.
- Fine-tuning costs the median model 5.7 CER on English, Spanish, French and German.
- LoRA on 4% of the weights comes within 1.1 points of full tuning, and forgets less on 2 of 3 models.
- Whisper-large-v3, when told a clip is Malay, translates the English mixed into it.

**Why it ranked:** It is a broad, practical map of how multilingual ASR transfers to low-resource languages, with forgetting measured, multiple seeds and confidence intervals that resample speakers. It is held at 6 for four reasons. The test set is tiny (25 clips per language, 2.56 h in total). Training and test come from the same scraped parent corpus, including Bible readings and a few FLEURS clips that were seen in training. It is a single-author company preprint. It proposes no new method.
