### 1. ★ GigaSpeech 3: Scaling Multi-Dialect Arabic Speech Recognition with Weakly Labeled Data — `2611.02476` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

Junzhe Liu, Yujie Tu, Yifan Yang, Peng Wang, Haoran Wang, Guodong Lin, Leyan Liao, Chengquan Zhang, Zhifu Gao, Xiangang Li, Qinyuan Cheng, Xipeng Qiu, Wei-Qiang Zhang, Guoguo Chen, Kai Yu, Xie Chen — **Shanghai Jiao Tong University (X-LANCE), SpeechColab, Seasalt.ai, Tsinghua University, Fudan University, Alibaba Token Foundry** · cs.CL · 2026-11-02 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02476)

*Problem:* Plenty of dialectal Arabic audio is on the web, but almost none of it has transcripts labelled by dialect.
*Method:* About 60 h of manual transcripts per dialect train ASR-biased SSL labelling models. These transcribe web audio matched to each dialect. Data selection and noisy-student rounds then produce 39k h across five dialects.
*Result:* Small Zipformers trained on the corpus reach 23.86% macro WER on GigaSpeechBench. The best third-party system, Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus, gets 28.62%. Fine-tuning Qwen3-ASR-1.7B on the corpus cuts its cross-corpus WER from 47.10 to 37.78.

**Why it ranked:** It is low-resource, multi-dialect ASR at corpus scale from followed authors Kai Yu and Xie Chen, with the corpus and recipes released. The gains also hold on three external corpora (MASC, Casablanca, SADA) and in a second model family. It is held at 8 for three reasons. GigaSpeechBench comes from the same group (Yujie Tu et al.). Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus still wins on Iraqi and Syrian, unless the multi-dialect model is given the true dialect label. Only Saudi and Syrian went through the full four noisy-student rounds.

### 2. ★ GSLM2: Multi-Stream Speech Representation Learning and Generative Spoken Language Modeling — `2611.01143` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 9/10**

Yosuke Higuchi, Tom Fizycki, Mahi Luthra, Charles-Éric Saint-James, Azeez Syed, Angel Villar-Corrales, Mido Assran, Nicolas Ballas, Jiayi Shen, Dongyan Lin, Phillip Rust, Surya Parimi, Vanessa Stark, Alexandre Allauzen, Balázs Kegl, Juan Pino, Emmanuel Dupoux — **Meta Superintelligence Labs, with Université Paris Dauphine-PSL and AMI Labs** · cs.CL, cs.SD, eess.AS · 2026-10-31 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01143)

*Problem:* Textless spoken LMs need far more data than text LMs to learn words and syntax. One reason is that a single stream of speech units carries little linguistic structure.
*Method:* SpidR 2.0, an SSL encoder with differentiable prediction heads, turns speech into discrete units at several levels of abstraction. A multi-stream LM models all the levels together over one shared temporal backbone.
*Result:* A 1.2B model trained on 60k h beats the 7B Moshi on all four zero-shot probes. At 6.5B parameters and 6M h it scores 80.7 on sWuggy, 72.9 on sBLIMP and 90.5 on tSC. Matching a text LM would take about 10²× more data, down from 10⁴×.

**Why it ranked:** This is the strongest speech-tokenizer and spoken-LM result this week, from followed author Juan Pino. The multi-level units scale better at every size, and the code and checkpoints are released. Relevance is only 7: it is English-only and textless, it is tested with linguistic probes rather than ASR or understanding tasks, and by the authors' own account it drops prosody.

### 3. ★ Learning Timeline-Preserving Speech-to-Speech Translation (TransDubber) — `2611.00690` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**

Zhisheng Zheng, David Harwath — **The University of Texas at Austin** · eess.AS, cs.CL, cs.SD · 2026-10-31 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00690)

*Problem:* Speech-to-speech translation keeps the content and the voice, but not the timing of speech and pauses that dubbing needs.
*Method:* Qwen3-ASR-1.7B is given speech-codec tokens and outputs transcript, translation and target speech, interleaved. It is trained on 642K duration-matched English→Chinese pairs, synthesized onto the source's speech-and-pause layout. RL with separate quality, duration and pause rewards then refines it.
*Result:* Against UniSS, the share of translations within 2% of the source duration rises from 25.6% to 61.5%. Pause F1 rises from 0.118 to 0.550, and COMET is about the same (0.788 vs 0.784).

**Why it ranked:** Speech translation is a primary interest. Followed author David Harwath defines a clear new task and releases code, data and models, with human ratings included. It is held at 7 for three reasons. It covers one direction (En→Zh) with synthetic targets, and the authors call it a work in progress. The ASR → MT → TTS cascade still wins on translation quality (COMET 0.854; human rating 3.97 vs 3.63).

### 4. ★ ReListen: Training-Free Evidence Localization in Hour-Long Audio with Frozen Audio Language Models — `2611.04480` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 7/10**

Ming-Hao Hsu, Shujie Liu, Yuxuan Hu, Yuxiang Wang, Yingda Shen, Jinyu Li, Zhizheng Wu — **The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen; Microsoft (CoreAI, Microsoft Research Asia)** · cs.CL, cs.SD, eess.AS · 2026-11-03 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.04480)

*Problem:* Hour-long recordings don't fit in an audio LLM's context. Transcribing them first loses sounds and voices, and agents that go back to the audio have to be trained to do so.
*Method:* A frozen audio LLM (Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B) rates each 20-second window by how likely it is to help answer the question. The model then answers from the highest-scoring minute.
*Result:* For 88.5% of VoiceGiraffe questions, that minute contains the annotated evidence (transcript retrieval: 58.8%). Answers beat reading the whole recording's transcript by 9.2 points. On LAT-Bench it matches a model trained for temporal grounding (47.7 vs 46.3).

**Why it ranked:** It comes from three followed authors (Jinyu Li, Shujie Liu and Zhizheng Wu) and gives a simple, training-free method for long-form understanding with speech LLMs. A clean comparison shows that reading the transcript is enough for speech content, but listening wins on sounds and voices. It is held at 7: there is one main reader model (a second appears only in the appendix), the three benchmarks are mostly English and Mandarin, and every question re-scans the whole recording.

### 5. Apriel Voice 27B Preview: An Open Model for Speech, Reasoning, and Tool Use — `2610.40738` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**

Aman Tiwari, Akshay Kalkunte, Akintunde Oladipo, Jash Shah, Tara Bogavelli, Hoang Nguyen, … Sai Rajeswar Mudumba (31 authors) — **ServiceNow** · cs.SD, cs.CL, cs.LG, eess.AS · 2026-10-29 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40738)

*Problem:* Adding speech to a strong reasoning LLM usually costs it some text ability, and voice agents also need to call tools.
*Method:* A frozen Whisper-large-v3-turbo encoder feeds Gemma 4 26B-A4B through a projector. First only the projector is trained. Then projector and backbone are trained on speech tasks, synthetic spoken dialogues and replayed text, with forward-KL distillation from the original model.
*Result:* Text scores barely move: MMLU-Pro goes from 85.27 to 84.42, GPQA from 77.8 to 80.4, and AIME is unchanged. The model scores 98.90 on Big Bench Audio and 81.13 on Speech-BFCL. Average English WER is 12.61%, and X→En translation reaches 42.87 BLEU.

**Why it ranked:** This is the most direct evidence this week on your knowledge-retention question. The whole backbone of a 26B MoE is adapted to speech, text reasoning stays essentially intact, and the model is released openly. It is held at 7 for three reasons. No ablation separates replay from KL, and the authors say component effects need controlled ablations. ASR trails Parakeet (10.06) and Audex-30B-A3B (11.40), and all of the ASR evaluation is in English.

### Close calls (checked, ranked, placed below the line)

- **★ Multilingual TASU** — `2611.00692` — SJTU X-LANCE (Kai Yu) / AISpeech — R 10 · Q 5.
  - The topic is squarely yours: aligning OWSM-CTC posteriors to Qwen2.5-1.5B from text alone, across 8 MLS languages.
  - Trained on text only, it reaches 10.51% WER, which is still worse than the CTC frontend alone (10.08%).
  - The useful finding: start from text-only alignment, then one epoch on 1/9 of the paired data reaches 9.19%. Full paired LoRA reaches 8.21%.
  - It is a small NCMMSC paper.
- **GSR-Bench (Grounded Generative Speech Recognition)** — `2610.41292` — NWPU ASLP (Lei Xie) / China Telecom — R 8 · Q 6.
  - It treats ASR as producing any text the audio supports, in four levels from literal to structured output.
  - Scoring uses LLM judges, validated against human preferences.
  - The framing is useful for speech-LLM ASR, but the metrics depend heavily on the judges.
- **Depth through Recurrence (on-device ASR)** — `2610.37833` — Qualcomm AI Research — R 8 · Q 6.
  - It distills the STT-480ms and Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v2 encoders into a few weight-shared recurrent Conformer blocks, with MoE-LoRA personalization.
  - The headline "97.4% of accuracy" is stated as accuracy rather than WER. For the 2-block student, average WER actually rises from 11.05 to 13.40, and LibriSpeech-other from 5.97 to 14.29.
  - The 105M Parakeet student is still 1.57 WER points behind its teacher.
- **★ Omnilingua-AudioMC** — `2611.04390` — Nankai University / Alibaba (Qwen) — R 7 · Q 6.
  - A parallel spoken version of Audio MultiChallenge in 10 languages (354 conversations).
  - Transcript scores predict spoken-input performance poorly.
  - For the two models with the largest gaps, replacing earlier user turns with transcripts recovers more than replacing the current turn, in all ten languages. This points to how spoken history is represented. The benchmark is small.
- **C3 (speech-guided KV retrieval)** — `2611.00333` — Amazon AGI / University at Buffalo — R 7 · Q 6.
  - Audio KVs go into a hierarchical index, and attention heads that share a KV head compete for one retrieval budget.
  - It is the best retrieval method tested on long-form ASR, translation and summarization across four speech LLMs. It is a workshop paper.
- **Kroko transducer on an ESP32-P4** — `2610.38739` — USTHB (single author) — R 7 · Q 6.
  - A 65.7M streaming Zipformer2 transducer is quantized to 2/4 bits (26.65 MB) and runs on a microcontroller with no neural accelerator.
  - Each 1.28 s chunk takes 0.90 s. LibriSpeech WER is 4.34/10.26, against 3.94/9.17 for the released model.
  - An unusually transparent engineering report that includes what failed.
- **Local or Escalate?** — `2611.01639` — UCLA / UIUC / CMU / Stanford et al. — R 6 · Q 6.
  - A logistic probe on MiniCPM-o 4.5's hidden states, read at the point where it switches from listening to speaking, decides when to hand a question to a stronger model.
  - Accuracy rises 27.1 points on the authors' internal benchmark, and time to first answer audio is 31.5% lower than always escalating.
- **History interference in speech-LLM memory** — `2611.05124` — BIT / Northeastern University (China) / Ant Group et al. — R 6 · Q 6.
  - Causal interventions on MOSS-Audio and MiMo-Audio show that irrelevant earlier audio pulls answers toward specific competing values.
- **Voice-native LLMs hear frustration but don't listen** — `2611.02893` — R 6 · Q 6.
  - Probes can decode a frustrated voice from the encoders of the 2 open-weight models. Yet when the wording is neutral, all 8 models call the voice frustrated in at most 3.1% of clips.
  - `2610.38570` (HEAR, on pain) shows the same pattern: the signal can be decoded in early layers and fades in deeper ones.
- **GhostPrefix** — `2610.42026` — R 6 · Q 6.
  - A reusable adversarial audio prefix that needs no synchronization and hijacks 13 open ASR models.
  - It succeeds 89.5–91.5% of the time over the air.
