## ★ 1. TEMPO: Temporally-grounded Multi-task Post-training for Large Audio-Language Models — `2608.29999` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 9/10**

*Apoorva Kulkarni, Kaousheik Jayakumar, Sreyan Ghosh, Ramani Duraiswami, Dinesh Manocha* — **University of Maryland, College Park**; *Utathya Aich* — **CNH Industrial India** · cs.SD, cs.AI, cs.LG · EMNLP 2026 Main · 2026-08-30 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29999)

*Problem:* Large audio-language models describe a clip but cannot say *when* anything in it happened, so multi-speaker ASR, diarisation and dense captioning stay out of reach for them.
*Method:* Post-train Audio Flamingo 3 (Whisper-large encoder → 2-layer MLP projector → Qwen2-7B) with atomic timestamp tokens, a time-aware projector injecting sinusoidal wall-clock encodings into frame embeddings, and a distance-aware Gaussian loss, on a synthetic-to-real curriculum; then refine with GRPO under verifiable temporal rewards.
*Result:* Multi-speaker ASR WER of 43.5% against Audio Flamingo Next's 167.1% and Qwen3-Omni's 69.7%, and 71.1% speaker-labelled diarisation F1 against Qwen3-Omni's 31.5%, with 119K training and 10K evaluation samples released.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed authors (Sreyan Ghosh, Dinesh Manocha; UMD, the Audio Flamingo lineage). Relevance 8 — timestamping is not your stated topic, but the failure it fixes is: an LALM asked *who said what, when* has to route acoustic evidence to the output rather than reconstruct a plausible answer from the transcript, and the 167.1% WER on Audio Flamingo Next is what happens when it cannot. Multi-speaker ASR, diarisation and dense captioning under one post-training recipe is also the practical shape you would want if you ever have to make an audio LLM produce alignment-grade output. Quality 9 — this is the most complete piece of work in the window: five tasks over AMI, ICSI, AudioSet Strong, TACOS and Slakh2100, two genuinely SOTA-beating margins against models *explicitly trained on timestamped data*, released data at both ends, EMNLP Main, and a project page. The honesty is what earns the last point: the paper states plainly that SFT delivers most of the gain and GRPO is a refinement providing "consistent but moderate" improvements, which is the opposite of the usual RL framing, and the limitations section volunteers five real ones — no overlapping multi-domain events, a fixed 0.1 s timestamp resolution that rules out sub-100 ms work, inherited constraints from the frozen Whisper encoder, no multi-seed validation, and a music component trained only on synthesised MIDI rather than acoustic recordings. Read 43.5% WER for what it is: a large relative win over other LALMs and still far worse than a conventional diarise-then-recognise cascade on AMI. The claim is that a single LALM can now do this at all, not that it should replace your pipeline.

## 2. Stride-k Subsampling: Train-Free Audio Token Reduction for Whisper — `2608.30927` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Chanhee Cho, Junhyuk Choi, Bugeun Kim* — **Department of Artificial Intelligence, Chung-Ang University, Seoul** · cs.SD, cs.AI · EMNLP 2026 Main · 2026-08-31 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30927)

*Problem:* Whisper hands every downstream decoder and Speech-LLM a fixed 1500-token encoder interface, and nobody has checked how much of that is redundant.
*Method:* Keep every k-th token after the convolutional stem and again after the encoder transformer — a deterministic indexing operation, no training, no auxiliary computation — and use CKA to attribute why it survives: acoustic overlap at the stem, attention-induced redistribution at the encoder output.
*Result:* Stride-2 at both positions cuts audio tokens 75% and GFLOPs 52–58% across all five Whisper scales, cutting end-to-end latency 19.6–27.4% on three Whisper-based SpeechLMs (Audio Flamingo 3-7B, Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct, LLaMA-Omni2-7B).

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — the highest in the window, because this is about the exact interface your work sits on. If Whisper's audio-token stream carries 4× more capacity than the downstream task consumes, that is spare budget for context, for longer audio, or for a bigger LLM in the same memory envelope, and you can test the claim this afternoon with an indexing operation. The CKA analysis is the part worth keeping even if you never ship the trick: it says *why* the redundancy is there, which tells you where a learned reduction would have room to beat a stride. Quality 8 for the completeness of the sweep — five Whisper scales × two insertion points × three SpeechLMs, with an honest ablation of each. **But the abstract oversells and the tables correct it.** "k=2 preserves baseline WER" holds on the clean sets (LibriTTS +1.07 WER, ESD +1.01 at large-v3) and collapses on the hard one: Common Voice costs **+9.90 WER at large-v3, +9.56 at medium, +13.40 at small**. Accented, noisy, far-field speech is exactly where the redundancy was doing work, so treat "75% free" as a clean-speech result and re-measure on your own domain before believing it. Held at 8 rather than higher for that gap between framing and evidence, and because the authors are candid that the method needs windowed-frame encoders (it fails on raw-waveform HuBERT and wav2vec 2.0) and internal encoder access, so it will not transfer to an API-only model. The train-free framing is also a self-imposed limit the paper never tests against: a short fine-tune after subsampling is the obvious next experiment and it is left undone.

## 3. Likelihood-Constrained Acoustic Reranking for Training-Free Hallucination Mitigation in LLM-Based ASR — `2608.30776` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Jiasheng Kuang, Linru Zheng, Hongjin Song, Zhaoqi Cui, Song Li* — **Shengwang (Agora)** · eess.AS · 5 pages, 4 figures · 2026-08-31 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30776)

*Problem:* An LLM-based ASR system's linguistic prior can override the acoustic evidence outright — the model translates instead of transcribing, executes an instruction it heard, loops, or deletes a span entirely.
*Method:* LCAR, a training-free decoding intervention: at each step keep only tokens whose base-model likelihood is within a margin δ of the greedy token, then rerank that set by an acoustic compatibility score computed from attention-pooled audio embeddings through the existing LM head — no extra model, detector, reference, or training.
*Result:* At δ=0.60, LCAR removes 38.8–57.1% of detector-identified hallucination failures across Qwen3-ASR-1.7B, Qwen2-Audio-7B, Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct and GLM-ASR-Nano-2512, while largely holding WER/CER on standard open-source test sets.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — this is your speech–text alignment problem stated as a decoding-time bug and fixed without retraining. The failure taxonomy alone is worth the read: *unintended translation, instruction execution, repetition, catastrophic deletion* is a precise description of what goes wrong when a strong text prior meets weak acoustic conditioning, and it is the operational cost of every modality-adaptation shortcut. Constraining the intervention to tokens the base model already finds plausible is the right design instinct — it buys acoustic grounding without letting a weak audio signal drag the output off the language model's manifold, which is how naive audio-biased decoding usually fails. And it is genuinely free to try on a model you already have. Quality 7, held there by the paper's size rather than its correctness. In its favour: the four systems are the right current ones rather than convenient old ones, and the authors are from **Shengwang (Agora)**, a real-time voice platform, so the hallucination taxonomy comes from production traffic rather than a thought experiment. Against: it is a five-page ICASSP-format submission, the hallucination set is human-audited **TTS** rather than natural speech (so the trigger distribution is synthetic, and TTS artefacts are precisely the kind of acoustic degradation that provokes prior-dominance), "largely maintaining WER/CER" is doing quiet work for a decoding change that must cost something, δ needs tuning per deployment with no guidance on picking it, and there is no code link. A strong idea reported at conference-abstract depth — cite it, then verify δ on your own audio.

## 4. HEAR Who Said What: Unlocking Speaker-Attributed Reasoning via Counterfactual Voice Grounding — `2608.29120` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Dongwook Lee, Sangkwon Park, Che Hyun Lee, Youngho Cho, June Young Yi, Heeseung Kim, Sungroh Yoon* — **IPAI / Dept. of ECE, Seoul National University**; *Eunwoo Song* — **Yonsei University**; *Junho Kim* — **University of Seoul** · cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.SD · EMNLP 2026 Main · 2026-08-29 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29120)

*Problem:* Speech LLMs are deployed into multi-speaker rooms without anyone establishing whether they attribute utterances by *voice* or merely by what the words imply.
*Method:* HEAR, a hierarchical benchmark of 2.4K human-verified samples over 887 multi-party clips, diagnosing speaker-attributed reasoning from the foundations up; then A2R, built by GRPO-optimising Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct on CASH — Counterfactual Audio with Speaker-level Hard negatives, designed so the linguistic route to the answer is wrong.
*Result:* Across 20 leading SLMs the foundational tasks are largely unsolved and models lean on semantic priors over vocal cues; A2R scores strongly on HEAR and transfers zero-shot to unrelated multi-speaker downstream tasks, with all resources released.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — the counterfactual construction is the contribution you can reuse. Building audio where the acoustic and linguistic answers *disagree* is the only clean way to prove a model is using the speech signal, and it generalises straight to your entity and rare-word setting: swap "which speaker" for "which name" and you have a probe for whether an ASR system is hearing a proper noun or predicting it from context. The zero-shot transfer result is the interesting claim — that speaker attribution is a bottleneck capability whose unlocking releases downstream multi-speaker performance the model already latently had. Quality 8 for doing both halves properly: a benchmark most groups would have stopped at, plus a 30B model trained to fix what the benchmark exposes, plus 20 systems evaluated, plus a public release, at EMNLP Main. Not 9, for three reasons. 2.4K samples from 887 clips is thin for a benchmark meant to arbitrate a capability claim, and the hierarchy makes it thinner per level. A2R is a GRPO fine-tune of Qwen3-Omni-30B evaluated on a benchmark from the same authors, which is the standard co-design risk — the honest evidence for the method is the zero-shot downstream transfer, not the HEAR score, and that transfer is the part the abstract quantifies least. And "counterfactual audio with speaker-level hard negatives" is a synthesis pipeline whose realism decides everything; if the negatives are constructed by voice-swapping, the model may be learning the swap artefact rather than speaker identity. Worth checking the CASH construction before adopting it.

## 5. Anchoring Speech with Semantics: A Multimodal Adapter Mechanism for Automatic Speech Recognition in Low-Resource Languages — `2608.29239` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Kuan-Tang Huang, Cheng-Yeh Yang, Chien-Chun Wang, Hung-Shin Lee, Berlin Chen* — **National Taiwan Normal University** (Huang also **EZAI**); *Hsin-Min Wang* — **Academia Sinica** · cs.CL, cs.SD, eess.AS · EMNLP 2026 Main · 2026-08-29 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29239)

*Problem:* Low-resource ASR is bottlenecked on the decoder side — scarce transcripts give almost no supervised evidence for target-side generation, however good the acoustic encoder is.
*Method:* SAMA-ASR, a lightweight adapter on a frozen Whisper Medium decoder that conditions each decoder state on two anchors before token prediction: a *semantic* anchor embedded from an auxiliary translation of the utterance, and an *acoustic* anchor from the speech itself.
*Result:* On 30-hour Taiwanese Hokkien (YT-THDC) and Hakka (HAT) sets it beats acoustic, prompt-based and semantic-only translation-guided baselines, reaching 23.48% and 22.48% CER with anchors produced automatically by an upstream ST model rather than supplied as oracle translations.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — low-resource ASR and speech translation are both Primary Focus for you and neither appears anywhere else in this window, and the mechanism is squarely your modality-alignment question: it uses a translation as a *semantic scaffold* to give the decoder meaning-level evidence the transcripts are too sparse to supply. That is the same move as leaning on an LLM's world knowledge in a Speech-LLM, done at 30 hours of data instead of 30 thousand. Quality 7 — the paper earns its keep on the experiment it did not have to run. Translation-guided ASR results are usually reported with oracle translations, which makes them unfalsifiable in deployment; these authors close the loop with an actual upstream ST model and report the degraded number, then add a translator-capacity analysis showing a *compact* ST model still produces useful anchors. That is the difference between a result and a demonstration. Berlin Chen and Hsin-Min Wang are also a long-standing pairing in Taiwanese-language speech, so Hokkien and Hakka here are a research programme rather than a convenient pair of low-resource labels. Held at 7 because the contribution is architecturally modest — an adapter conditioning decoder states on two extra embeddings, in a crowded design space — the evaluation is two 30-hour corpora of closely related Sinitic varieties (so cross-lingual generality is asserted, not shown), the improvement margins are not quantified in the abstract, and 22–23% CER is a working system rather than a solved task. It is also circular in a way the paper acknowledges but cannot escape: the ST model needs data from the same scarce languages the ASR system lacks.

- **★ `2608.30622` — Textual Acoustic Grounding for Generalizable LLM-Based Deepfake Voice Detection** (Yassine El Kheir, Xin Wang, Junichi Yamagishi et al. — DFKI / **NII**; SLT submission). Deepfake detection is Secondary for you, but the framing is not: it attacks the modality gap between continuous audio embeddings and the LLM's semantic space by injecting openSMILE features as structured *text* tokens. Two findings transfer beyond the task — fine-tuning the LLM risks out-of-domain overfitting so a **frozen** LLM is the stronger baseline, and textual grounding helps the frozen model *and* makes later fine-tuning more effective. +16.2% absolute Macro-F1 OOD on ITW and MLAAD; models public on HuggingFace.
- **`2609.00727` — Heard but Not Heeded: Paralinguistic Information Encoding and Loss in Audio-Language Models** (Bhuvan Koduru, Dareen Safar B Alharthi, Rita Singh, Bhiksha Raj — **CMU**). The mechanistic companion to #4: CKA, leave-one-speaker-out probing and a content–prosody leakage metric trace speaking style through Whisper-large-v2, Qwen2-Audio-7B, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and Chroma-4B. Style is strongly encoded in the **late encoder** and consistently degraded before the output; the projector reshapes geometry without destroying information, and decoders differ in how much they preserve. The gap between what a model encodes and what it *uses* is your question exactly — kept out of the top five only because it is analysis-only, four models, with no venue or artefact listed.
- **`2609.00562` — BiMTokenizer** (Xin Zhang, Lin Li, Chuanbo Liu, Jianquan Liu, Kong Aik Lee; EMNLP 2026 Main, code + weights released). A ~1.1 kbps single-tower codec that beats dual-tower designs at under half the parameters, with the lowest WER among low-bitrate codec baselines in clean *and* noisy conditions. Residual Spherical Leech Quantization — a fixed lattice bottleneck instead of a learned codebook — is a genuinely unusual idea and sidesteps codebook collapse by construction.
- **`2608.30348` — Perceptually Better, Semantically Worse** (Randy Frans Fela, Pejman Mowlaee; EMNLP 2026). The most quotable negative result in the window: MetricGAN+ **more than doubles** downstream LLM intent-flip rate versus unenhanced noisy speech (0.318 vs 0.135) *while improving PESQ*, and unmitigated echo hits 0.836 via speaker substitution — a failure WER cannot see at all. If you front any Speech-LLM with an enhancement stage, this is the argument for measuring it end-to-end.
- **`2609.01542` — TAG-Bench** (Yuhang Dai, Xin Shu, Zengxi Li, Lei Xie, Xiangang Li, Jianwei Yu). Temporal audio grounding over 1,750 human-verified pairs and 149.5 hours; the best of 21 systems reaches 31.2 mIoU and only 21.5% recall at IoU ≥ 0.7, and no model exceeds 13.2% count accuracy on one-to-many queries. The evaluation-side mirror of #1 — useful as an independent check on TEMPO's claims, since it is not the TEMPO authors' own benchmark.
- **`2608.31170` — Context-Aware Interleaved Batching for WhisperX** (Carlos Bain, **Max Bain** — WhisperX's author). Uses VAD segment boundaries to keep Whisper's text conditioning stable across batched segments, claiming lower WER and better proper-noun transcription at WhisperX throughput. Directly relevant to long-form ASR and named-entity recovery, and the author has the credibility. **Not ranked** because the abstract reports no numbers at all and lists no venue — flagged so you can watch for a fuller version.
- **`2608.28916` — VoiceCodeBench** (300 workplace recordings, 1,482 audited entities, 26 entity types, 12 ASR systems). Entity-level exact-recovery scoring: WER correlates with structured-token recovery at only ρ = −0.73, and the best system still leaves a workflow-critical value unrecovered in ~31% of recordings. Small, but the closest thing in the window to an entity-aware ASR benchmark.
