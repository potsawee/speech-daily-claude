## 1. Persistent Delivery Optimization for Streaming Speech-to-Text Translation with Revisions — `2609.26427` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Zixiang Wan, Delin Chen, Wei Shi, Haihua Xu, Youxi Xie, Yuexian Zou* — **Peking University, Shenzhen** and **Timekettle AI Lab**, Shenzhen · eess.AS · submitted 2026-09-22 · submitted to ICASSP 2027 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26427)

*Problem:* Revision-capable streaming translation is rewarded on the text a user currently sees, so process rewards credit drafts that are later withdrawn.
*Method:* Persistent Delivery Optimization pays intermediate reward only to content that survives every subsequent revision, and scores final translation quality on a separate term.
*Result:* Best BLEU in four of five directions and higher COMET than every external streaming baseline in all five, with 10.8%/11.3% lower mean/P90 finalization-aware latency, 15.8% less erasure, and code and weights released.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — simultaneous/streaming speech translation is Primary Focus #5, and this is the reward-design question inside it rather than another architecture. Quality 8 because the evaluation is unusually multi-faceted for four pages: it reports translation quality on **two** metrics, latency at mean *and* P90, a normalized erasure rate, and then a **zero-shot check on Europarl-ST and CoVoST 2** to show the gains are not a FLEURS-adaptation artifact — that last table is the one most ICASSP submissions omit, and it is the one that makes the result believable. It also publishes that it loses one of five directions on BLEU rather than reporting only the macro average. Held at 8, not higher: 7.49 hours of adaptation data on five directions is a small footprint, the base is a single History-SFT initialization with no second backbone, there is no human evaluation of whether the reduced erasure actually reads better, and this is an unreviewed ICASSP submission. Code and weights at `github.com/ggiggit/PDO_S2TT`.

## 2. ★ Spoken Language Models that Think Aloud — `2609.26488` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Junyi Ao, Kainan Peng, Mingbo Ma, Shun Zhang, Zhenyu Tang, Xutai Ma, Xiang Li, Yinghao Li, Yuancheng Wang, Zhizheng Wu, Haizhou Li, Qing He, Xubo Liu* — **Meta Superintelligence Labs** and **The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen** · cs.CL, cs.SD, eess.AS · submitted 2026-09-22 · **SLT 2026** · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26488)

*Problem:* Chain-of-thought reasoning makes a spoken language model go silent while it thinks, and the serial "think-then-speak" pattern breaks real-time interaction.
*Method:* Inside a Thinker-Talker architecture, a lightweight second stream emits short, task-grounded progress utterances conditioned on the evolving reasoning state; a runtime policy fires extra ones to cover gaps and cancels pending speech once the answer is ready.
*Result:* User-audible silence during reasoning falls substantially while answer accuracy on spoken reasoning and QA benchmarks stays level with the serial baseline.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — full-duplex and real-time spoken LMs are a Core Interest, and this is the first treatment I have seen of *reasoning latency* as the thing that actually breaks spoken interaction, rather than model latency. Quality 8 on provenance and framing: **Meta Superintelligence Labs**, accepted at **SLT 2026**, an author list carrying Zhizheng Wu, Haizhou Li and Xutai Ma (simultaneous translation), and a genuinely well-posed problem — the asynchronous two-stream decomposition with a cancel policy is the right shape of answer. The honest reading of the contribution, though, is that it is a **UX result, not a capability result**: accuracy is "comparable to" the serial baseline, so the paper buys silence-reduction and pays nothing, which is good engineering but is not a claim that the model got better. The abstract quotes **no numbers at all** — not the silence reduction, not the accuracy — which for a peer-reviewed paper is a stylistic choice rather than a red flag, but it means the size of the win has to be taken on trust until you open the PDF. No code or demo link on the arXiv page.

## 3. ★ A Practical Recipe for Semi-Supervised Federated ASR: Online Pseudo-Labels with Server Update Stabilization — `2609.25471` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 9/10**

*Wonho Bae, Zakaria Aldeneh, Martin Pelikan, Jan "Honza" Silovsky, Tatiana Likhomanenko, Sheikh Shams Azam* — **Apple** · cs.LG, cs.AI, stat.ML · submitted 2026-09-21 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25471)

*Problem:* In semi-supervised federated ASR, pseudo-label errors compound along the output sequence and across rounds into divergence, leaving a wide gap to fully-supervised federated training.
*Method:* Two coupled axes — the **teacher** (each client's own evolving model, a broadcast global model, or a global→online transition at round *r*) and the **anchor**, server-side training on the labeled seed *between* rounds.
*Result:* A per-client online teacher diverges alone but matches or beats the global teacher once anchored; the recipe improves on the strongest prior method in 9 of 11 pairs, by 20.8% in-domain and 10.0% cross-domain.

**Why it ranked:** Quality 9 — this is the most rigorous paper in the window and the clearest case for the profile's standout-quality exception. It does the thing almost nobody does: it identifies two design choices that are usually reported in isolation, shows they are **inseparable** (aggressive teachers only pay once the anchor stabilizes training), traces the interaction down to the settings that control it — augmentation strength and batch size, i.e. how much noise the server injects — and then characterizes *when* stabilization is needed as a function of seed-data dispersion and its overlap with client data. It reports **9 of 11**, not the average, so the two losses are in the paper. The author list is the reason to trust the pseudo-labeling claims specifically: **Tatiana Likhomanenko** (slimIPL) and **Jan Silovsky** have spent years on exactly this failure mode. Relevance held to 7 and not higher: ASR is Primary Focus #1, but semi-supervised *federated* learning is a deployment regime your profile never mentions, so the transferable value here is the teacher/anchor decomposition as a methods pattern rather than the federated numbers. No code release noted.

## 4. ★ MuLA-Bench: A Multilingual Long-Form Audio Understanding Benchmark via Multi-Tier Auditing — `2609.23416` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Zeyu Yang, Xinyu Zhang, Zibo Bi, Pei Zhang, Xize Cheng, Jin Xu, Baosong Yang, Satoshi Nakamura* — **Alibaba Group** and **The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen** · cs.SD, cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-20 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23416)

*Problem:* Long-form audio ability gets summarized as context length plus one aggregate score, which hides how language, evidence type and task jointly set difficulty.
*Method:* 5,038 open-ended questions over 1,769 in-the-wild recordings — 1,377.9 hours, 16 languages, eight domains — split into a balanced Language×Domain semantic track and an acoustic track that preserves naturally occurring non-speech evidence, audited with evidence-grounded generation, shortcut checks and language-expert review.
*Result:* Across ten audio-language models, language rankings flip between domains and tasks, long-range retrieval is comparatively strong, and precise clock alignment plus factual grounding of natural acoustic events stay fragile.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — multilingual benchmarks and knowledge-heavy evaluation sets are an explicit Core Interest, and at 16 languages over in-the-wild audio this is the most substantial multilingual audio-understanding artifact to appear in weeks. Quality 8 for three construction choices that matter and are usually skipped: it **does not translate a shared source set** into the other languages (so it avoids translationese and the English-centric question distribution that ruins most "multilingual" benchmarks), it **does not inject target sounds** into the acoustic track, and it runs shortcut checks so questions cannot be answered without the audio. The reported findings are diagnostic rather than a leaderboard — the observation that *temporal errors persist after the correct event is identified* is the kind of conditional failure a single score cannot show, and is directly useful if you care about grounding. Held at 8: it is a benchmark, so its value depends on adoption, the ten-model cohort is reduced to eight for the pooled diagnostics without that choice being justified in the abstract, and it is unreviewed. Public at `github.com/QwenLM/Omnilingua-Bench`.

## 5. ★ Qwen-Audio-3.1-Realtime: Towards Reliable Agentic Voice Interaction — `2609.25176` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Lujia Bao, Qian Chen, Luyao Cheng, Chong Deng, Yuxiang Kong, Xiangang Li, Xu Li, Jiaqing Liu, Chao-Hong Tan, Haoyu Wang, Wen Wang, Xilou Wang, Junhao Xu, Liang Yi, Binbin Zhang, Qinglin Zhang, Qiquan Zhang* — **Alibaba Group** · eess.AS, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.SD · submitted 2026-09-21 · 20-page technical report · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25176)

*Problem:* A real-time voice assistant has to reason over a request that is still changing, execute tool calls, and know when **not** to speak — objectives normally traded against one another.
*Method:* Three stages: Think (Core-Cocktail SFT plus multi-teacher on-policy distillation, transferring language ability while growing native audio skills), Act (GRPO with multi-granularity rollouts in self-evolving executable environments), and Speak-and-Coordinate for turn-taking.
*Result:* Task success rises 78.4%→82.0% over version 3.0 on a half-duplex τ-Voice adaptation, and the response rate to background speech drops 73.0%→13.0% on Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — full-duplex speech LLMs are a Core Interest, the Think stage is explicitly a knowledge-transfer-versus-native-audio-skill tradeoff (Primary Focus #4), and Alibaba is a followed lab whose audio releases set defaults the rest of the field then compares against. The **73.0%→13.0%** figure is the number to take away: responding to background speech is the failure that makes always-on voice assistants unusable, and a 5.6× reduction on a public benchmark is a real result, not a vanity metric. Quality held to 7, and the reasons are structural to the genre. The headline task-success number is measured on "**our** half-duplex speech-to-text adaptation of τ-Voice" — an in-house modification of the benchmark, which makes 82.0% incomparable to anyone else's number. Every comparison in the abstract is against **Qwen-Audio-3.0**, its own predecessor, so the report establishes that the team improved on itself and not where it sits against GPT-Realtime, Moshi or Step-Audio. The method is a stack of named components (M²-OPD, Core-Cocktail, multi-granularity rollouts) with no ablation isolating which one bought the full-duplex gain, and no weights or code link appears on the arXiv page. Read it as a capability disclosure, not as evidence.
