### 1. ★ D2: High-Capability Full-Duplex Spoken Language Models from Half-Duplex Backbones — `2610.17124` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 9/10**

Cheng-Kuang Chang, Alexander H. Liu, Brian Kingsbury, Hilde Kuehne, Rogerio Feris, Samuel Thomas, James Glass, Kai-Wei Chang — **MIT CSAIL, MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab** · cs.AI, cs.CL, eess.AS · 2026-10-12 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17124)

*Problem:* Full-duplex models that listen and speak at once still lag half-duplex ones on general capability.
*Method:* Dialogue-to-Duplex converts a pretrained half-duplex SLM — adapting its audio encoder to streaming via decoder-free representation distillation, then supervised fine-tuning for duplex interaction — with a systematic chunk-size study.
*Result:* Qwen3-Omni-D2 at 12.5 Hz hits 99.2% turn-taking success at 521 ms latency, 99.5% interruption success, and a state-of-the-art VoiceBench 70.1; also demonstrated on LLaMA-Omni 2.

**Why it ranked:** Followed lab (MIT CSAIL) on a Core-interest topic, and the strongest-evidenced paper of the window — two distinct backbones, a real latency/capability trade-off study, and a concrete SOTA number rather than a vibe claim.

### 2. ★ ASSET: Adaptive Speech Segmentation via Reinforcement Learning — `2610.14309` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**

Cihan Xiao, Yuan Tseng, Matthew Wiesner, Desh Raj, Leibny Paola Garcia Perera, Sanjeev Khudanpur, David Harwath — **Johns Hopkins University (CLSP / HLTCOE), UT Austin, NVIDIA** · cs.CL · 2026-10-09 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14309)

*Problem:* Acoustic encoders emit far longer sequences than text, and LLM decoders scale poorly with input length.
*Method:* A GRPO-trained segmenter cuts frozen WavLM/HuBERT frames into variable-duration segments, each pooled by a residual BiGRU into one continuous audio token, rewarded by task performance minus a token-rate penalty.
*Result:* On LibriSpeech-960 it matches a character-boundary oracle (2.75/5.67 vs 2.87/5.66 WER) using ~25% fewer audio tokens; on AISHELL-1 it beats the phone oracle at a comparable token rate.

**Why it ranked:** Followed author (David Harwath) and lab (JHU) on the speech→LLM interface you track closely; learned compression beating a transcript-assisted oracle is a genuinely non-obvious result, with oracle and fixed-grid baselines that make the claim checkable.

### 3. Distilling Text LMs into Speech-to-Speech LMs by Matching Internal Text Predictions — `2610.17461` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

Ju-Chieh Chou, Jiawei Zhou, Karen Livescu — **TTI-Chicago, Stony Brook University** · cs.CL · 2026-10-12 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17461)

*Problem:* Speech-to-speech LMs cannot acquire the factual and linguistic knowledge text LMs get from large-scale pretraining, and prior distillation work only covered speech-in/text-out models.
*Method:* The model consumes speech context without transcription, switches to predicting text internally, then lets that predicted text lead speech generation word by word — an internal text interface that is distilled directly from the base text LM.
*Result:* Generative perplexity falls from 151.7 (speech-only) to 51.0; Known-1000 factual Pass@5 reaches 37.9% against 23.5% for SpiritLM and 2.9% for Llama-Mimi-8B.

**Why it ranked:** The closest fit of the whole window to your central question — how to keep the text backbone's knowledge through modality adaptation — and the first to do it for speech-out models; matched-condition baselines plus external comparisons back the numbers, docked only for a 21.3k/45k-hour inconsistency between tables.

### 4. Local Prototype Reconstruction for Text-Compatible Speech-to-LLM Bridge Pretraining — `2610.11159` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

Xinnian Zhao, Chia-Hua Wu, Pu Wang, Hugo Van Hamme — **KU Leuven (ESAT), Academia Sinica** · cs.CL, cs.SD · 2026-10-08 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.11159)

*Problem:* The small bridge joining a frozen speech encoder to a frozen LLM defines the interface geometry, yet next-word and contrastive pretraining leave bridge tokens lexically incompatible with the LLM's own embeddings.
*Method:* A training-only regularizer requires each aligned bridge token to be reconstructable from a small neighbourhood of frozen LLM token embeddings, paired with a head-free, timestamp-free diagnostic that measures this compatibility for any objective.
*Result:* On CoVoST 2 with Whisper-large-v3 and Llama-3-8B, low-resource ST rises 35.24 → 39.49 BLEU and ASR WER drops 10.28 → 9.08; the diagnostic tracks downstream gains.

**Why it ranked:** Squarely on speech–text modality alignment, with the useful twist that the diagnostic predicts reusability before you fine-tune; controlled against no-pretraining, NWP and contrastive baselines, though confined to one bridge type and five European source languages.

### 5. Boundary-Free Contextual Biasing: Depth-Adaptive Gating and Reading-Space Matching for Unsegmented Languages — `2610.09467` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

Muhammad Huzaifah, Yu Pan, Zachary Yeo, Ningjie Bai, Guangzhao Yang — **Recho Inc. (Tokyo)** · cs.CL, cs.SD, eess.AS · 2026-10-07 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09467)

*Problem:* Contextual biasing relies on word boundaries, which Japanese and Chinese orthography does not provide.
*Method:* A training-free, single-pass decoder for frozen public CTC models (SenseVoice-small, OWSM-CTC) built on a character-level Aho-Corasick automaton, with a depth-adaptive gate setting bias strength from match depth and reading-space matching for when the audio is right but the characters are wrong.
*Result:* 66.5% R1 recall on Aishell-1 NE, transferring untuned to WenetSpeech and a second architecture; their new open Japanese benchmark gains 25 rare-word recall points above 97% precision.

**Why it ranked:** The only paper this window on entity/rare-word recognition, and the first open Japanese biasing benchmark is a reusable artifact. One caveat the abstract soft-pedals: it clears the trained CLAS baseline (64%) but sits well below SeACo (80%) and SeACo+ASF (87%) — read it as a strong zero-training option, not as SOTA.
