### 1. ★ Granary-v2: Scalable Multilingual Speech Data Curation for Predictable and Controllable Model Outputs — `2611.28248` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

Nithin Rao Koluguri, Meline Mkrtchyan, Nune Tadevosyan, Aaftab V, Sushmitha Deva, Fan Qian, Davit Karamyan, Kunal Dhawan, Taejin Park, Piotr Zelasko, Arushi Dhingra, Viraj Karandikar, Shuoyang Ding, Slyne Deng, Onur Yilmaz, Praateek Mahajan, Utkarsh Vaidya, Nikolay Karpov, Jagadeesh Balam, Boris Ginsburg — **NVIDIA** · eess.AS · 2026-11-20 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28248)

*Problem:* Instruction-following speech LLMs need each recording paired with many output forms: verbatim or written, code-switched, contextual, translated. Open multilingual corpora give one transcript per clip.
*Method:* A NeMo Curator pipeline does language-routed ASR pseudo-labelling with agreement and hallucination checks, then label repair with Qwen3-Omni. It also uses LLMs to produce transcript variants, contextual-ASR prompts with distractor terms, captions, QA and translations, all packed as instruction–target pairs.
*Result:* The collection holds 843.6k hours in 48 languages, 463k of them public. Filtering cuts mean English WER on 9 benchmarks from 4.91% to 4.67%. The data trains Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe (FLEURS 37-language WER/CER 9.29%).

**Why it ranked:** It comes from a followed author and lab, and it is an open multilingual data foundation built for LLM-ASR, contextual biasing, code-switching and ST, the kind of resource many labs will train on. It is held at 7 for three reasons. The only controlled experiment is an English label-filtering ablation on one architecture. There is no v1-versus-v2 or per-task comparison. Labels for the YouTube and ReazonSpeech sources (about 45% of the hours) are left out of the public release.

### 2. ★ Efficient Conversational Multi-Talker ASR with Predicted Speaker-Agnostic Activity Streams — `2611.26796` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 6/10**

Xiluo He, Magdalena Rybicka, Laureano Moro-Velazquez, Jesús Villalba, Thomas Thebaud, Najim Dehak — **Johns Hopkins University (HLTCOE / CLSP); AGH University of Krakow** · eess.AS · 2026-11-19 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26796)

*Problem:* DiCoW-style target-speaker ASR needs a full diarization pass and one Whisper pass per speaker, so its cost grows with the number of talkers.
*Method:* HEAT merges any number of speakers into two speaker-agnostic activity streams, so a Whisper-large-v3-turbo DiCoW model decodes only twice. A WavLM-based predictor then outputs both stream masks directly from the audio, replacing diarization and clustering. Speakers are attributed afterwards.
*Result:* On AMI and ICSI, two-stream conditioning is 44% and 87% faster than per-speaker conditioning at under 2% relative WER cost. Direct prediction raises the speedups to 79% and 132%, at 10% and 21% relative WER cost.

**Why it ranked:** It is from a followed lab (JHU CLSP). It gives a clear, well-analysed efficiency recipe for meeting ASR, tested on AMI, ICSI, NOTSOFAR-1, CHiME-6 and SparseLibriMix, and it continues last week's multi-talker thread. It is held at 6 for four reasons:
- The fully direct variant costs real accuracy.
- The ICSI test set is only 3 sessions.
- It is an incremental extension of HEAT plus DiCoW.
- No code is mentioned.

### 3. Induction Head Origins of SLAM-ASR Speech Recognition Models — `2611.24721` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 6/10**

Artem Fedorchenko, Tanel Alumäe — **Tallinn University of Technology** · cs.CL · 2026-11-18 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24721)

*Problem:* SLAM-ASR trains only a small projector between a frozen Whisper encoder and a frozen LLM, yet it recognizes speech well. Which LLM circuits actually read the projected speech?
*Method:* Projectors are trained on LibriSpeech-100h for three frozen decoders (Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-2-2B and Ministral-3-3B, 3 seeds each). The study blocks speech attention for the top induction (prefix-matching) heads against layer-matched random heads. It finds head pairs that act as differential attention, and it up-weights induction heads under noise.
*Result:* Blocking 10 induction heads raises WER from 3.3–3.6% to 7.1–8.3% on Llama and from 3.8–4.0% to 23–54% on Gemma; blocking random heads barely changes it. Scaling the heads by 1.5× lowers Llama's noisy-mean WER from 41.9% to 39.9%. It helps 2 of 3 decoders.

**Why it ranked:** It answers your modality-alignment question at the circuit level: the frozen LLM reads projected speech through the same induction (copy) heads it uses for text. That also suggests which heads to adapt. It is held at 6 for three reasons. It is a 4-page ICASSP submission. The scale is small (100 h of training data, 2–3B-parameter LLMs, LibriSpeech only). The inference-time gain comes mostly at 0 dB babble.

### 4. No Audio, No Transcripts, No Problem: LLM-based ASR Adaptation Using Only Domain Documents — `2611.27434` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 6/10**

Sergio Burdisso, Dairazalia Sánchez-Cortés, Esaú Villatoro-Tello, Thibault Bañeras-Roux, Manjunath K E, Kadri Hacioglu, Petr Motlicek, Andreas Stolcke — **Idiap Research Institute; Uniphore; Brno University of Technology** · cs.CL, cs.AI · 2026-11-20 · SLT 2026 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27434)

*Problem:* Text-only adaptation of LLM-based ASR assumes in-domain transcripts exist. A new customer usually hands over only documents: product pages, policies, manuals.
*Method:* Documents are used either as plain written sentences or turned by Gemma-4-31B into disfluent, ASR-normalized synthetic call dialogs. Either form feeds multi-view denoising adaptation, which mixes in source audio to stop alignment collapse, on a WavLM + Llama-3.2-3B SLAM-ASR model.
*Result:* On five call-center domains, the dialog renderings come within 0.28 WER of real transcripts, and on Retail they beat them (17.18 against 17.59). Raw sentences can hurt: Healthcare WER goes from 6.85 to 7.63.

**Why it ranked:** It is a practical route to domain and entity adaptation of LLM-ASR with no audio, with forgetting controlled. Code and a document-grounded benchmark are released. It is held at 6 for three reasons:
- The "documents" are written by an LLM from each domain's own transcript entities and keywords (≥90% coverage enforced), which flatters the setup.
- The main corpus is proprietary.
- Only one architecture is tested.

### 5. Definition-Guided In-Context Learning for Zero-Shot Spoken Language Understanding — `2611.28929` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 6/10**

Neeraj Agrawala, Sriram Ganapathy — **Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bengaluru** · cs.CL, cs.AI · 2026-11-21 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28929)

*Problem:* Speech LLMs fine-tuned for SLU latch onto label names as lexical shortcuts. They cannot take new or renamed categories at inference without retraining.
*Method:* DG-ICL LoRA-tunes the model with each example's labels replaced by random symbols or permuted words. The model must therefore use the natural-language definitions in the prompt. It is trained on dialogue acts, emotion and banking intent.
*Result:* On held-out tasks with novel symbols (Indian-accented intent, sung emotion, French and Korean intent), macro-F1 beats instruction tuning on all three models:
- Audio Flamingo 3: 0.663 against 0.449.
- Qwen2-Audio: 0.703 against 0.620.
- SALMONN: 0.582 against 0.399.

**Why it ranked:** It addresses instruction-following generalization in speech LLMs for understanding, with three backbones, significance tests and released code. It is held at 6 for four reasons:
- It trains on only three tasks.
- The held-out tasks are near neighbours of the training tasks.
- SALMONN is run with a single seed.
- The core idea adapts text symbol tuning.

### Close calls (checked, ranked, placed below the line)

- **Xing-Audio-NVVASR** — `2611.26000` — China Telecom AI (Xingchen AGI Lab) — R 7 · Q 6.
  - Qwen3-ASR-1.7B is fine-tuned on about 800k utterances with inline tags for 6 non-verbal vocalization classes (breathing, laughter, crying and others). The labels were cross-audited over 5 rounds.
  - It ranks first among 9 models on four human-annotated sets, with a composite score 6.03 points above StepAudio 3 Chat Preview. A release is planned.
- **Synthetic Twins for ASR test sets** — `2611.30694` — ATP Tech (Turkey) — R 7 · Q 6.
  - A TTS test set that clones each real speaker is worth 70–122 real recordings for estimating a recognizer's real error rate. A stock voice is worth about 9.
  - The result replicates on VoxForge, and the paper ends with a reporting checklist.
- **BiSA: binaural multi-talker transcription** — `2611.29012` — XJTLU / University of Liverpool — R 7 · Q 6.
  - MiDashengLM-Spatial's semantic encoder listens with only one ear.
  - A cross-ear adapter with 0.027% trainable parameters lifts correctly attributed joint transcription from 0% to 57.8%, and Clarity CEC1 WER from 59.3% to 39.6%.
- **Duopra** — `2611.25773` — SJTU and Tencent — R 6 · Q 6.
  - A full-duplex speech model learns tool calls and backend delegation through SFT, full-duplex RL and a talker repair step that trains only the projector.
  - On Full-Duplex-Bench v3, Pass@1 rises from 4.7% to 44.7% with the same backend. The benchmark has only 100 recordings.
- **When Sounds Turn Heads** — `2611.25960` — KAIST (Joon Son Chung) — R 5 · Q 7.
  - A few attention heads in audio LLMs track when a queried sound occurs, even when the model's answer gets the time wrong.
  - Training-free head-guided decoding lifts event mIoU from 0.07–0.36 to 0.48–0.56 on post-cutoff recordings for Qwen2.5-Omni, Qwen2-Audio and AF3.
  - It is a natural companion to #3.
- **Reproducing Qwen3-Omni's Talker labels** — `2611.32286` — CUHK-Shenzhen (single author) — R 6 · Q 6.
  - The Talker's codes match the first 16 codebooks of Kyutai's Mimi, encoded in bf16.
  - The `transformers` MimiModel encodes the last frame differently, which causes noise bursts in 44–55% of fine-tuned outputs. Padding the input fixes it, and a toolkit is released.
- **DiaCap** — `2611.33153` — Adobe Research, Georgia Tech — R 6 · Q 6.
  - An audiovisual LLM produces who-spoke-when dialogue captions and is refined with RL on 2.9k human-corrected clips.
  - It matches Gemini 4 Argon on AV diarization and beats it on utterance timestamps (TIME 76.59 against 42.76).
- **BiSAT tokenizer** — `2611.30723` — JAIST — R 6 · Q 6.
  - Separate semantic and acoustic branches share a single code index, so they produce one low-bitrate stream without a fixed semantic teacher.
  - BiSAT-TTS matches state-of-the-art speech-LM TTS with less data. Recognition is checked only with frozen-code SUPERB/ML-SUPERB probes; the main use case is TTS.
- **Speech LLMs on unanswerable audio questions** — `2611.29393` — student project (SMILES 2026) — R 7 · Q 5.
  - On TTS audio, Qwen2-Audio fabricates answers to 92.5% of unanswerable questions, while a Whisper cascade does so on 2.5%.
  - On 376 human-read questions the gap vanishes (69.8% against 67.7%), so synthetic test speech reversed the conclusion. A pre-generation probe reaches 0.773 AUROC.
- **CAST** — `2611.31055` — Warner Bros. Discovery; AAAI-27 IAAI — R 7 · Q 5.
  - A deployed frozen-Whisper-large-v3 captioning cascade adds hierarchical VAD, lyrics routing and hallucination repair.
  - On 60 hours of in-house TV, normalized WER falls from 6.99% to 5.36% against WhisperX.
