## 1. ★ Qwen-Audio-3.0-ASR Technical Report — `2609.07549` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 9/10**

*Chuanmeng Bian, Zhifu Gao, Jianwei Yu, Wen Wang, Tianzi Wang, Bin Ma, Xiangang Li, Binbin Zhang* and 38 co-authors — **Alibaba Token Foundry** · cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-07 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07549)

*Problem:* Benchmark ASR does not survive production: regional dialects, dynamic entities and hotwords, long-range context and disfluent spontaneous speech all break systems that score well on read speech.
*Method:* A Mixture-of-Experts LLM-based recogniser on the Qwen backbone, trained on tens of millions of hours, exposing transcription, hierarchical hotword customisation, industry entity recognition and single-pass polishing through one instruction-following interface.
*Result:* 2.02% CER on AISHELL-2 against GPT-4o Transcribe's 4.47%; 1.19/2.24% WER on LibriSpeech clean/other; 4.57% macro-average on Common Voice 15; 99.4% P0 hotword recall; a streaming variant under 200 ms first-token latency.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 10 and the highest possible fit in the window — this single system is Tier-1 on five separate axes at once (LLM-based ASR, multilingual ASR across 30 languages, 16 Chinese dialectal varieties over eight dialect regions, contextual biasing via hierarchical hotwords, and entity-aware recognition), and the streaming variant adds a sixth; if your work touches entity recognition from speech or multilingual ASR, this is the system your numbers will be compared against from now on. Quality 9 for the combination of scale, breadth and honest head-to-head reporting: it does not just claim SOTA against open-source systems but publishes tables against **GPT-4o Transcribe and Gemini 3.1 Pro**, which most technical reports quietly avoid, and it reports the streaming accuracy cost rather than hiding it (9.24% Chinese CER / 13.38% English WER at 400 ms algorithmic latency — materially worse than the offline model, and stated plainly). The domain-entity evaluation is the part worth your attention: highest recall across all 15 industry domains, with 99.43% and 99.42% P0 recall on person names and subject terms, which is a direct measurement of the rare-word problem you care about rather than a proxy. Held off 10 by the two things a technical report structurally cannot give you: **no ablations isolating which of the many production capabilities cause which gain**, and no clear open-weights commitment — the report references a project page without stating release terms, so treat the numbers as a vendor bar to clear, not a reproducible baseline.

## 2. Where Does the Sound Go? Tracing Acoustic Information Loss in Audio-Conditioned LLMs — `2609.05871` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Song-ha Jo, Sehyun Lee, Soyoon Kim, Jaesik Choi, Sanghyuk Choi* — **Seoul National University**, **KAIST** and **NAVER Cloud** · cs.SD, cs.AI · submitted 2026-09-05 · EMNLP 2026 Findings · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05871)

*Problem:* Audio-conditioned LLMs underuse prosody, emotion and non-speech sound. The received explanation is that ASR-supervised frontends discard this before it reaches the language model — but nobody has tested that.
*Method:* Swap Whisper-Tiny/Small against EnCodec, DAC-VAE and WavTokenizer inside one fixed Qwen3.5-4B pipeline, then trace task-relevant information through encoder, projector, LM layers and LM head with linear probes, LogitLens and a targeted head intervention.
*Result:* Encoder replacement does not fix it — Whisper stays strongest even on emotion and sound captioning. Discriminative acoustic structure survives to the final LM layer while MCQA accuracy trails probe accuracy by up to 83 points.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 because this is your stated research question stated back at you — how speech information survives, or fails to survive, its journey into a text backbone — and it answers it at the mechanism level rather than by proposing yet another adapter. Quality 8 for a genuinely well-controlled study whose *negative* result is the valuable part: the field's working assumption is that ASR-supervised encoders throw acoustic detail away, and the fix is a better tokenizer; this paper holds the pipeline fixed, swaps four frontends, and shows the assumption is wrong, because the information is demonstrably still present at the final layer. The 83-point probe-versus-MCQA gap on NSynth (90% probe, 8% answered) is the headline, but the load-bearing evidence is the controls — answer format and decoding procedure are held constant, so the gap cannot be dismissed as generic format bias, and the **targeted intervention on only the relevant rows of the unembedding matrix recovers 30–42 pp of MCQA accuracy per encoder**, which converts a diagnosis into something actionable. That reframes acoustic underuse as a **readout-alignment** problem, and it implies a cheap fix worth trying on your own models before you touch the encoder. Held off 9 by scale and reach: one 4B backbone, six analysis tasks, three encoder families, MCQA as the near-exclusive downstream probe — and no code release is stated, which for a paper whose whole contribution is an analysis method is the real cost. Read it for the reframing; verify the head intervention yourself.

## 3. ★ TASTE2: Text-Aligned Speech Modeling and Deployment toward Full-Duplex Voice Interaction — `2609.08956` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Yi-Chang Chen, Chun Wei Chen, Dien-Ruei Wu, Jie Lin, Yu-Kuan Fu, Yang-Hsien Lin, Eddie TC Huang, Simon See, Hung-yi Lee, Da-Shan Shiu* — **MediaTek Research**, **National Taiwan University** and **NVIDIA** · cs.SD · submitted 2026-09-08 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08956)

*Problem:* Full-duplex voice interaction needs incremental speech processing, floor management and barge-in handling — while keeping the pretrained LLM's linguistic competence and the speech signal's paralinguistic cues intact.
*Method:* Turn utterance-level TASTE into an incremental stack: a shared text-token vocabulary across tokenizer, spoken LM and detokenizer removes word-level averaging, and modality-aligned training predicts one continuous audio latent per text token instead of interleaving heterogeneous streams.
*Result:* 56.3% on LLaMA-Questions against a 57.3% Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct text-only reference — **98.2% knowledge retention** — plus a working VoiceBot handling barge-in at 0.060 s on Full-Duplex-Bench v1.0.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 because the 98.2% figure is a direct measurement of the thing your profile names as a primary focus — how much of the backbone LLM's knowledge survives speech-modality adaptation — and the paper reports it against the same backbone's text-only ceiling rather than against other speech models, which is the only framing that actually answers the question. The comparison table is the useful artifact: Mini-Omni 29.7%, Moshi 83.1%, Llama-Omni 93.9%, TASTE2 98.2%, which is close to a retention leaderboard for the field. Quality 8 for the unusual decision to ship the whole thing and report what broke: code, pretrained checkpoints on Hugging Face, the VoiceBot deployment stack and the paralinguistic dialogue corpus are all released, and the paper states that emotion control was **inconsistent** while speaking-rate control worked, and that deployed time-to-first-audio is **2.701 s on two RTX A6000 even after TensorRT** — a number most groups would bury, and one that says plainly this is not yet a product. Held off 9 because the architectural claim rests on a single backbone and the streaming synthesis leans on CosyVoice2 rather than being learned end-to-end, so how much of the retention comes from TASTE's text-aligned vocabulary versus from simply not interleaving token streams is not isolated. Argue with it on that point; the released checkpoints make that argument cheap to have.

## 4. ★ Dynamic Lagging for Simultaneous Translation — `2609.05799` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Hieu Hoang, Amittai Axelrod* — **Microsoft** · cs.CL · submitted 2026-09-05 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05799)

*Problem:* In cascaded simultaneous speech translation the MT stage cannot control the recogniser's read–write schedule; it must decide from a growing source prefix how much target to commit, and revisions produce visible flicker.
*Method:* Fine-tune Qwen3-8B on *stable prefixes* — the longest prefix any translation of the current partial source shares with the model's own full-source output — mixed with full sentences, then force-decode a single turn carrying committed target forward as more source arrives.
*Result:* Full-sentence quality preserved (COMET 87.1/90.4/88.8 for EN→DE/JA/ZH) while worst-position chunk quality improves; expected calibration error falls 31–46% overall and 48–55% on early prefixes.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — simultaneous and streaming translation sit in your Primary Focus, and this is the rare paper that treats the commit decision as a *calibration* problem rather than a scheduling heuristic, which is a transferable idea: the same "is this token safe to emit yet" question is what streaming ASR endpointing and incremental SLU both need. Quality 7 for a careful, honestly-scoped study — the flicker-free property is architectural rather than a post-hoc filter ("flicker-free by construction"), the evaluation spans three language pairs across FLEURS, WMT24++ and CoVoST-2 under both COMET and MetricX, and the headline claim is a comparison the authors could have skipped: a single training-free confidence threshold traces a **continuous** quality–latency frontier that beats both wait-*k* and target-suffix-deletion, the two mechanisms this subfield actually uses. Held to 7 by one structural limitation the authors state up front and you should not read past: the source stream is simulated with **reference-transcript prefixes**, so ASR errors, hesitation and the recogniser's own revision behaviour — the things that make cascaded simultaneous ST hard in practice — never enter the experiment. No code release is stated either. Take the stable-prefix objective and the calibration framing as the contribution; treat the latency frontier as an upper bound that a real recogniser will erode.

## 5. ★ AuK Technical Report: An Open-Source Foundational Model for Speech Generation and Editing — `2609.08936` — **Relevance 6/10 · Quality 9/10**

*Ziyang Ma, Zhikang Niu, Wenming Tu, Tianrui Wang, Ruiqi Yan, Shan Yang, Liefeng Bo, Zilong Zheng, Kai Yu, Eng-Siong Chng, Xie Chen* and 22 co-authors — **Tencent Hunyuan** with **SJTU X-LANCE** and **NTU Singapore** · cs.SD, cs.CL, cs.MM · submitted 2026-09-08 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08936)

*Problem:* Speech generation, editing, enhancement and separation are each served by separate specialist models, with no common interface and no shared supervision across the tasks.
*Method:* One instruction-conditioned model over natural-language instructions plus audio context, trained on ~3.03 billion instruction–audio instances and 1.95 million hours spanning five task families — generation, content editing, enhancement and separation, paralinguistic editing and acoustic editing.
*Result:* 2.65% average WER and 0.795 speaker similarity on Seed-TTS-Eval, ahead of Qwen3-TTS (3.07%) and Seed-TTS (0.778 SIM); 91.83% content-editing accuracy on SpeechEditBench; code and weights released under MIT.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 6 and the lowest in this digest — speech synthesis and editing are Secondary in your profile, and nothing here targets recognition, alignment or the modality gap. It earns the slot on the quality asymmetry instead: this is a standout release by any measure, and it carries **two follow-list authors (Kai Yu, Xie Chen) plus SJTU X-LANCE**, so the auto-include applies twice over. Quality 9 because the release is unusually complete for a technical report — MIT-licensed code and weights on both Hugging Face and ModelScope, a distilled 4-step **AuK-Flash** variant shipped alongside the base model, and benchmarks that put it against the current commercial-grade systems (Qwen3-TTS, Seed-TTS) rather than against academic baselines it was sure to beat. The 1.95M-hour / 3.03B-instance supervision scale is genuinely at the frontier for instruction-conditioned audio, and the five task families are evaluated separately (Seed-TTS-Eval, InstructTTSEval, MMAE-Speech, SpeechEditBench, DNS/CHiME-4/Libri2Mix) rather than folded into one aggregate score. Held off 10 for the usual technical-report gap: **no ablation showing that unifying the five families helps any one of them** versus five specialists trained on the same data, which is the paper's central implicit claim. The reason to spend time on it despite the relevance score is the released weights — a 1.95M-hour instruction-conditioned audio model with open weights is a useful substrate for recognition-side work it was not built for.
