## 1. The Trade-off Was in the Labels: Causal Supervision for Turn-Aware Streaming ASR — `2609.04225` — **Relevance 10/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Bojie Li, Noah Shi* — **Pine AI** and the **University of Washington** · eess.AS, cs.SD · submitted 2026-07-12, announced today after moderation hold · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04225)

*Problem:* A voice agent must decide moment to moment whether the user has finished, but within-turn pauses routinely exceed between-turn gaps, so VAD plus a silence timeout cannot separate them.
*Method:* A LoRA adapter on Qwen3-ASR-0.6B trained under one rule — every streaming-decision label must be computable from input available at the decision point — so no label encodes the future.
*Result:* 0.97 boundary recall at 0.39 s median latency and 0.3 false fires per speech-minute, beating every silence timeout; the recall/precision trade-off proved to be a labelling artifact.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 10, the highest possible fit and the highest in the window: streaming ASR, LLM-based ASR and contextual biasing in one system, which is Tier-1 territory three times over. Two findings are worth more than the headline. First, the diagnostic: appending **one second of silence raised a "broken" model's end-of-turn recall from 0.10 to 1.00**, which is how they proved the recall-versus-precision trade-off everyone designs around was manufactured by offline corpora encoding the future — an invalidating result, not an incremental one, and the causal-labelling principle generalises to any streaming decision you supervise. Second, the biasing result is your entity problem exactly: an always-matching context prefix became a copied shortcut (**40% intrusion**), and training against counterfactuals that disagree with the audio cut it to **0.8% while keeping most of a +28.9 pp entity-recall gain** — a clean statement of how to make a model use context as evidence rather than as an answer key. Quality 8 for unusual methodological honesty at small scale: code and benchmark released (`github.com/19PINE-AI/turn-aware-asr`), compared against Silero-VAD and RMS timeout cascades plus LiveKit, Kyutai STT and Parakeet-EOU, an eight-configuration supervision-composition study documenting the path to the final recipe, schema ablations, and results replicated on a fresh test set. Held off 9 by scale and self-declared scope: **~20k synthesized examples** from LibriSpeech and AMI, a single 0.6B backbone, and a limitations section that concedes single-speaker single-channel English with all conversational endpointing evidence from held-out AMI, so cross-corpus and multi-speaker generalisation is untested. The authors call it a research prototype demonstrating a recipe, not a production checkpoint — read it for the principle, which is the durable part.

## 2. Training-Free Speech-Centric Omni Understanding with Frozen VLMs — `2609.04242` — **Relevance 9/10 · Quality 8/10**

*Ankan Deria, Hanoona Rasheed, Xilin He, Fahad Shahbaz Khan, Salman Khan* — **MBZUAI** (Mohamed bin Zayed University of AI, Abu Dhabi) · eess.AS, cs.CV, cs.SD · 18 pages · submitted 2026-08-07, announced today after moderation hold · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04242)

*Problem:* Native omni training bolts a dedicated audio encoder onto a VLM and re-aligns it, which is expensive, ties omni capability to one backbone, and may weaken the visual and reasoning abilities already there.
*Method:* Training-Free Omni routes Whisper's confidence-filtered, timestamped transcripts through the VLM's existing language interface, leaving weights and the visual pathway untouched — no architectural change, no re-alignment.
*Result:* Across 56 benchmarks and 21 languages it matches native omni models on audio-visual tasks, beats them on audio-only in all five settings, and preserves more image, coding and math ability.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 9 — this is your knowledge-retention question run as a controlled experiment rather than argued. The comparison is genuinely matched (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B, MiniCPM-V-4.5, NVILA-8B and Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B against Qwen2.5-Omni, MiniCPM4.5-O, OmniVinci and Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B), so "the frozen backbone keeps more visual grounding, coding, mathematical reasoning and medical QA than its natively-trained counterpart" is a **direct measurement of what modality adaptation costs you** — the number your line of work needs and rarely gets. The uncomfortable implication is the interesting one: if a cascade that discards everything but the transcript wins on 56 benchmarks, then either current omni training is damaging backbones for little return, or the benchmarks do not reward the acoustic information a real encoder carries. The paper is honest enough to ask the third question in its own framing — *where do richer acoustic representations remain essential* — which is the right place to aim your reading. Quality 8 for evaluation breadth that is hard to argue with (56 benchmarks, 21 languages, five backbone settings, matched checkpoints) from a lab with a track record in exactly this kind of systematic multimodal evaluation, with the harness released as `mbzuai-oryx/OmniEvalKit`. Held at 8 because the method is deliberately a cascade: it inherits Whisper's error profile wholesale, cannot represent prosody, speaker identity, emotion or non-speech audio at all, and the "speech-centric" qualifier in the title is doing real work — the result is a strong claim about *transcribable* speech, not about audio understanding.

## 3. Tracing Audio Grounding and Answer Selection in Audio LLMs — `2609.04637` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Hyebin Cho, Suho Yoo, Jihoo Jung, Joon Son Chung* — **KAIST** (Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology) · cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.SD · preprint · 2026-09-04 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04637)

*Problem:* Audio LLMs often answer from linguistic priors rather than the audio; training on text-unanswerable data fixes the score, but nobody has shown what changes inside the model.
*Method:* Compare pretrained against LoRA-tuned Qwen2-Audio and Qwen2.5-Omni under silent and mismatched audio, then localise the effect with layer-wise attention knockout and by disabling learned updates in specific layer bands.
*Result:* Acoustic evidence shapes answer-choice representations in early-to-middle layers, while training raises audio's influence on the final prediction in middle-to-late layers; learned weights concentrate their effect in narrow bands.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — this is speech–text alignment examined from the inside, and the layer-band result is the transferable piece. Knowing that acoustic information enters the answer representation early but only becomes decisive late tells you **where in the stack an alignment intervention can actually bite**, which is a more useful design constraint than another benchmark delta: if the learned updates that matter live in a narrow band, that is where adapters, alignment losses and retention regularisers should be aimed. It also supplies the missing half of the standard recipe — everyone trains on text-unanswerable data, and this is the first account of what that training does mechanistically rather than what it scores. Quality 7 for method discipline: the three-way behavioural probe (original, silence, mismatched audio) cleanly separates audio reliance from prior reliance, attention knockout and LoRA-band disabling are the right complementary tools, and the evaluation spans four benchmarks including the official DCASE 2026 ADQA-Bench set plus MMAU-test-mini, MMAR and MMSU. Held at 7 by scope and packaging: two closely-related Qwen-family models tuned on a single ~19.5k-sample dataset is a narrow base for a claim about Audio LLMs in general, the findings — audio matters more after audio training, effects concentrate in middle layers — are directionally what you would have guessed, so the value is in the localisation rather than the discovery, and there is no code release and no venue. The abstract also states the layer bands only qualitatively; the numbers are in the paper and you will want them.

## 4. TRILOGUE: A Trilingual Spoken Dialogue Fact-Checking Benchmark with Evidence and Paired Audio — `2609.04452` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**

*Chaewan Chun, Meruyert Aristombayeva, Jiyoung Choi, Mahjabin Nahar, Delvin Ce Zhang, Dongwon Lee* — **Penn State**, **Satbayev University** (Kazakhstan) and the **University of Sheffield** · cs.CL · EMNLP 2026 · 2026-09-03 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04452)

*Problem:* Fact-checking is evaluated on clean written claims, but misinformation is heard first; spoken claims span turns, depend on context, and degrade when ASR errors distort the transcript.
*Method:* Build a trilingual benchmark of source-grounded spoken dialogue — English, Russian and Kazakh — with paired audio, Whisper-large-v3 transcripts and word-level timestamps, supporting check-worthiness, evidence retrieval and verification under clean, gold and retrieved evidence.
*Result:* Nearly 12K dialogues, 187K turns and 390 hours of audio; English is untouched by ASR error while Kazakh loses 11.9 points of retrieval recall and 12.5 F1 on verification.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 8 — this measures the thing your Primary Focus keeps running into: **how much downstream understanding a low-resource language loses to its own ASR**. The English-Russian-Kazakh spread is the design choice that makes it valuable, because it isolates transcription quality as the variable — the same task, the same pipeline, the same evidence, and a clean-versus-ASR gap that goes from roughly nothing in English to **88.8 → 76.3 verification F1 in Kazakh**. That is a quantified answer to "what does ASR error actually cost a downstream system", and it is the argument for entity- and low-resource-robust recognition stated in someone else's currency. 390 hours of paired audio with word-level alignments, including ~5K human-recorded Russian and Kazakh dialogues, is also a genuinely reusable resource well beyond fact-checking — released at `huggingface.co/datasets/chaewanC/TRILOGUE`. Quality 7: EMNLP 2026 Main, real human recording effort, a Kazakh-institution co-author rather than synthetic coverage of a language the authors do not speak, and three evidence conditions that let you separate retrieval failure from verification failure. Held at 7 by the baselines, which are the weak part of an otherwise careful paper: TF-IDF+LR, XLM-RoBERTa-base and mDeBERTa-v3-base for classification, BM25 and E5 for retrieval — no LLM baseline anywhere in 2026, so the reported difficulty is difficulty *for small encoders*, and how much of the Kazakh gap survives a strong multilingual model is exactly the question left open. Take the resource and the ASR-degradation methodology; treat the leaderboard as a floor.

## 5. Scalable Context Orchestration for Serving LLMs Over Voice — `2609.04288` — **Relevance 6/10 · Quality 9/10**

*Linyi Jiang, Silvery D. Fu, Yifei Zhu* — **Shanghai Jiao Tong University** (Global College) and **AgenticSys** · cs.SD, cs.AI, eess.AS · **ACM SOSP 2026** · 2026-09-03 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04288)

*Problem:* LLM serving stacks model conversation as a flat, growing message list, leaving *how* the user spoke and the channel conditions implicit in audio that has already been discarded.
*Method:* llmovoice is middleware that builds a bounded per-turn voice context from the current input, relevant history and explicit paralinguistic and environmental state, then has the serving LLM emit runtime directives from it.
*Result:* Speaking-rate alignment error down 52.4%, false-interruption rate under packet loss down from 46.0% to 0.9%, model cost down 79.2%, and up to 24.9× cheaper per turn in long sessions at 98.7% of baseline answer quality.

**Why it ranked:** Relevance 6 and on the list anyway, under the standout-quality rule — this is serving infrastructure rather than speech modelling, and it earns its slot two ways. First the venue: **SOSP is one of the two top systems conferences in the world**, roughly 15–20% acceptance, and a voice-AI paper clearing that bar is a signal about engineering rigour that no preprint in this window can match. Second, it is the systems-side answer to #1's question, which makes the pairing more useful than either alone: #1 asks what the *model* must learn to know a turn has ended, llmovoice asks what the *serving stack* must retain so the model is not misled — and **46.0% → 0.9% false interruptions under packet loss** says a large share of what reads as a model failure in deployed voice agents is context the pipeline threw away before inference. The framing is the transferable part: paralinguistic and environmental state as *explicit, bounded context* rather than something implicitly recoverable from audio, which is a useful way to think about what a Speech-LLM should be handed versus asked to infer. Quality 9 for peer review at that bar, effect sizes large enough to survive being halved, evaluation on real voice applications rather than a simulator alone, and open-source packages and demos at `llmovoice.com`. Not 10 because the headline numbers are pipeline-configuration-dependent and the paper does not claim otherwise — 24.9× per-turn savings is against an unbounded flat-context baseline, which is the weakest thing you could compare to, and the quality retention (98.7%) is measured on their own answer-quality metric. Read it for the context-model design, not the multipliers.
