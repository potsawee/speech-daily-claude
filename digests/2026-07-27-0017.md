## ★ 1. VibeVoice-ASR-BitNet Technical Report — `2607.21075` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Songchen Xu, Ting Song, Shaohan Huang, Zhiliang Peng, Yan Xia, Yujie Tu, Xin Huang, Jianwei Yu, Li Dong, Furu Wei* — **Microsoft Research** · cs.SD, cs.CL, eess.AS · 2026-07-23 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21075)

*Problem:* Running accurate ASR in real time on edge CPUs is hard — full-precision speech models are too heavy for phones and wearables.
*Method:* Heterogeneous quantization — INT8 for the VAE acoustic tokenizer, BitNet ternary weights for the autoregressive LM — with progressive quantization-aware training and custom SIMD/fused ggml kernels for ARM and x86.
*Result:* Real-time recognition (RTF < 1) on as few as 3 CPU threads, 1.6–2.3× faster than Whisper.cpp at ~1.6 GB, with only modest accuracy loss vs FP16.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed author Furu Wei (Microsoft) — an on-device ASR result from the team behind BitNet, squarely relevant to efficient speech deployment, though it is an incremental compressed variant of an existing model rather than a new method.

## ★ 2. Toward Interpretable Speech Deepfake Detection using Artifact-Specific Experts and Calibrated Detection Scores — `2607.21127` — **Relevance 7/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Viola Negroni, Xin Wang, Wanying Ge, Paolo Bestagini, Junichi Yamagishi, Stefano Tubaro* — **National Institute of Informatics (Japan) & Politecnico di Milano** · cs.SD · 2026-07-23 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21127)

*Problem:* Speech deepfake detectors are black boxes, giving no human-readable evidence — a liability in high-stakes forensic or legal settings.
*Method:* Train five artifact-specific expert models, each calibrated into a log-likelihood-ratio evidence score for one synthesis artifact, then aggregate their outputs into an interpretable real-vs-fake ensemble.
*Result:* Experts reliably capture their target artifacts across multiple generation pipelines and yield calibrated, human-understandable evidence for each decision, without collapsing to a single opaque classifier.

**Why it ranked:** ★ Followed author Junichi Yamagishi, with Xin Wang (NII) — leaders in anti-spoofing; the interpretability/calibration framing is a credible, useful direction even without a headline SOTA number.

## 3. TF-MossFormer: Integrating Convolution Gated Local-Global Attentions for Enhanced Time-Frequency Domain Monaural Speech Separation — `2607.21128` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Shengkui Zhao, Zexu Pan, Haoxu Wang, Biao Tian, Bin Ma, Xiangang Li* — **Alibaba Group (Tongyi Speech Lab)** · cs.SD · 2026-07-23 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21128)

*Problem:* Global-attention separators capture long-range context but miss the fine-grained local continuity speech separation needs, and time-domain chunking ignores frequency structure.
*Method:* A time-frequency transformer pairing content-aware sliding-window local attention with global attention over the 2D spectrogram, plus convolutional gating between attention layers for stronger feature flow.
*Result:* SI-SDRi of 22.6 / 24.0 / 24.4 dB on WSJ0-2Mix at 5.9M / 16.9M / 25.4M parameters, outperforming prior approaches.

**Why it ranked:** Core-interest source separation with SOTA-competitive WSJ0-2Mix numbers from the well-regarded MossFormer lineage at Alibaba — a clean, high-confidence "worth sharing" result.

## 4. X³-OPD: Distilling Reasoning into Large Audio-Language Models via On-Policy Alignment — `2607.21550` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 8/10**
*Dongjie Fu, Di Cao, Xize Cheng, Zihan Zhang, Wenxu Jia, Yifu Chen, Shengpeng Ji, Yu Zhang, Tao Jin* — **Zhejiang University** · cs.LG · 2026-07-23 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21550)

*Problem:* Large audio-language models perceive sound well but reason poorly, held back by the scarcity of high-quality audio reasoning data.
*Method:* Cross-modal on-policy distillation — the audio student generates reasoning conditioned on its own perception while a text teacher gives token-level guidance from matched text plus verified answers — over a three-tier corpus (spoken text, audio-event, paralinguistic dialogue).
*Result:* Substantial gains on MMSU, MMAU, BIG Bench Audio and MMAR with better chain-of-thought quality, while largely preserving prior capabilities under domain shift.

**Why it ranked:** Attacks the central weakness of audio LLMs (reasoning) with a genuinely cross-modal on-policy method and broad benchmark coverage from a credible Zhejiang audio group — timely and highly relevant.

## 5. Audio-Zero: Label-Free Self-Evolution for Fine-Grained Audio Reasoning — `2607.20166` — **Relevance 8/10 · Quality 7/10**
*Siqian Tong, Xuan Li, Chaozhuo Li, Baolong Bi, Yiwei Wang, Yujun Cai, Shenghua Liu, Chengpeng Hao* — **Beihang University & Chinese Academy of Sciences (academic collaboration)** · cs.SD, cs.AI · 2026-07-22 · [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20166)

*Problem:* Audio LLMs struggle with fine-grained reasoning (event order, repetition, duration), and existing fixes need expensive labels or give only coarse signals.
*Method:* A label-free "odd-listener" self-play game over unlabeled audio contrast pairs — the model describes what it hears and identifies the odd variant, yielding verifiable rewards by construction with no annotations.
*Result:* On Qwen2-Audio-7B and Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, improves fine-grained auditory reasoning on TREA / MMAU / MMAR while preserving broad audio understanding, with finer descriptions emerging from game pressure.

**Why it ranked:** A novel, self-supervised RLVR-style training paradigm for audio — exactly the "new training idea" signal the profile prizes — though improvements are shown qualitatively rather than as hard SOTA margins.
